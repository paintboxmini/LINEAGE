**Shore (with the static sea)**  
- The static isn’t only visual: close to the waterline the drizzle makes a fine hiss that syncs with the grey, as if the rain and the Unheld are the same interference.  
- White sand sticks to skin in a dry, powdery way even under drizzle — more ash-fine than beach-fine.  
- Wrackclaws leave tiny pincer-tracks that the next wash of static-water erases incompletely; half-prints linger like a bad frame.  
- No seabirds. The silence above the hiss is noticeable once someone names it.  
- Smell: cold mineral + faint ozone, not clean salt.

**Crest and path**  
- From below, the path is a darker seam in the brush, easy to miss if you’re looking for a road.  
- At the crest, the yellow haze thins half a shade; mainland air smells slightly more like wet grass and less like static. The change is small and easy to distrust.  
- Looking back from the crest: the Unheld has no horizon line, only static fading up into the same grey the sky is made of.

**Walk inland (before the bridge)**  
- Rolling grass, grey rock breaking through, trail firm enough to follow without being maintained.  
- Occasional pale sand still in boot treads from the shore — a reminder for the first hour, then gone.  
- One or two places where the drizzle collects on leaves and runs off the wrong direction for a second before gravity wins (optional, light touch).

**River crossing**  
- Wood + rope bridge: rope dark with old wet, planks worn smooth in the middle.  
- Water clearer than the Unheld — you can see the coral shelves on both banks, wrong for a river and no one present to explain it.  
- Flapjack octopus: one dart, pale against the coral, then under a ledge.  
- Add: the sound under the bridge is ordinary current, which feels loud after hours of static-hush.  
- Coral is muted colors (dusty pink, grey-green), not tropical bright — fits the world’s desaturated light.

**Tight package for the file**  
Static Unheld + white sand + yellow haze + drizzle + Wrackclaws + Hullback→ faint north path over the crest → few hours’ walk → rope bridge over a coral-edged river with one flapjack octopus → Roadhouse beyond. That’s a full sensory corridor without crowding the mystery.

**In-Between walk → Roadhouse**  
**Non-combat environmental encounter: the blended mile**

After the coral bridge the maintained sense of “we’re on a path north” starts to thin. Not a monster step-in — the *ground* fails the Cuts.

**What they notice**
- Terrain doesn’t transition. A strip of white shore-sand runs between two different grasslands with no edge. A few steps later the sand is simply gone and the grass underfoot is the other kind, as if both were always here and the path only failed to keep them apart.
- Sound stacks wrong. Drizzle hiss from the coast sits under inland birdless quiet, then under a dry wind that has no grass moving with it. Layers don’t replace each other; they pile.
- Distance lies once. A grey outcropping looks an hour off; after a short walk it’s beside the trail and the country behind it isn’t the country they approached through.
- Something paid-for is missing: a cairn or trail marker shown by the ruts (stones set on purpose) is absent on the spot — only a slightly flatter patch of ground. On a return trip it still wouldn’t be there. Nothing stays paid for.

**Trisect / blended beat (one clear image)**  
For a short stretch the path runs through three claims at once: coastal white grit in the boot treads, river-mud smell with no river in sight, and dry upland grass. None of it becomes “the real” terrain. The trail remains the only consistent line — Regency payment holding a strip while everything beside it bleeds.

**Uncanny Encounter**
- No enemy steps out.
- Optional: a single set of footprints joins theirs from the side, same direction, then stops in the middle of the path with no body and no turn-off — not blood, not drama, just an end.
- Or: the yellow haze thickens in a band across the trail so the party walks ten steps half-blind and comes out with the light the same and the country not.

**Exit**  
The Roadhouse appears the way havens do here: crown seal, solid timber, smoke, the feeling of a place someone is still paying for. Behind them the blended mile doesn’t resolve into a mapable landscape. Ahead, people and a roof.

**GM restraint**  
Don’t explain the footprints, the Hullback, or the stacked terrain. Don’t roll a fight to “use” the scene. The In-Between’s job is to make hurrying make sense before they ever hear a local say it.

**Corrected layout**

**Unheld (west / behind on the beach)**  
Grey static water. No life. No Hullback, no Wrackclaws, nothing. Empty threshold.

**River (east / players’ right)**  
A real river runs parallel to the northbound path, then cuts west across the trail at the wood-and-rope bridge before feeding into the Unheld at the coast. **This** is where local marine life is:

- Wrackclaws on the river margins and where river meets white sand  
- Coral on both banks at the bridge  
- Flapjack octopus under the span  
- **Hullback sighting** in the river’s wider stretch or at the mouth as it meets the static sea — living water beside dead water, one dark curve, then gone  

**Path**  
Beach → crest → north with the river on the right → bridge where the river crosses the path → on into the In-Between walk → Roadhouse.

**Locked**

**Washed Ashore — Shore to Roadhouse**

**The beach**  
Cold pure-white sand, powder-fine even in the drizzle. Overhead, the world’s flat yellow-grey haze. Behind you: the Unheld — grey water like analog static, look and sound, a low even hush with no true surf and **no life in it**. No gulls. Smell of cold mineral and faint ozone. Ahead the sand slopes uphill north to a brushy crest; a path is hard to see until you’re almost on it, a darker seam through the foliage. Past that crest is mainland Eclipseria.

**The river (east / right of the path)**  
A living river runs parallel to the northbound trail, then cuts across it at the bridge and feeds into the Unheld at the coast. Where river meets white sand, **Wrackclaws** scuttle for scraps. Wider water holds what the static sea does not: a single **Hullback** curve breaches once and is gone. No explanation. The Unheld beside it stays empty.

**The bridge**  
A few hours’ walk from the crest. Simple wood and rope, rope dark with old wet, planks worn in the middle. Below, the current is ordinary and almost loud after the Unheld’s hiss. **Coral** shelves both banks in muted pinks and grey-greens. A **flapjack octopus** darts pale against the coral and vanishes under a ledge.

**The In-Between**  
Past the bridge the Cuts run thin. Terrain bleeds: shore-grit, river-mud smell, upland grass in the same stretch with no honest boundary. Sound stacks instead of replacing itself. Distance lies once — an outcropping an hour off is suddenly beside the trail, and the country behind it is not the country you approached through. A place where the ruts say a cairn was paid for is only flat ground; nothing stays paid for here.  

**Footprints** join yours from the side, same direction, then stop in the middle of the path — no body, no turn-off, just an end.

The trail remains the only consistent line. When the **Roadhouse** appears — crown seal, timber, smoke — it feels like ground someone is still paying to hold. Behind you the blended mile does not resolve into a map.

**Roadhouse — approach and entry**

**On approach (from the trail)**  
The In-Between’s blended ground gives way to something that holds. Ahead: a two-storey **stone** building on the Kings Road. **Crown seal** above the door, faded but legible. The sign has **no name** — only a **black rectangle with a painted candle**. Smoke from a hearth that runs through the night. It feels paid-for: solid timber and stone after miles of ground that wouldn’t keep a cairn.

A short stretch of packed earth and gravel immediately around the building, distinct from the wild trail; a hitching post with no animals on it, a clothesline tied to it; lamplight in one downstairs window already lit though the haze hasn’t truly “dusked.”

**Saile on approach**

Roadhouse ahead. Window, no pants, bush. He extracts himself, asks if they have a spare pair.

While they talk he clocks a **laundry line**, lifts a pair mid-conversation, and pulls them on without breaking charm. Dark hair, silver-blue eyes. He wants to know where they’re *from*, leans in — shore/Unheld is better than a boat story. *“Sounds like my kind of place. Gotta be better than here — Regency keeps everything on a tight leash. No fun.”*

Then, clear and easy: he’s heading **east, to Vulture’s Nest**, where people still drink and gamble and aren’t so careful. Pants acquired. He takes his leave down the road toward the Nest. Details about the conversation later won't quite match if they compare notes.

**At the door**  
One of the **two Regency guards** handles the door and the **register**. Road assignment, not elite — practical, quiet. Cooperative and unremarkable parties get treated like furniture. Signing the register is **not optional**. Nobody explains where the records go. The door is heavy, iron-fitted, opens inward; the guard doesn’t step aside until the first name is written.

**On entry**  
**Common room:** four tables, functional, nothing charming. Hearth going. Food is fine because the contract says it has to be. **Six rooms** upstairs. **Keeper** at or near the counter — middle-aged, efficient, no interest in conversation; will share road conditions if asked directly; does not remember faces; **does** remember if someone refused to sign.

**Barracks** at the back, closed off from guest rooms; door never locked; guests aren’t invited.

**Tone (repo):** Nothing is wrong here. That is not reassuring.
