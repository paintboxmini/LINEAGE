# Briarwatch Geography and Ecology — Design Trail

## What this trail preserves

This is the historical design trail for Briarwatch: how a placeholder "farming settlement" with unnamed livestock and a nearby travel-woodland became a single, coherent piece of ecology — a farm-claim cut into Turnroot Weald's own edge, with a named grazing animal, a two-fence system built for two different reasons, and a creature (the Tollbird, née Fencerow Shrike) whose kit, stat block, and larder all trace back to the same fact: this ground used to belong to the forest.

This was the largest single cluster in `archives/key-design-decisions.md` (roughly its 2026-07-19 entries covering Briarwatch, Roadhouse, the Wardens, the Fencerow Shrike/Tollbird, Skeinwing, and the Masons) before that file was retired on 2026-08-12. This file folds that cluster into one narrative trail, in the order the corrections actually landed. It is historical context, not current authority — current facts live in `places/briarwatch.md`, `places/roadhouse.md`, `bestiary/tollbird.md`, `bestiary/skeinwing.md`, `quests/the-larder-fence.md`, and `factions/the-masons.md`.

## The starting problem: a placeholder wearing a name

Briarwatch began as a "farming settlement" — a town — with a separate travel-corridor woodland, the Briarwoods, running the full two days from Vulture's Nest through the Roadhouse to the Briarwatch fork, hosting its own creatures under a `BRIARWOODS` card-source tag. Its livestock was never actually named. "Livestock" sat load-bearing across half a dozen files — the Warden Post's report, Bartho's ledger, Weck's culls, the Stonecoils' diet, an archived quest's "sheep prints" — without ever being a real, designed thing.

Drew flagged the gap rather than filling it himself: *"what's the livestock in briarwatch? I've avoided going into it because I don't want to just list real animals."* That's the Translation Principle's "formalize what's strongly implied" case exactly — the slot had been open for a while, waiting.

## Fenwool named

One fully-realized concept was proposed in chat rather than a menu of options — broad, splay-toed grazers whose feet don't sink in Briarwatch's soft limestone; an oiled, water-shedding coat that still shears, keeping the "wool" economic role without being a literal sheep; grazing the hedgerow bramble at the farmland's edges, the same regrowing scrub the displaced Jackrabbits (later Jackalopes) and Scratchers use; skittish, herding tight, sensing wrongness first, reusing the world's existing "livestock knows first" motif from Weatherheart Vale rather than inventing a new one. Drew: *"let's run with it."*

**Fenwool** replaced "livestock" everywhere it specifically meant Briarwatch's own herd — the Warden content, the Stonecoil diet, the larder fence, the Fencerow Shrike's GM Secret, the archived Tracks-Stop file's leftover "sheep" line, fixed even in the archive because it cost nothing and Drew had specifically flagged it. Left alone deliberately where "livestock" was already generic (Weatherheart Vale's own separate herd) or already redundant with a Fenwool mention nearby in the same file. A whole-repo grep afterward found zero remaining "sheep."

## Briarwatch redefined: not a town, a claim

Drew ruled directly: Briarwatch is not a farming settlement. It is a farm-claim cut a little way into Turnroot Weald's own territory — and the Briarwoods, as a separate travel-corridor woodland, doesn't exist. Four follow-up calls resolved the shape of the change: the road between Vulture's Nest, the Roadhouse, and Briarwatch is just road, with the Weald only becoming relevant at Briarwatch's own fenced edge; the `BRIARWOODS` card-source tag became `BRIARWATCH` on every card that carried it; `quests/where-the-tracks-stop.md` had its wording scrubbed but its relocation deferred, flagged rather than forgotten (it was later archived outright — see below); and the Roadhouse itself stayed exactly as it was, untouched as a stop on the route.

The core move was making explicit what the GM Secret already implied: the Masons had "carved out the ground Briarwatch farms" from the Weald all along, but that fact had been buried in a secret rather than stated as the location's front-facing identity. Creatures displaced by the encroachment stayed close, per Drew's own line — *"creatures displaced live on the farmland"* — so Briarwatch Jackalopes nest in the watchtower ruins and Briar Scratchers work the hedgerows, both now framed as displaced Weald wildlife rather than invented set-dressing. `places/briarwoods.md` was deleted outright; `items/briarwoods-items.md` became `items/briarwatch-items.md`; every cross-reference across geography, quest, bestiary, and faction files was swept, including stray leftover "the town" phrasing caught only on a verification re-grep, not the first pass.

## Geography fix, part two: an accidental cut and a real ruling, tangled together

Drew edited `places/briarwatch.md` and `bestiary/fenwool.md` directly, pushed, and said "I removed more stuff." Checking the diff rather than assuming it was all intentional turned out to matter: the file's own title, its opening paragraph, and the tail of the Valhug Lake sentence — cut off mid-thought, no period — had all gone missing. Confirmed with Drew as accidental; restored.

The removal of the Wardens section, by contrast, was a real ruling, not a mistake. Drew: *"the wardens goes with the roadhoues. if it doesn't move it dies."* Wardens-as-people — the missing-Warden mystery, boots turning up at Harwick Sundries in Vulture's Nest — is road/travel lore, not farmland lore, and moved wholesale into `places/roadhouse.md`, parallel to the Guards/Barracks structure already there. Whether the physical Warden Post building (notice board, bounties, the sinkhole and Fenwool reports) should move too, or stay in Briarwatch as its own checkpoint, was asked rather than guessed — it changes where players actually encounter the sinkhole bounty. Drew's answer closed it completely: *"there should be zero warden content in briarwatch. move to the roadhouse."* The whole bullet moved, nested under Guards the same way the Barracks already was. A follow-up grep confirmed zero "warden" matches left in Briarwatch's file.

## Correction: no "Briarwatch has no center" rule was ever made

A separate entry had claimed that Valhug Lake sitting "in the center of Briarwatch" contradicted "Drew's own prior correction that Briarwatch has no center." Drew: *"I never ruled that there was no center."* Checked back — what he'd actually said, several turns earlier, was that the Roadhouse and Warden Post specifically shouldn't sit "dead center" of the farmland: a placement complaint about two buildings, not a general geometric law about the place having no center at all. An invented general principle had gotten cited back at Drew as if it were his own ruling — the exact failure mode ("silent assumption dressed up as established fact") this whole design pass was hunting elsewhere in the repo, caught here turned on itself. The correction stands on its own, appended rather than silently rewriting the earlier entry: Valhug Lake in the center of Briarwatch has no contradiction to answer for.

## Why the Weald sits on Briarwatch's west side

Not an arbitrary pick, and not left permanently unstated either — the continental geometry forces one answer. `world/geography-overview.md` already established that Turnroot Weald "wraps the eastern edge of the Ashfall Wastes," meaning the Weald sits between the basin and Ashfall. A basin settlement reached by ordinary road from Vulture's Nest — nothing to do with ash-buried ruins or strange heat — can only border the Weald from the basin's side. Drew confirmed it plainly: *"putting briarwatch on the opposite side puts it in ashfall."* The opposite side isn't a valid alternative reading; it's a different region entirely. One clarifying sentence went into `places/turnroot-weald.md`'s Geography section so the reasoning is available next time instead of needing to be re-derived, or re-guessed wrong, from scratch.

## Briarwatch added to exemplars (later retired)

Drew, direct: *"briarwatch is a location exemplar."* It became the first location entry in what was then `agent-tools/exemplars.md`, framed around a different angle than the Tollbird's own entry — where the creature demonstrates a kit as behavior, Briarwatch demonstrated a location's entire identity worked through as one piece of ecology: displaced wildlife, two fences built of different materials for opposite reasons, a GM Secret that hides only what's genuinely unknowable. The exemplars tool itself no longer exists — it was later folded into a broader doctrine-layer cut and archived along with the rest of `agent-tools/`'s refinement-doctrine files. That cut is its own separate story, recorded in `archives/consolidated/agent-tools-and-harness-evolution.md`; it doesn't change anything the Briarwatch entry itself established.

## Turnroot Weald moved west, larder fence vs. giant fence clarified

Drew edited `places/briarwatch.md` directly — watchtower ruins east→west, "the larder fence" partly renamed "the giant fence," Valhug Lake relocated, some content trimmed — and said "check out briarwatch now 😏." The diff was reported back rather than intent assumed, since several of the changes were large enough to be either a real pivot or a test: the east→west flip touched geography established in multiple files, and "the larder fence" becoming "the giant fence" collided with the quest file's own title.

Drew confirmed the geography directly and resolved the naming collision himself: *"the Weald is on the westside. fix anything that says eastern,"* and *"the larder fence is a specific section of the giant fence that is on the western edge of briarwatch."* Not two names for one thing — whole and part. The giant fence is the Masons' full western perimeter; the larder fence is specifically the Tollbirds' stretch of it. The fix was scoped carefully rather than applied globally: `world/geography-overview.md`, `world/eclipsera-overview.md`, and `places/turnroot-weald.md` all independently state the Weald "wraps the eastern edge of the Ashfall Wastes" — a continental frame describing the Weald's relationship to Ashfall, not to Briarwatch, and left untouched. `places/briarwatch.md` and `factions/the-masons.md` both got the western-edge fix plus a new sentence stating the larder-fence/giant-fence relationship explicitly; `places/roadhouse.md`'s cave-system notice-board note flipped east→west too, flagged as a judgment call since it accesses the same cave cluster via the now-western well. A handful of loose ends (the trailing "deep and cold" sentence missing its period, a deleted Notable Locations entry, a deleted routing line) were flagged rather than fixed until Drew closed them explicitly: *"yup changes are canon. you can add a period."*

## Farmers, farmhouses, red dirt roads, fences

Drew, direct: *"briarwatch needs a handful of farmers and some farmhouse. red dirt roads running between fences. the fences are made from wood and are waist height."* Applied as stated, and it resolved a quiet contradiction along the way: the file's opening line called Briarwatch's fencing "stone," but this same design pass had already established a genuinely stone, oversized, fortified perimeter — the larder fence, built by the Masons to hold the Weald back. Two structures sharing the word "stone" was heading toward the same confusion the Warden cleanup existed to prevent. Made explicit instead of asked about: ordinary farm fences are wood and waist-high, built to keep the Fenwool in rather than anything out; the larder fence stays stone and oversized, for the opposite reason, further out.

## "Warden" retired entirely

Drew: *"change the name warden. it's overloaded. just guards please. capital guards posted up at the roadhouse. the barracks is not a seperate building."* Two rulings landed in that one line. First, no separate organization — the Wardens (Briarwatch's own local watch, just relocated to the Roadhouse in the geography-fix-part-two entry above) don't exist as a distinct group; they folded into the Roadhouse's existing Regency Guards, who now cover the extended Briarwatch-side duties as part of the same posting. Second, no separate building — the invented "Warden Post," a squat stone building at the fork, is gone; its notice board lives inside the Barracks, the same back room that already held the weapon rack and posting order.

Scope was checked before touching anything, since "warden" turned out to be more overloaded than just this corner: Warden Pazuzu (a major named boss tied to the Demon Court) and the Moss-Warden (a Turnroot Weald creature/riddle-NPC) are both real, established, and completely unrelated — left untouched. `quests/hollow-below-briarwatch.md`'s "an old guild of border wardens" describing the historical Masons-founders was also left alone, as was a sim engine roster key that happens to share the word. A repo-wide grep confirmed zero "warden" left anywhere in the Briarwatch/Roadhouse cluster.

## Warden Post and Roadhouse repositioned outside Briarwatch's farmland proper

Drew caught this one reading his own Warden Post text back: *"briarwatch is nothing but farmlands. the roadhouse and the warden post are supposed to be at the crossroads on the way to briarwatch not dead center."* A real inconsistency — the Roadhouse had been listed as one of Briarwatch's own Notable Locations, despite being a fully separate location file with its own Session 1 Routing entry a full day's travel earlier, at the actual Kings Road junction. The Warden Post had a milder version of the same error, sitting "at the center of the farmland" in a place that had just been established as having no fixed center. The Roadhouse bullet was cut from Briarwatch's Notable Locations entirely, replaced by a Related Documents pointer clarifying it isn't part of Briarwatch itself; the Warden Post (before it was retired outright in the entry above) was repositioned to the fork where the road reaches Briarwatch's fields, reusing the already-established routing rather than inventing new geography.

## Tollbird strength fixed, larder made definite — and the Fenwool carcass question, closed

Drew caught a real stat/fiction mismatch that had been carried since the creature's Fencerow Shrike days: *"please for the love of God stop implying that people are too large for the toll bird. they have a body of 3. they are as strong as the average human."* Text across multiple files had implied the creature was too weak or small to threaten anything person-sized, directly contradicting its own Body 3, Creature Threat Rating 9 stat block — a creature statted as a genuine fight shouldn't read as incapable of the fight it's built for. The fix gave it real measurements instead of vague "size of a dog" comparisons, and made explicit that it **can** drag a grown adult to the fence; the limitation Drew asked to preserve — why people still escape sometimes — got reframed as distance, not capability. It can't drag a body far, so the whole hunt depends on the fence never being more than a dive away. This is the version that shipped: `bestiary/tollbird.md` states plainly that it "can drag a grown adult to the fence. It can't drag one far — which is exactly why the fence matters as much as the bird does."

Per Drew's direct instruction, the larder was also made definite — *"the larder needs to say it has a skeinwing and a fenwool on it"* — both previously soft (the Skeinwing only "if the GM wants it"; the Fenwool only implied via a "wet wool" scrap). An explicit Fenwool carcass was added to the fence's Setup, notably heavier than the routine catches, which only makes narrative sense because of the strength fix landing in the same pass — the two changes are load-bearing on each other, not independent.

Adding a whole Fenwool to the larder risked contradicting the GM Secret's existing misdirection — the Warden Post's tracked Fenwool losses were deliberately unconnected to this fence. At the time, this was resolved by making the carcass an explicit lone stray, unconnected to the tracked north-field losses, preserving the original misdirection while still literally answering the instruction — flagged, not asked first, since the fix itself was clear enough to just make. But the note left behind was explicit that reversing it later — making this carcass **be** the mystery's answer instead of a separate incident — would be a one-line change, flagged specifically so it would be easy to catch and revisit.

**That reversal was asked about directly and closed today (2026-08-12).** Drew's answer: *"don't connect it."* The Fenwool on the larder fence stays a separate incident — a lone stray that wandered too close and paid for it, not the answer to the tracked Warden-Post-turned-Roadhouse Fenwool losses. The misdirection in `quests/the-larder-fence.md`'s GM Secret — *"The Fenwool reports at the Roadhouse are unrelated to the one on the fence... Don't let a table connect it to the tracked losses without a reason to"* — is confirmed as intended, not a loose thread waiting to be tied off. This is closed, not merely left dangling.

## Tollbird / Larder Fence split, pass two

A first split (below) had already moved location-specific content from the bestiary into the quest file. Drew asked the boundary be checked in the other direction too: *"what's in the encounter doc that doesn't belong there? anything getting descriptive about the flow of combat or tactics."* A boundary drawn once in only one direction usually still has leaks, and this pass found two: the quest file's "The Rhythm" opening paragraph was a near-verbatim restatement of the bestiary's Combat Behavior section, trimmed to a one-line pointer; and its Win Condition restated the bestiary's Territory reasoning and duplicated the 4-HP retreat threshold already stated there, trimmed to just the encounter-scoped facts. Neither cut lost information — both facts still exist, in the one file general enough to actually own them.

## Fencerow Shrike renamed to Tollbird

The last real-animal name on this creature, closed in the same red-team thread, in a playful mood: Drew, *"I like how it implies how they act as tollkeepers for anything that wanders too close 😂."* "Shrike" was the creature's literal identity, not a comparison — same category as Jackrabbit→Jackalope and the sheep→Fenwool fix, just not swept when those happened because the Shrike's own content was mid-restructure at the time. One fully-reasoned name was proposed in chat rather than a menu: **Tollbird**, dropping "Fencerow" too since the entry now reads habitat-general rather than Briarwatch-specific. It reframes "impales and caches prey" as taking a toll, without needing real shrike biology to explain it. Appearance was rewritten around it — a hooked beak and two hooked claws, visually rhyming with the fence's own thorns and answering why it can build a larder anywhere, and a tail that ticks at a steady beat tying into its established "punctual" personality. Mechanics, stats, and behavior were untouched. Every cross-reference across the bestiary, cards, quest file, and design tools was swept; a whole-repo grep confirmed zero remaining "shrike" outside historical log entries.

## Fencerow Shrike / Larder Fence content split

Before the rename, Drew caught the deeper structural problem: *"a lot of the material in the beastiary entry is supposed to be in the encounter. everything about the location needs moved. shrikes impale their prey on anything that works in their environment."* The bestiary entry had drifted from describing the species into describing one specific fence at Briarwatch — appropriate for the encounter, wrong for a creature entry meant to be reusable anywhere a GM wants one. The dividing principle came straight from Drew's own line about real shrikes: general species behavior belongs in the bestiary; the specific fence, its larder contents, its specific terrain rules, and the Masons' secret all belong to the one encounter.

Both files got a full rewrite along that line. Staying in the bestiary: stats, cards, deck, general appearance, the generalized impaling-behavior paragraph no longer tied to any one fence, kit-derived combat tactics, a genuinely species-general Territory section. Moving to the quest file: the fence's construction and appearance, the specific larder contents, Frontline/Backline zone mapping, the Barbed damage rules, the Rushdown-can't-reach-the-post ruling, the Pull counter-tactic, and the entire GM Secret. The one live cross-reference this broke — the Masons' Known Works pointing at the bestiary file for the GM Secret — was repointed to the quest file, where that section now actually lives.

## Fencerow Shrike red-team pass

Drew, reading the entry fresh, caught two more things. First, real-animal names still sitting in the larder's contents — "voles" and "a hare," the same category of problem Fenwool and the Jackalope rename had already fixed elsewhere in Briarwatch's ecosystem, just not yet swept here. Found in five places across three files via grep, not just the one flagged spot, and fixed by reusing established fauna where it added something (a "young jackalope" for the hare, tying the larder back to the same displaced creature) and going non-taxonomic where a new unrecorded species wasn't worth inventing ("something small and rust-furred" for the vole).

Second, a misfiled secret: *"the barbs and how they came to be on the fence is not restricted to dm facing knowledge, that's stuff players will easily see for themselves."* The GM Secret had confirmed outright that the birds themselves wove the oversized thorn-brush into the fence, while Appearance only gestured at it as an unresolved village mystery. The two real secrets got split apart: who built the base stone perimeter and why — the Masons, a genuinely secret faction — stayed hidden, since nothing about looking at a fence reveals that. That the birds are the ones who thorned it over isn't actually hidden at all — a party watching one return with brush in its claws reaches that conclusion on their own — so it moved into Appearance as directly observable description.

A follow-up in the same pass fixed one more thing Drew flagged: THORN LARDER, a Melee-range card, had no real window in a strict two-beat dive/perch script. Drew's fix: *"instead of describing a strict series of actions. just give a general description of it behaviors/tactics based off of its cards."* Combat Behavior was rewritten from a mandatory script into prose tactics derived from the actual kit — the version that survives in `bestiary/tollbird.md` today, where a Tollbird "may stay one extra beat to land THORN LARDER first" if the opening hasn't been punished yet, a real situational reason to play the card instead of a structural exclusion.

## New creature: Skeinwing

Drew described a fully-realized new species in one message: a wide-wingspan, blind, echolocating, membrane-winged high-altitude glider — insects caught in bulk on the wing at altitude, "tiny sky whales eating plankton"; a slow, hibernation-paced metabolism it stays socially engaged through; blue at rest, warming toward danger-red when active; mating by entwining and locking tails mid-air, gliding together for days on shared currents, explicitly not a bat. The creature's core invariant — "the sky is home, the ground is danger... it doesn't die because it's weak, it dies because its adaptations are hyper-specialized" — plus the larder-reveal beat of mundane finds followed by one impossible blue thing that shouldn't be there, were adopted directly into the final write-up.

Named Skeinwing — a skein being both a flock in flight and a tangled skein of thread, landing the social-gliding and the tail-lock in one word. Statted Mind 2 / Body 1 / Soul 4, HP 9, Creature Threat Rating 7, with Soul as the clear lead stat and Body as the deliberate weak point — "never built to take a hit, ever." Deliberately given no independent encounter design: this is primarily a discovery object, the concrete answer to the Larder Fence's "one thing that should not be on this fence" line, with the stat block existing only for the rare table that wants to run one live, grounded and dying. Wired into `bestiary/skeinwing.md`, `places/turnroot-weald.md`'s Notable Species list, and `quests/the-larder-fence.md`'s Win Condition.

## The Shrike's Fence, Take Two through Take Five

Four successive passes tightened the fence's terrain rules against the fiction, each catching a real error left by the pass before it.

**Take Two.** Drew caught a self-contradiction: the file's older, pre-existing Combat Behavior section had always had Perched=Backline / dive=Frontline, but a newer rewrite of the Fence Itself/Setup prose had drifted to putting the fence at Backline instead. Drew's correction — "place the fence in the Frontline position, flying goes Backline" — fixed an inconsistency, not a new design ask. The Frontline/Backline mapping was re-derived from actual card text (PUSH, PULL, Rushdown) rather than assumption, confirming only Rushdown and Pull force something onto the fence; self-directed movement never does. The real upgrade in this pass was making Barbed sticky rather than a one-time chip: entry costs 1 unpreventable damage (forced only, never voluntary), every attack and every successful block made while Barbed costs 1 more, and freeing yourself costs 1 more again — a bad Rushdown or Pull could now cost 4+ unpreventable damage before an actual attack lands.

**Take Three.** Drew corrected two more things. Backline for a flying creature isn't "off the wire," it's altitude — a flavor fix, not a rules change, since `rules/combat.md` already treats both positions as abstract zones. And a real walk-back: a prior pass's "generative finding" that the party could Rushdown a perched Shrike onto its own fence was simply wrong — you cannot run up to something in the air. Rushdown can't target a flying Backline creature, framed as fictional consistency identical in kind to Melee's existing "both Frontline" gate, not a redefinition of Rushdown's canonical text elsewhere. Territorial disengagement got the same fictional grounding: a bird can't haul person-sized prey home from beyond its own firing range, so "just walk away" became an explicit third Win Condition. The Mason lore — tower and fence as one project, containing the Weald's encroachment, maintained on periodic visits — was fully confirmed in this pass and recorded once in `factions/the-masons.md` as the single source of truth, cross-referenced rather than duplicated elsewhere.

**Take Four.** The word "perch" was still fighting the Backline-as-altitude fix Drew had just made — "Backline = flying/airborne" contradicted the card's own name (WATCHFUL PERCH) and every flavor line calling it perched, since a bird doesn't hover, it perches on something solid. The resolution needed no invention: the fence is two structural zones under one name. The wire and bramble between the posts, where the larder hangs, is the Frontline; the stone corner posts, taller and clean, are the Backline — the exact geometry `quests/the-larder-fence.md` states today ("the cable and bramble strung between the posts... is the Frontline... the tall stone corner posts are the Backline"). Rushdown's block was reworded from "can't reach the air" to "can't climb a post mid-fight," and a leftover pre-Backline line ("returns to the wire," meaning safety) was caught and fixed to "returns to its post" before it could contradict wire-as-Frontline-as-hazard.

**Take Five.** Three final corrections, each removing an assumption made without being told to. Placement was fixed once more, explicitly: this is Briarwatch's outer perimeter against the Weald, not a fence near farmland, and Briarwatch deliberately keeps fields and livestock back from that border — "the farmers know better." The encounter was decoupled from the missing-livestock hook entirely: an earlier draft had linked it to the Warden Post's livestock report, a link that never had a basis, and the GM Secret gained the explicit "these are unrelated" line that survives in canon today (and that Drew confirmed again, directly, on 2026-08-12 — see above). And the oversized thorns' true cause was recorded for the first time: not the Weald's ambient growth, not Mason design, but the birds themselves dragging thorn-brush in from the Weald across generations, working it into an otherwise plain Mason perimeter — with an explicitly unforced thread left open, not asserted as active danger, that a wall built to keep the Weald out now has literal Weald-thorn woven into it by nobody's tracking.

## Closing: the three-encounter stress test, its flagged items resolved

Briarwatch's ecosystem was also the proving ground for a stress test of the encounter-generation pipeline: three standalone Early-tier encounters built end to end — The Larder Fence, Where the Tracks Stop, and The Man Who Buys Wounds. Two systemic findings came out of that batch flagged for Drew's ruling rather than resolved in the moment. Both are closed now, safe to treat as settled history rather than open threads:

**The hand-size finding.** The stress test surfaced that hand size = Mind left every Mind-1 creature — including the Shrike itself — holding exactly one card and defenseless after acting, a de facto rebalance of all Early-tier content nobody had actually chosen. Drew's ruling closed it: **hand size = Mind, minimum 2** — "the simplest implementation that gets the best behavior overall." The Tollbird's own vulnerability window, once built on that Mind-1 nakedness as an emergent accident, was reframed as designed text instead: IMPALING DIVE's "cannot defend until your next turn" is the actual mechanism now, a commitment-cost lesson rather than a side effect of a hand-size bug.

**The Borrower-livestock finding.** Where the Tracks Stop's central image — tracks that don't continue — implied the Hollow's Borrowers keep or herd surface livestock below, which was new faction behavior surfaced for a ruling, not yet canon at the time. It has since been absorbed directly into `quests/hollow-below-briarwatch.md`: Fenwool taken alive and pinned in a predator's den rather than immediately consumed, a rescue objective layered onto a fight; Stonecoils that have "grown large due to increased diet of Borrowers and Fenwool"; and the sinkhole/Fenwool-loss connection Iron suspects but can't confirm on his own. The thread the stress test opened is now load-bearing canon, not a dangling flag.

## What was learned

1. **A named placeholder is still a placeholder.** "Livestock" and "the town" carried real narrative weight for a long time before either got an actual identity — the gap wasn't a bug until Drew named it as one, and even then the fix was proposed as one fully-realized concept rather than a menu.
2. **A correction that touches geography rarely stays local.** The east/west flip, the giant-fence/larder-fence naming split, and the Wardens-to-Roadhouse move each rippled across multiple files — checking scope carefully (what actually needs to change vs. what merely shares a word) mattered more than applying the fix fast.
3. **Don't invent a rule and then cite it back at the person who never made it.** The "Briarwatch has no center" correction is the sharpest instance of this pattern in the whole cluster — a specific complaint about two buildings became, in the retelling, a general geometric law, then got treated as settled precedent. Caught only because the earlier claim was checked against what Drew had actually said, not what it was convenient to assume he'd said.
4. **A creature's stat block and its flavor text have to agree.** The Tollbird carried a Body-3, CTR-9 stat block while its prose implied it couldn't threaten a grown adult — the fix wasn't to make the fiction match a weaker creature, but to make the fiction match the creature that was already statted.
5. **A flagged reversal should stay flagged until it's actually answered.** The Fenwool-carcass note ("if he actually wanted this to be the mystery's answer, that's a one-line reversal") sat open for weeks before Drew closed it directly — "don't connect it" — turning a live possibility into settled canon rather than letting it quietly resolve itself by default.
6. **A split drawn in one direction usually still leaks in the other.** The bestiary→quest split (species-general vs. location-specific) needed a second pass checking quest→bestiary before the boundary actually held.
7. **A systemic finding surfaced honestly during unrelated work is still worth tracking to its resolution.** Both the hand-size floor and the Borrower-livestock thread started as side flags on a stress test whose actual subject was pipeline tooling — neither got lost, and both eventually became real, load-bearing canon.
