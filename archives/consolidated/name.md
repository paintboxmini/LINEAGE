# Name — Design Evolution

## What this trail preserves

This is the historical evolution of **Name** as one of the Three Cuts. It preserves how naming moved from a broad cosmological idea toward a formal design principle that applies across people, places, and things, and how card identity was separated from the act of spending a card.

The current rules and design principles are authoritative elsewhere. This file preserves the reasoning trail, not a parallel rules reference.

## The Three Cuts

Name, Price, and Distance were recognized as the three structural cuts that recur across the cosmology. The useful working distinction became:

- **Name** is what something is.
- **Price** is what must be paid.
- **Distance** is what it can never be or have.

The three are related but not interchangeable. Naming establishes identity; Price describes the cost or binding that follows; Distance describes the boundary that remains.

The connection was made explicit when the Oracle's summons was found to reproduce the same Name / Price / Distance structure as the creation myth's Three Cuts. That made the trio more than atmospheric terminology: they form a recurring structural model used at different scales.

## Naming becomes a formal step

The next important clarification was that naming is not merely giving prose a convenient label. Drew described naming as what happens when an NPC is given a face and confirmed that it is a formal step required for all three categories:

- **people**
- **places**
- **things**

This was reflected in the applied rules/index material for people, places, and artifacts. The same formal concept therefore spans the world rather than being an NPC-only storytelling convention.

This also matters to Seats and resonance. People, items, and places can each participate in the Seat structure; naming is part of making those entities distinct enough to occupy their place in that structure. The historical clarification that a place can fill a Seat also reinforced that the three bearer types are genuine parallel cases rather than a person-first system with exceptions for objects or places.

## Fidelity becomes part of Name

As the framework matured, Name needed **fidelity**. A name is not merely a tag that can be freely substituted once the underlying identity is established. When the design is speaking through a named thing, the name and the identity it establishes need to remain faithful to what the thing actually is.

This became especially important because the repository contains names that carry mechanical, cosmological, or structural significance. Naming therefore has to preserve distinctions rather than blur them for convenience.

The broader lesson is that **Name should be treated as an identity-bearing design operation, not decorative flavor text**.

## The card-play problem

Name's mechanical boundary became clearer during a conflict between two existing framings of card play.

A new combat mapping placed card play under Name because a card's identity is what it is. But the already-shipped Oracle summons described the Second Cut — Price — as the part of the ritual associated with the deck and combat. Treating all of card play as either Name or Price created a contradiction between the new mapping and already-authoritative text.

The resolution was a split rather than choosing one side and erasing the other:

- **Card identity belongs to Name.** What the card is, including its identity as a defined card, belongs to the first cut.
- **Spending the card belongs to Price.** The act of paying its cost / committing the resource belongs to the second cut.

This preserved both existing structures and made the distinction more precise. The historical correction is important because it demonstrates the intended relationship between the Cuts: a single action or object can touch more than one Cut at different conceptual levels without making the Cuts interchangeable.

## Why the framework matters

The Name work moved from an evocative cosmological phrase toward a reusable design operation:

1. **Name establishes identity.**
2. **That identity must remain faithful.**
3. **Naming applies to people, places, and things.**
4. **Named entities can participate in larger structures such as Seats.**
5. **Name can coexist with Price and Distance without collapsing into them.**

The framework is strongest when these distinctions are preserved rather than forced into a single mechanical mapping.

## Durable design lessons

- Name is an identity-bearing operation, not merely a label or piece of flavor.
- Naming is a formal step that applies to **people, places, and things**.
- Fidelity matters: once a name carries design significance, writing should preserve the identity and distinctions it establishes.
- Name, Price, and Distance are related structural concepts but describe different relationships.
- A single object or action may engage more than one Cut at different conceptual levels; that does not make the Cuts interchangeable.
- For cards specifically, **identity is Name while spending is Price**. The distinction resolved a real conflict between the combat mapping and the already-shipped Three Cuts framing.
