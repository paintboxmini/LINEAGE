# Seats, Echoes, and Archons — Early Formalization

This is the design trail for how Seats, their bearers, and the beings aligned to them first became a formal cosmological system — before "Echo" was renamed to Resonant Person, and before Resonant Items and Resonant Places existed as parallel ladders alongside it. Current canon lives in `mythology/`; this file preserves how it got formalized in the first place.

## The Unheld as a hard edge, not a soft one

Before Seats were even defined as a system, one boundary was already locked down: the Unheld Ocean's coastline. From the earliest 2026-07-19 batch of `memory.md` entries (later moved to `archives/key-design-decisions.md`, retired and consolidated into this file on 2026-08-12): nothing sails or fishes the Unheld, no islands exist in it, and unheldness itself does not cross the coastline by flowing or by being carried — inland water is mundane, only the tidal motion travels upstream. This is why ritual bathing has to happen at the coast, and it's the reason the Island in a Ship stayed the setting's one deliberate exception rather than becoming a precedent. The hard-threshold framing mattered later precisely because it kept getting cited as the template for other cosmological edges — including, eventually, what a Seat can and can't do.

## Seats and Echoes, defined for the first time

The same early batch is where "Seat" and "Echo" first appear as a paired system, in a single compressed entry: Seats are fixed metaphysical positions — structural features of reality. Echoes are beings whose identity has aligned with a Seat's domain, moving through a Resonance → Alignment → Incarnation/Archon progression. Failed Seat-takers were already named, at this earliest stage, as the most powerful and least stable Echoes — a line that would go on to explain the Regency council, and later Pendragon himself as the Scar (see `archives/consolidated/regency-oswald-history.md`).

This one paragraph is the seed of everything that follows: the three-stage ladder, the idea that alignment is not a rank handed down but a change in what a being *is*, and the observation that failing to take a Seat leaves a permanent, unstable mark rather than nothing at all.

## Black Maggie ratifies the standing mechanics

The next real expansion came out of reviewing `experimental/black-maggie.md`, where Drew exercised Authority 3 directly in chat and settled five things at once:

1. **Seats cannot fade.** Drew explicitly declined to cross that line — "let's not cross that particular one." Seats are permanent structural features, full stop.
2. **Archons can fade**, and this needed an actual mechanism, not just the word. A domain whose practice dies in the world starves its bearer; the bearer thins until the Seat stands Empty. This is what added **Fading** as its own row in the Seat States table, distinct from Filled/Empty/Contested.
3. **Time's Archon, Lysander, became the standing precedent for fading** — he had already faded far enough that the world forgot his name. That made MoroM (Witnessed Suffering) the second instance of an established pattern rather than a new invention.
4. **The Thessians were ratified as a race** — nomadic healers, curled horns, pain-transference through touch and ritual, Black Maggie as the last of them.
5. **MoroM's nature was deliberately left hazy.** Drew's own words: "still hazy to me." Canon entries for MoroM stayed minimal on purpose, pointing at Black Maggie's own file rather than resolving what MoroM actually is.

This is also where `black-maggie.md` and its lore fragments were promoted out of `experimental/` and into `characters/`.

## Aege fated, not promoted

Once the Stage III / Archonhood equivalence was settled, it surfaced a real gap: the Seat of Death had no bearer, and Aege — a Stage II Echo of Death — sat one rung below it. Drew's ruling: "Aege can be death's archon. someone has to do it."

The important word was *can*, not *is*. Writing it as an immediate promotion to Stage III would have contradicted Aege's own file outright (which said plainly she was not the Archon) and would have cost her everything Archonhood takes — internal contradiction ending, the individual becoming function — when her entire usable presence at the table depends on her still being a person with a family and a reason to keep walking strangers to places. So the ruling was written as a **fate**, not a scene: unresolved when or how, matching the pattern `characters/greed.md` had already established for the Storm Seat. Two Seats now stood unheld-with-a-named-successor rather than simply empty, which read as a deliberate shape rather than two loose ends. This was flagged as reversible in case Drew meant the present tense, and ratified as written on 2026-08-03.

The same pass harmonized the Death Seat's own description, which had been split across two files with two different claims — "unbearered" in one, "not publicly known" in the other. Harmonized to "no bearer is known," then settled further the same night by Aege's fating: unheld, with a named successor, the same shape as Storm. Not Empty. Settled.

## Cosmology consolidated into mythology/

Up to this point, Seats, Echo stages, and the Seat roster were written out in full in two places at once — `mythology/seats.md` and `mythology/echoes.md` on one side, `world/seats-archons-echoes.md` on the other. Drew's call: "let's consolidate them." Auditing the duplication before merging it surfaced two real divergences that had crept in on their own, with nobody touching either file recently:

- The Seat States table had **lost the Fading state** in the world file, silently reverting a fact the Black Maggie ruling had just established.
- The Seat roster **disagreed with itself on two Seats**. Storm turned out to be a notation problem, not a real conflict — one file listed Greed as present-tense "Bearer," when three other files agreed he was only fated to the Seat; fixed to read "None seated. Greed is fated to it." Death was a genuine phrasing mismatch between "unbearered" and "not publicly known," resolved as described above.

The fix consolidated all three systems into `mythology/`, reduced the world file to a short index that defines nothing on its own, and created `mythology/archons.md` as a new home for Archons specifically — a system that had, until then, existed only inside the world file, which is part of why that file couldn't be trimmed without somewhere for Archons to go.

## What came after this trail

Everything above happened while the being-aligned-to-a-Seat concept was still called an **Echo**. It was later renamed to **Resonant Person**, and the same underlying alignment pattern was extended to two more ladders that didn't exist yet at the time of this trail: **Resonant Items** (an object's own path to Archon, non-biological) and **Resonant Places** (a third path, by accumulation rather than biology or use). None of that renaming or expansion is repeated here — it's a later layer built on top of the formalization this file describes, not a correction of it.

## What's true now

Current canon for Seats, Archons, and Resonant People (formerly Echoes) lives in `mythology/seats.md`, `mythology/archons.md`, and `mythology/resonant-people.md`. Resonant Items and Resonant Places, the later additions, live in `mythology/resonant-items.md` and `mythology/resonant-places.md`. None of those files should be read as if this trail were still their vocabulary — "Echo" is gone from current canon language, even though the concept it named is exactly what "Resonant Person" now describes.
