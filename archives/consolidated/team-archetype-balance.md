# Team Archetype Balance — Design Trail

1v1 simulation initially made the color identities appear imbalanced: Blue looked disproportionately strong because Mind/control is naturally powerful in a duel, while Green looked weak because much of its intended value is ally support.

Team simulation changed the interpretation. Once the colors were evaluated in the format where their roles actually exist, the intended archetype relationship emerged as **Blue > Red > Green > Blue** — the card-level RPS triangle reproduced at the team-archetype level.

The discovery is that **color balance cannot be judged reliably from a single format**. An effect that appears weak or dominant in isolation may be correctly balanced when its intended role and format are present.

## Early combat-simulator findings (roster: frost/steele/mire only)

The first simulator findings, from before the team sim existed, moved here 2026-08-01 during a full `memory.md` audit — the section had gone stale as current-state text ("Wound" had long since been renamed "Injury" system-wide, the roster had grown from 3 decks to 22, and the "team sim not yet built" framing was superseded by `team_engine.py`/`team_policies.py`/`team_run.py`). The underlying findings were real and are presumably still directionally true, but the specific numbers are from a much smaller, since-superseded roster and were never re-run at scale.

Corrected findings (an earlier draft tested with the weak `reader` brain and wrongly concluded Frost dominates):

- **Recency beats frequency.** `greedy` (predicts the foe's LAST color) crushes `reader` (predicts the MOST-COMMON color) head to head — 59/41 Frost mirror, 90/10 Mire mirror. `reader` is the weakest non-random brain.
- **Deck ranking under the strongest brain (`tactician`): Steele > Mire > Frost.** Steele's Body 4 raw stats win (red damage + balanced colors, not HP).
- **Axiom is a real edge, not an "I win."** Valuing Axiom wins the Frost mirror 59% and lifts Frost vs Steele 38.8% → 43.5%, but does NOT flip Steele's stat advantage. (An earlier "Axiom > Paradox, Frost dominates" read was overstated — Axiom helps, stats win.)
- **Anti-read color flattening FAILS** — only helps a deck whose off-colors are as strong as its main color; cut from `tactician`.
- Initiative ~52–55% under strong play.
- **Mire is bottom-tier in PvP** (loses ~70% to both Frost and Steele). Its home is PvE, where durable enemies give the Wound engine its long game.
- **Deck-tracking is situational, not predictive.** A pure `tracker` brain (predict next color from deck-minus-discard) FAILED (~85% loss) — tiny reshuffling decks make "what's left" a bad predictor. Removed. But tracking's narrow CERTAIN use — safe-play detection (if all copies of a color are visible in the foe's discard, an attack that color can't beat is risk-free) — is real: folded into `tactician` (`_color_exhausted`), lifting it over `greedy` across every deck (Frost 59→65%, Mire 50→58%). Lesson: track to know when you can't lose, not to guess what's coming.

`tactician` = best brain (greedy recency-read + aggression + Axiom/Spark weighting + situational exhaustion safe-play).

**Self-Wound cost removed from stat-reduction cards (Drew).** Sunder (Mind), Erode (Soul), Wither (Body) no longer "Shuffle 1 Wound into your deck" — that self-cost was crippling under persistent Wounds. Sim confirmed: Mire jumped ~23% → 59.7% vs Frost. New deck ranking under `tactician`: Steele > Mire > Frost (Mire overtook Frost). Wound-removal cards (Press def, Taint def, Field Medicine, Shed Skin) now say "destroy" (permanent) not "exile" (returns at combat end), and remove from hand/discard only. Clean design loop: sim flagged the bug, the card was fixed, the sim confirmed it.

**Stat-maxing finding: stat-maxing is a TRAP, not a balance problem** — the system self-corrects on two axes. A Body-5 red-heavy deck ("volk" in roster) loses to balanced Frost 21% and is crushed 89% by the anti-mono `punisher` brain (hoards the counter color to the foe's dominant color — Drew's idea, and it works). Mono-color → hard-countered by RPS. Balanced colors on a 5/2/2 line → off-stat cards hit for Mind/Soul 2, toothless (~40–48% vs field). The actually-strong build is Steele's 4/3/2 (high primary + viable secondary color) — a spread, not a max. If anything's too strong it's the moderate spread, and the lever would be Body's double-dip (damage + HP), not stat allocation. `punisher` and `volk` were added to the sim off this finding.

**Sim next step (superseded).** The original next-step note read: "the real goal is a full TEAM-vs-TEAM simulator. Current 1v1 is step one — some cards (Mockery taunt, Partition shield, ally buffs) correctly do nothing in a duel and wait for the team sim. Not expanding to teams yet." That team sim was subsequently built (`team_engine.py`, `team_policies.py`, `team_run.py` — see below) and is now in heavy use; this note is fully superseded and preserved only as the record of the plan that led there.

**Methodological caveat: Frost and Steele are the only ground-truth pair.** Given directly by Drew, worth remembering every time a sim number gets cited: Frost and Steele were built by his daughter and Kevin, real players building real decks for their real characters, with no algorithmic shortcut anywhere in them — "excellent comparison points... but the bestiary and NPC decks as well as any other decks in the sims are all built by [Claude] with varying degrees of input from me, but none have been given a precision deck-building pass by me." Checked against the real files: `testcampaigndecks/frost.md` (Player: Ollie) and `testcampaigndecks/steele.md` (Player: Kevin) confirm it, and `content.py`'s `FROST_DECK`/`STEELE_DECK` match those decklists exactly. Crimson and Sky (Drew's own, from `playtesting/live-test-2v2.md`) are the same real-human-built category, though their sim-usable form is a flagged reconstruction and they aren't in `content.py`'s ROSTER at all.

Practical consequence: every other deck the sim runs — mire, volk, sage, adept, warden, vanguard, tempo, garret/brasca/wyn/tallis, the Patient Host itself, every bestiary/NPC kit — is Claude-built, never given the kind of deliberate optimization pass a real player gives their own sheet. A win-rate number from any matchup that doesn't include Frost or Steele on at least one side is real data about that specific matchup, but is NOT clean evidence about either side's balance in isolation, because neither side got a real deckbuilder's pass. This caveat applies broadly, not just to the entry it was first raised against.

## Minmax trifecta and the Team Sim (Stage 1 & 2)

**Minmax trifecta added to the sim (Latin square, each stat takes 4/3/2 once):** Steele Body4/Mind3/Soul2 (4R/3B/2G, canonical), Sage Mind4/Soul3/Body2 (4B/3G/2R), Adept Soul4/Body3/Mind2 (4G/3R/2B). Round-robin under `tactician` overturned the "blue is weakest" premise. Color-archetype ranking: **Blue(Sage) 72% avg > Red(Steele) 62% > neutral(Frost) 41% > Green(Adept) 23%.** Blue is the strongest 1v1 archetype (Axiom+Paradox control + Mind-4 fat hand; crushes Frost 90%). Green is weakest — but structurally: its best effects are ally buffs (dead in a duel). Critically, the 1v1 sim structurally overrates blue (selfish control) and underrates green (team support). Color identities revealed: Red = consistent bruiser, Blue = 1v1 controller, Green = team anchor. Conclusion at the time: don't buff blue (already #1), don't buff green's selfish power off 1v1 numbers (would break team play where its support already works) — green balance, like hand-size, was un-evaluable until the team sim existed.

**TEAM SIM built (Stage 1)** — `team_engine.py` (Battle: N-v-N, interleaved initiative wheel, target selection, taunt-forced targeting, team win), `team_policies.py` (TeamTactician: focus-fire lowest HP, recency defense), `team_run.py` (runner). Shares the 1v1 card effects: ally effects route through `engine.allies(me)` — empty in 1v1 (behavior unchanged, verified: Steele>Frost still 66.3, Sage>Frost 90.2), populated in teams. Wired a few green ally effects live (Renewal heal, Twin Strike buff, Mockery taunt, Blood Tithe ally-heal). Stage-1 3v3 findings: **hand-size/blocking CONFIRMED** — Sage (Mind4, hand5) went 56%→87% vs Red in teams, because a big hand blocks the extra between-turn attacks. Validated the block-capacity thesis and the decision to keep the then-current +1. Green did NOT come alive (Adept 21→16% vs Red), but that wasn't trustworthy: only a thin slice of green's support kit was implemented and the naive focus-fire policy couldn't pilot support (no proactive heals, buff timing, protective taunts). Stage 2 was needed for a fair green test.

**TEAM SIM Stage 2 (green vindicated):** implemented green's support kit (Resonate all-ally +2/Resist, Support/Conduct buff+draw, Witness heal, Shared Burden damage-redirect tank) + Warden deck (Soul4 green support) + a support-aware team policy. Green went from worst-by-far to team anchor: Warden vs Sage(blue) 56% (was Adept 20%!), vs Steele(red) 28% (was 16%). 1v1 unchanged (support cards inert, Sage>Frost 90.5). **Big finding — emergent archetype RPS in team play: Blue(Sage)>Red(Steele) 87%, Red>Green(Warden) 72%, Green>Blue 56%** — the exact card RPS wheel (Blue>Red>Green>Blue) reproduced at the deck-archetype level. Every color has a home and a predator; no color is "best." Conclusion on the balance arc at the time: the system is well-balanced across formats; red's 1v1 damage, blue's 1v1 control, green's team support are context-specialized, not broken. Don't buff/nerf colors off single-format data.

**GREEN SELF-TARGETING buff, canonized then reverted.** Drew initially canonized it: green (Soul) cards would count the user among their allies — green's heals/buffs/Resist/draw could target the caster, with redirect-to-someone-else effects (Shared Burden) still needing a separate target. Rule was added to `rules/cards.md` (You Are Not Your Own Ally → "Green counts itself among its allies"). Effect: gave green-support decks a 1v1 floor (Warden viable: beats Sage 59%, even w/ Frost 50%, loses only to aggro) while attacker-green (Adept) stayed weak. In teams it pushed green's counter to blue 56%→63.5%, and the team RPS triangle read Blue>Red 71%, Red>Green 68%, Green>Blue 63.5% (green>blue was the softest leg — Drew wanted more of a blowout, which was left open).

The buff was subsequently reverted — Drew: "should green be counting itself? undo." The exception section was removed from `rules/cards.md`; You Are Not Your Own Ally is again absolute, with no color exempt. `combat-example.md`'s two green-exception clauses were trimmed and the exemplars line updated. In the sim, implicit-"ally" green cards (Renewal, Twin Strike def, Resonate both sides, Support effect, Witness both sides, Rooted Oath tick) reverted to allies-only, while explicit party/"you and" wording (Communion, Synchrony's own text) legitimately kept self-targeting. Note on impact: this undid the buff that had fixed green's team weakness, but the old green-vs-blue 63.5% finding predates many changes since — the modern support kit carries green without the self-inclusion in this matchup (current team baseline moved only 41.5/58.3 → 41.6/58.1). Worth watching at the table.

**Why Blue(Sage) > Red(Steele) is 71% in teams (Drew wanted it ~65%).** Analysis: it's NOT a color imbalance and NOT Axiom — Axiom is a TEAM DUD (removing it from Sage made Sage *stronger*, 71→83% vs Steele; its single-target one-reveal ban barely matters in a 6-person fight; Axiom is a 1v1 star only). The real driver is blue's Mind-4 blocking wall plus card advantage, which walls midrange decks. Steele is balanced midrange, so it feeds the wall. But aggressive red beats blue: Volk (Body5, 7-red) vs Sage = Volk 58%. And green sustain beats blue (Warden 63%). So the meta reads as the classic AGGRO > CONTROL(blue) > MIDRANGE, with green-sustain also beating control. Blue's blocking is coupled to both its legs (can't lower Blue>Red without raising Green>Blue past 65%). Recommendation given at the time: don't nerf — 71% is control correctly eating midrange; the counter to blue is aggression or sustain, both available. The "clean color triangle" read as a richer archetype metagame than a strict RPS wheel. No change was made off this entry.

## Campaign Status history

**Clean slate, 2026-08-06 — Drew: "remove anything that says Frost and Steele are players."** The actual campaign has no established party. `characters/frost.md` and `characters/steele.md` are retained, unarchived, purely as the combat simulator's hand-built ground-truth reference pair (real decks a real player built, still load-bearing for balance calibration) — **not** as this campaign's PCs, past or present. A short test session was played under those two characters (`playtesting/session-1-combat-review.md`, real data, kept as history) before this reset; it doesn't establish who the actual party is going forward. **The real opening is `quests/washed-ashore.md`.** Expected party size: 3 players, +/- 1. No predetermined characters — the party gets built at the actual Session 0 (`places/island-in-a-ship.md`), the night they wash ashore. Low-resource survival start: characters begin with clothes and weapons already in hand (flavor only, no mechanical benefit, per `rules/character-creation.md`'s no-starting-gold baseline), but no gold and no items otherwise.

## Combat simulator infrastructure history

This content is now live in `combatsimulations/README.md`; preserved here only as historical record of how the simulator's shape evolved. Two engines, both Python, no dependencies: `engine.py` (1v1 duels, `run.py`) and `team_engine.py` (N-vs-N battles, `team_run.py`), sharing `content.py`'s card definitions. 23 decks in `ROSTER` at the time this was written (22 archetypes/characters + `burnout`, a diagnostic deck that exists only to keep the Exhaust path executing) — Frost/Steele are the one real hand-built ground-truth pair (see the methodological caveat above); everything else is built in-repo with varying depth of Drew's input. Five policy brains: random/reader/greedy/tactician/punisher. Design instrument only, not canon. Outputs: errata queue (`rulings-log.md`) plus Monte Carlo balance stats, including team-battle death-rate tracking against Drew's "a PC dies ~once per 20 sessions" target.

**Frost/Steele erosion note, broadened.** Original framing (relocated 2026-07-28 from `unresolved-concerns.md`): Frost and Steele are a comparison anchor that erodes with every card change, not a fixed deck-quality baseline — every card tweak quietly erodes how stable they remain as a reference point, with periodic reference-deck rebuilds a normal maintenance cost, not a gap to close. **Corrected 2026-08-11, Drew: "cards changes can effect all simulator decks."** The erosion isn't Frost/Steele-specific — any card change can affect any deck's standing as a stable comparison point across the whole `ROSTER`, Frost/Steele included. The narrower original framing is kept here for provenance; the broader fact is close enough to self-evident from how a shared card pool works that it doesn't need its own standing doctrine entry.

## Resolved: 2026-07-19 lethality finding, archived

An early finding (2026-07-19) logged that PvP mirrors were dying far more often than intended: three roughly-balanced 3v3 mirrors (frost/steele, mire/frost, vanguard/frost) landed a death in 45–62% of battles against Drew's "roughly once every 20 sessions" target, and a related boss-lethality finding on the Patient Host ran 1v3 at an 84–92% win rate with a 73–75% death rate. Both were logged as genuinely unresolved at the time — "this needs your read on which lever it actually is" — with two live open questions never decided: whether the flat per-attack finishing-blow chance was simply too high, or whether testing against symmetric player-mirror decks (no dedicated healer role) was the wrong instrument for the question in the first place.

Asked on 2026-08-12 whether to re-measure this against current canon or archive it as superseded, Drew's call: **archive.** This reads as an early, now-heavily-superseded finding — the HP formula has changed twice more since it was logged, self-Wound costs were removed from the stat-reduction cards, team targeting was rebuilt entirely around the random-pick/pile-on/drop-on-collapse model, and real boss encounters (Root Heart, Hullback, Wrackclaw) have since been individually tuned rather than measured against generic PvP mirrors. It is not carried forward as still-open debt.
