**[2026-07-17 7:41 PM CDT] NEXT-ATTACK BONUS CUT AND REDIRECTED TO DEADLY/WEAK — and Deadly/Weak turned out not to exist in the sim at all until tonight.**

Started from Drew's framing: "have diverse flat buffs and reductions or none at all." Landed on: cut the general "next attack +X" pattern, redirect onto Deadly (buffs) and Weak (debuffs), with two confirmed exceptions kept as their own unique identity rather than folded in — GAMBLER'S RUIN's "Add 1d4 to your next attack" (its whole signature move, explicitly kept) and, after checking, one real correction to Drew's own recollection: he'd said Gambler's Ruin and Twin Strike "don't use next attack buff/reduction," which was true of their primary Attack/Effect lines (exploding dice, the double-hit) but not their Defensive Bonus lines, which both genuinely did. Twin Strike's got folded into the redirect; Gambler's Ruin's stayed.

**Real gap found while scoping this: Deadly and Weak were never actually implemented in the sim.** Not a redirect target that already existed — had to build the mechanic from nothing. Added `deadly`/`weak` stack counters to `Combatant`, and a `_rolled_die()` helper in `engine.py` that rolls the base die twice and takes the higher (Deadly) or lower (Weak) result, consuming one stack, wired into `Card.damage()`'s default formula. Deliberately does NOT wrap custom `_damage` functions (Gambler's Ruin's exploding dice, Twin Strike's double-roll) — applying "roll twice" generically to an arbitrary custom function risks doubling unrelated side effects, not just the die. Verified directly with a seeded RNG test before trusting it anywhere: Deadly correctly returns the max of two rolls and consumes exactly one stack; Weak the min; no-stack rolls are untouched. Deadly wins if a combatant somehow holds both at once — not a ruled case, a defensible tie-break, said so rather than pretending it was specified.

**REFRACT got a real implementation, not another unmodeled shortcut.** Drew: "REFRACT should redirect the full damage but only when it wins rps not on ties." Its Defensive Bonus was never faithfully modeled in the sim at all — the old code approximated "attacker deals half damage to a target of your choice" with a flat `next_attack_bonus -= 3`, nothing like the real card. New canon: "Redirect this attack's damage, in full, to a target of your choice. Only on a clean win — not a tie." Implementation needed a genuine architecture addition: both engines' `attack()` methods now compute the attacker's hypothetical damage roll once, right on the clean-defender-win branch only (never on a tie, never on an attacker-win), store it as `attacker._redirect_dmg`, let the defense function read it, then clear it immediately after — so it can never leak into a turn that doesn't ask for it, and per the ruling from two entries back, doesn't touch the attacker's own `next_attack_bonus`/Deadly/Weak at all. Verified with a direct test, not assumed: a clean win correctly redirects to the lowest-HP *other* enemy (never back onto the original attacker), and a tie correctly does nothing since `_redirect_dmg` is never set there.

**Also found and removed while in here: CONDUCT was dead code.** `_conduct_effect`/`_conduct_defense` existed in `content.py`, fully written, referencing `next_attack_bonus` — but no card named CONDUCT exists anywhere in canon, and nothing ever registered it to a card via `add()`. Pure leftover from something cut or renamed without cleanup. Deleted outright rather than converted, since there's nothing to convert.
