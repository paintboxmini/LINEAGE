# FourthEye

*The drug the whole Nest thread is about. The chain runs harvest → transport → sale: `bestiary/ocellus.md`, `factions-and-races/the-cellar-custodians.md`, `places/capital/underground-bazaar.md`.*

A thumbnail of grey-iridescent grit, taken under the tongue. It is the film an Ocellus weeps, scraped off, dried and refined several times over. It has almost no taste, and people who have taken it will tell you the absence is the taste — that it is like putting nothing in your mouth on purpose.

---

## What It Does

**You see somewhere else, and what you see is true.** Not a memory, not a symbol, not a prophecy. A place, as it is right now, while you are watching it. Four or five minutes, and it is clearer than sight — there is nothing to do in a vision but look, so you look properly, which most people have never done in their lives.

**You do not pick the place, and you never get the moment.** This is the part everyone tries to argue with and nobody has beaten. **The vision lands on a hinge** — a place at the edge of being attended to. The minutes before people arrive in a room, or the minutes after the last of them walked out. Never during.

So you get the table laid for six and no six. The cups still warm and the chairs pushed back. The lamps already lit in a room where nothing has happened yet and, in a little while, will.

**It shows you traffic, not geography.** A place nobody ever goes to has no hinge and never appears: a sealed vault, a dead cellar, a cabin nobody has opened in years is invisible to FourthEye permanently. What it can show you is anywhere people actually *use* — the counting-room between clerks, the corridor between rounds, the hold in the hour before the crew comes down, the four seconds in a packed street where every head happened to be turned the other way.

**It cannot find you a hiding place. It can only find you a place somebody is about to use.**

**Which follows from what it's made of.** The film is unspent *noticing*, taken in traces off a crowd that is *moving* — the animal sits where the foot traffic is thickest and eats attention in transit (`bestiary/ocellus.md`). What comes out of it is attention in transit, and that is all it knows how to do once it is in you. It goes to the hinges. It has never once sat still in a room and watched a thing happen, because it was never made out of anybody doing that.

Two eyes for what's in front of you and a third one for whatever the mystics are selling. The fourth is the one that looks where no one is, a moment too early or a moment too late. That is street etymology, not doctrine, and the people selling it did not name it.

## Why People Take It Twice

**You always just missed it.** That is the engine, and it is a worse one than simple craving.

Every vision is a near miss. You see the room four minutes before the meeting or four minutes after it broke up. You know something happened there. You know roughly when. You did not see it. And the drug cannot ever show you the thing itself — not because it is unreliable but because the thing itself is the moment somebody is paying attention to, and attention is the one place it cannot go.

So you take another one, because this time it might land closer. Sometimes it does. You never arrive.

**The come-down is the other half.** For as long as it is in you, everything is worth registering — every detail of the empty room arrives at full weight, and the faculty doing that work is running hotter than it has ever run, because it is somebody else's faculty stacked on top of your own. Then it passes, and the room you are actually standing in goes flat. Nothing in front of you is worth the work of looking at. Habitual users call the sober world *furniture* — still there, still functional, not worth a second glance. They are not describing a mood. They are describing a real and measurable loss of the exact thing the animal eats.

**A long user stops witnessing.** They miss the obvious: the argument in front of them, the fire two doors down, the face of the man who has robbed them twice. They are not stupid and they are not absent. The part of them that registers what is around them has been spent somewhere else, and it does not come back on its own.

## Why the Regency Calls It a Pandemic

It crosses all three hard lines — too addictive, too destructive, no cut for the council (`places/vultures-nest.md`) — but the third line is the one that gets it *noticed* and the second is the one that actually frightens them.

**A district of users is a district that does not witness.** Crimes go unreported because nobody registered them. Fires burn for an hour before anyone mentions it. Wardens take statements from forty people who were standing right there and get forty shrugs. Every instrument of government that Eclipseria has ever built assumes that a street full of people is a street full of eyes, and FourthEye quietly repeals that assumption one customer at a time.

**The other half of the problem is the testimony.** A user has stood in a room they were never in, minutes to either side of it mattering. Ask them what they saw and you get a true report with the centre cut out of it: a table laid for six and no six, a door closing on a back they cannot describe, a strongbox open and a strongbox that will be open shortly. Enough to prove something happened and never enough to say what. It makes users into witnesses who cannot testify — and it makes them a liability to the people selling to them, because the most dangerous thing to have downstream of a smuggling chain is a hundred customers who keep almost seeing it.

Erubeth is at the far end of this thread, working backward from the bazaar (`characters/erubeth.md`, `places/vultures-nest.md`). He knows the shape of the chain and does not have the animal. Everything he has is a near miss, which is the case behaving exactly like the thing at the centre of it.

## Using It At The Table

**Out of combat — the vision.** The taker names nothing and steers nothing. The GM picks one place, real and current, sitting on a hinge — about to be occupied, or just vacated — and picks it from somewhere the party's situation makes it matter: the room they are about to walk into, the room they just walked out of, the cabin above them, the cellar under the floor they are standing on.

Describe it empty. Then describe the traffic: what is still warm, what has been laid out, what has been moved since yesterday, what has been moved in the last five minutes. The room is the sentence and the evidence of people is the verb.

**Every vision is also a clock.** Somebody is about to walk into that room, or somebody walked out of it recently enough to still be close. Hand that over with the vision — the item's real function at the table is not information, it is pressure.

Be generous with the detail and stingy with the frame. It always tells the truth, it never answers the question, and it always starts a timer.

**No people in it.** One dose, one vision, no reroll, and nobody in frame. The rule exists so that breaking it means something.

**In combat — the flash.** Using a dose is an action: **Scry 3** on any one deck in the fight, including an enemy's (`rules/card-glossary.md`). The keyword is the right one on purpose — Scry looks at what is *coming*, never at what is happening. Then take a **FOURTHEYE** curse card into your deck.

**The curse.** FOURTHEYE is a Status — Curse card (`rules/card-glossary.md`). Like every curse it sits outside the deck-size cap (`rules/character-creation.md`), and like every curse it comes off by story, not by resting. It is not a straight penalty, and that is the point of it — at the table the card will occasionally be the best thing that happens to you that turn, which is a more honest model of a drug than a card that only ever hurts.

## Price

**~40 gold a dose** in the Underground Bazaar, which buys one Scry 3 and a permanent curse — a bad trade at consumable rates (`rules/equipment.md`, Pricing Consumables) and priced by scarcity rather than utility. The people paying it are not paying for the combat use.

Outside the bazaar there is no price, because there is no supply. Everything that exists comes up the same chain.

## Not Yet Set

- **Whether anyone can ever aim it at the moment instead of the hinge.** Currently: no, and everyone who has tried has failed. Whether a long enough user, or the right ritual, or proximity to a live Ocellus changes that is open, and it is the single biggest escalation available here — a drug that could show the meeting instead of the empty room is a different object in the world entirely.
- **What it means when a person appears in a vision.** It is not supposed to happen. Nobody has written what it is when it does, which makes it a good thing to spend once and never explain.
- **Whether the dulling is permanent.** Same open question as the animal's Dulling trait (`bestiary/ocellus.md`), and it should have the same answer.
- **What the Regency does when someone points out how useful it is.** A drug that reads the room ten minutes before the meeting is exactly what a council of spymasters would want, and the official position is that it is banned three times over. Nobody in the repo has put that to the council yet.
- **Who at the Nest is running the animals.** Erubeth's whole reason for being there (`places/vultures-nest.md`).
