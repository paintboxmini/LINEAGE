# The In-Between

Everything that isn't a place.

The locations are dots. This is the paper.

---

## What It Is

Between any two named places there is ground nobody named. The roads cross it. The rivers cross it. Nothing on it holds still long enough to be worth putting on a map, and the people who draw maps stopped trying generations ago — the roads are drawn, the hubs are drawn, and the space between them is left blank because blank is accurate.

Nobody lives there. Nobody is from there.

**Most people rush.** Major hubs are roughly two days apart (`world/geography-overview.md`), and everyone understands that as two days of *moving*: leave at first light, make the halfway-inn, leave again at first light. Stopping is not forbidden. Nobody will stop you. It simply is not done, and the reason does not need explaining to anyone who has done it once.

---

## Why It Is Thin

Where all Three Cuts hold, reality persists (`mythology/creation-myth-the-three-cuts.md`). The In-Between is where they hold least.

**This is not the Unheld.** The Unheld ends at the coastline — hard, exact, without gradient (`world/the-unheld.md`). That has not changed and does not bend here. The Unheld is something outside pressing in, and it stops where the land begins.

The In-Between runs the other direction. Nothing is pressing. The Cuts are thin because nothing has been done about them here for a very long time — the world failing to hold itself, rather than something else getting through. The same symptoms as proximity to the Unheld, arrived at from the opposite side. The coastline has nothing to do with it.

---

## The Three Faces

One condition, wearing three faces. Each is a Cut gone thin.

**The land does not stay separate.** *(Distance — the boundary that keeps a thing from spreading until it has eaten everything else.)* Terrain does not transition; it bleeds. Salt flat gives onto old-growth forest with no treeline between them. A river runs through country with no watershed that could feed it. Walk an hour and the ground under you belongs to somewhere else. It is not that the In-Between has strange landscapes. It is that it has everyone's, insufficiently separated.

**Nothing is legible before it arrives.** *(Name — what makes a thing clear enough to be one thing.)* You cannot tell what is coming toward you until it is close, and knowing what it is does not tell you what it will do, because where a thing lives is most of what anyone knows about it and nothing here lives anywhere.

**Nothing stays paid for.** *(Price — the loss that anchors a real thing in place.)* Shelters do not persist. Cairns are gone on the return trip. A road repaired and left alone is not a road in five years. Everything the In-Between contains is either passing through or being actively held, and there is no third category.

---

## Havens

**A safe place in the In-Between is a place someone is paying to hold.** That is the whole rule and it has no exceptions.

The Regency maintains the roads and the halfway-inns. The Roadhouse has a crown seal above the door, two guards on rotation, and exists because the road requires it to (`locations/roadhouse.md`). The Masons return periodically to carve fresh glyphs into the fence posts at Briarwatch (`factions/the-masons.md`). Farmers tie new bundles every autumn and never take the old ones down (`bestiary/briarbundles.md`).

None of these is safe because its stretch of ground is calm. Each is safe because the payment has not stopped.

Where payment stops, the ground goes back. How long that takes is not established, has never been measured, and varies — a claim can stand empty for years and still be a claim (`quests/the-abandoned-homestead.md`), or it can stop being anywhere at all. Nobody has worked out what makes the difference.

---

## Encounters

Anything. Anywhere. Out of place.

A Skeinwing on the ground, a week's travel from any altitude it could have fallen out of. A Duskwick in open daylight with no threshold within a day's walk. Briar Scratchers in country that has never held a hedgerow.

**Nothing here is more dangerous than it is at home.** The stat block does not change. It is worse to meet, because everything a person actually uses to predict a creature — where it lives, what it wants, what it does at this hour — is knowledge about a place, and there is no place.

How it got there is not explained, in this file or at the table. Nobody in the world can explain it either.

---

## At the Table

- **Do not roll on a table.** There isn't one. Pull any creature from `bestiary/`, put it somewhere it makes no sense, and do not account for it.
- **Never describe the same terrain twice in a single crossing.** The party should not be able to say what kind of country they are in.
- **Nothing needs to happen.** Most crossings are two days of moving and a night at an inn. The In-Between is not an encounter generator; it is why everyone hurries.
- A party that decides to stop, camp, and look around has done something unusual, and the table should feel that before anything responds to it.

## The Scar

One thing in the In-Between is not displaced, not passing through, and not explained: a pale figure that attends. It does not approach and has never done anything. See `world/the-scar.md`.
