# The Island in a Ship

*"The Vessel is the Voyage. To Enter, Surrender the Shore."*

The Archipelago of the Mind. A threshold space. Not a location players navigate to — a place that finds them.

---

## What It Is

A small island. Inside a ship. Or the ship has become the island — the hull still visible, the mast still standing, but trees grown through the wood, roots in the ribs, sand where deck boards used to be. A waterfall falls from somewhere inside into a cavern at the island's center. The water doesn't touch the deck.

It is always small. You can see all of it from where you arrive.

---

## The Constants

These never change:

- The island is small
- The Oracle is at a table
- The cave is there

---

## The Variables

The structure — the ship, the building, the room — reflects the last threshold the players crossed.

End a session through a door in the Gilded Tusk: the Gilded Tusk sits on the beach. Mosaic tiles half-buried in sand. The boar skull visible through the window. Frankincense mixing with salt air.

End a session inside the War Engine: the castle-sized weapon sits tilted in shallow water, half-submerged, impossibly still. No sound from it now.

End a session in Turnroot: roots have already claimed whatever structure arrived. The island and the ship are becoming the same thing.

The structure is always recognizable. Always wrong in the way things are wrong when a place has absorbed something it wasn't built to hold.

**The players arrive before they notice the structure behind them.** The realization lands after they're already on the island. How long that takes depends on how the transition happened.

---

## Arriving — The Transition

Trigger: DM discretion. Always after a significant beat. Never mechanically mandatory.

The threshold is the mechanic. Players cross a dark doorway, a cave mouth, a door held open by someone else. Sometimes they're mid-conversation. Sometimes the stairs just keep going.

**Vary the speed. Vary the trigger distance. If the pattern becomes predictable, the magic flattens.**

- Long staircase: they commit to going down. The stairs keep going. Air changes. Sand underfoot. They've been here thirty seconds before someone notices.
- Mid-conversation: walking a corridor, someone says *"wait, is that sand?"* They're already there.
- Sleep at the Roadhouse: they wake up. The inn isn't there.
- Water crossing: already between places. The horizon is there, then it isn't. The beach arrives.
- The Island comes to them: rarely — only when the cost was real. The Oracle is sitting at a table in the corner of the room they're already in. The cave mouth is the door they came through.

**Players arrive alone for the Oracle ritual.** Each player experiences their own threshold.

---

## The Oracle

Exists only on the Island. Speaks in three frames:

**Name. Price. Distance.**

She does not explain. She does not guide. She is a selection ritual, not a prophecy.

### Ritual Flow

**Name** — Oracle poses one question. Player answers.

Questions she asks:
- *"What did you lose?"*
- *"What changed?"*
- *"What did you refuse?"*
- *"What do you carry forward?"*

**Price** — One card is revealed from the Oracle Deck. It is buried — returned to the Oracle deck, not removed from it. The player does not receive it. This is the cost of growth — something available that you don't get to take *yet*. The card remains in the pool; a player who wants it can plan toward it, and hope it surfaces again.

**Distance** — Three cards revealed. Player chooses one. That card enters their deck.

### The Oracle Deck

- Contains available reward cards for the current arc
- Enemy signature cards may enter it (DM adds after significant encounters)
- DM may add cards based on what happened in the session
- Players may propose new cards — DM decides if they enter the deck

Growth is framed as constrained choice. You never get everything available. You always get something real.

---

## The Cave

The cave is the third constant. It is always there. The waterfall falls into it. The Oracle's table faces away from it.

**After death:** The Oracle is not present. Just the open cave and nowhere else to go.

The character walks in. The scene fades to black.

What happens in the cave after death is not written here. That is a table discovery.

---

## Session 0 — First Visit

The night the players hear the sailor's story — drinking on deck, the night before arrival in Eclipseria — the Island finds them for the first time.

This is when characters are named and decks are built. The Oracle sees who they are before they do. Every card they draw for the rest of the campaign was seen first by someone who doesn't use her eyes.

The structure on the beach their first night: the ship they're already on.

### Deck Building

Players take turns selecting cards from the Oracle deck until each has 9. The method below is one option — see `rules/character-creation.md` for this one and the passing-piles alternative; either works here.

1. DM draws 3 cards at random from the Oracle deck and lays them face up.
2. The active player chooses 1. The others are set aside.
3. Rotate to the next player. Repeat from step 1.
4. Continue until each player has 9 cards.

After decks are built, players assign their stats.
