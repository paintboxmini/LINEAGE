# The Coil

*The island doesn't appear on charts. The Night Ferryman knows when to look for it.*

---

## The Island

Stone. Flat and dark, barely above the waterline when it surfaces. No vegetation. No shelter. The labyrinth entrance is the only feature — a low arch cut into rock, deliberate, old. Something built this. Nobody remembers who.

The walls inside are taller than the island looks from the water. Dry stone, close-fitted. The passages are wide enough for two abreast, narrow enough that you feel the walls on both sides. No torches. The party brings their own light.

---

## Appearance Conditions

The island surfaces on a condition the Night Ferryman knows and has not explained. It does not stay. How long it remains above water: he knows this too. He has never missed it.

*Develop the appearance condition when it becomes relevant to the story.*

---

## The Night Ferryman

Already established at Vulture's Nest — rows after curfew, claims the Unheld whispers less at night. He will not offer to take the party to the island. If they earn his trust, or ask the right question, he will take them. He charges nothing for this crossing. He doesn't explain why.

What he will say, if pressed:

*"Pick a hand before you go in. Keep it on the wall. Don't change it."*

If asked to elaborate: *"That's all I know."*

He waits in the boat. He does not look at the entrance while he waits.

---

## The Navigation Rule

**The hidden rule:** On entry, choose a wall — left or right — and keep that hand in contact with it for the entire traversal. Do not switch. Do not lift contact at junctions to deliberate. The rule is never stated inside the labyrinth.

The Ferryman's hint is the only external clue. Inside, the evidence is in the walls:

- One wall in each corridor is worn smooth at shoulder height. Years of hands. The other wall isn't.
- Near junctions where travelers changed direction: marks on the floor. Drag. Hesitation. Bodies, occasionally. They are found mid-turn.
- Near the entrance, partially obscured: a carving. A hand outline, palm flat against stone. No text.
- The passages that lead deeper smell different from the ones that double back. A party paying close attention will notice that the smell correlates with which wall they've been following.

**If the party never received the Ferryman's hint:** they have to read the walls. The evidence is visible — not hidden, not announced. They may figure it out. They may not.

**If the party figures out the rule mid-run:** they can re-commit to a wall from that point. Pressure doesn't drop retroactively, but no new breaks accumulate going forward.

**If the party splits and each half picks a different wall:** both are technically following the rule. They will separate. This is a valid approach with real consequences.

## The Glowing Thread

A thin line of light that briefly traces the correct path forward. It appears for a fraction of a second — blink and it's gone.

**It appears when:**
- A player is zapped by the orb at the First Fork
- A player finds the diamond beneath the chest at the First Fork
- The party frees the trapped deer (brighter, lasts slightly longer)
- *Additional triggers — add when the story warrants*

**Noticing it:** Perception check.
- Success: the player sees it clearly — a luminous thread along the floor and wall, tracing a path.
- Close but not enough: *"You could almost make something out — something briefly lit, like a reflection that moved wrong."* They're encouraged to look again.
- Failure: nothing registered.

**Following the thread:** Does not raise the pressure track, even if the path it shows conflicts with the wall-following rule. The thread and the rule are two different things pointing at the same destination. A party that finds and follows the thread skips the navigation problem entirely — but the thread doesn't last and can't be summoned on demand.

---

## The First Fork

The first junction inside the Coil. Both objects are visible from the fork before the party commits to a direction.

**Right passage:** A wooden chest sits against the wall, unlocked. It is empty. Beneath it, partially obscured by grime, is a small diamond. Finding the diamond requires either moving the chest or a close inspection of the floor around it.

*When the diamond is found:* The glowing thread appears briefly. Perception check to catch it.

**Left passage:** A glowing orb floats above a stone pedestal. It pulses slowly. It looks like it wants to be touched.

*When touched:* 1 damage. Immediately after, the glowing thread appears. Perception check to catch it.

*The orb cannot be moved, extinguished, or destroyed by conventional means.*

**What the fork is actually doing:** Splitting the party's attention before they've committed to a wall. Players who go different directions are already on different wall-following paths. Players who debate too long at the junction have already hesitated. The friction starts here.

---

## The Light/Dark Fork

Further in. A junction where one passage is lit — warm, steady, sourceless — and one is completely dark. Carved into the wall at the split: a stone face. Rough-featured, old. It speaks when the party arrives.

*"Will you bask in the light, or serve in the dark?"*

It waits. It does not elaborate. It will not answer follow-up questions.

**The face disapproves of any answer.** It does not matter which path they choose. The labyrinth is not testing the choice — it is testing what the choice costs the party. Do they decide together? Does one person speak for everyone? Do they argue? Does someone walk off while the debate is still happening?

If the party reaches a decision without friction: the face is silent. They proceed.

If the party argues — raised voices, visible tension, anyone moving toward a path before a decision is made: pressure track +1. The face does not react to this. It has already seen it.

**Both paths lead to the same place.** The lit passage and the dark passage converge further in. The face knew this. The question was never about the destination.

---

## The Pressure Track

Starts at 0 on entry. Resets only when the party reaches the center or exits.

| Track | State |
|-------|-------|
| 0–2 | Silence. The labyrinth feels empty. |
| 3–4 | A sound — low, resonant, coming from somewhere ahead. The minotaurs know where you are. |
| 5–6 | The sound is closer. Breathing between the stones. |
| 7+ | They are moving toward you. |

**Raises the track:**

| Trigger | +Pressure |
|---------|-----------|
| Breaking the wall rule (switching walls or lifting contact at a junction to deliberate) | +1 |
| Running | +1 |
| Combat noise — shout, spell, weapon clash | +1 |
| Light source extinguished then relit | +1 |

**Lowers the track:**

| Trigger | −Pressure |
|---------|-----------|
| Full passage traversed in silence without incident | −1 |

*Additional triggers — add when the story warrants.*

**Note:** Following the glowing thread never raises the pressure track, regardless of which wall it leads you to.

## The Minotaurs

They do not guard the entrance. They are already inside.

**At track 3–4:** A cry echoes through the stone. Not a roar — something larger and slower than a roar. It comes from everywhere at once. Players who have been breaking the rule will hear it differently from players who haven't, though they won't know why.

**At track 5–6:** Movement sounds. At a junction ahead: something large, unhurried, turning away. They are not rushing. They know the labyrinth better than the party does.

**At track 7+:** Encounter. They fight in tight corridors. They do not enter the center.

## The Chitterers

Small. Fast. Somewhere between fey and vermin. They live in the walls — or behind them, or in gaps that shouldn't fit anything — and emerge when something interesting is happening. Frantic chittering language the party won't understand. They sound like an argument conducted entirely in static.

They are not part of the pressure track. They are not aggressive. They are neutral entities operating on their own logic, which has nothing to do with the party and everything to do with the labyrinth.

They carry crystal knives and arrowheads — small, sharp, clearly worked. The crystal is pale and slightly luminous.

If the party lingers near their territory or handles things they shouldn't: the Chitterers chitter furiously. Like a squirrel that has decided to make its feelings known. They do not attack. They do not stop chittering until the party moves on.

### The Trapped Deer

An encounter inside the Coil, separate from the minotaur pressure.

A small deer stands caught in a Chitterer snare — fine-spun cord, neatly made. Its horns are crystal. The same pale, slightly luminous crystal as the Chitterers' blades.

Chitterers are nearby. Watching. They chitter with low urgency when the party gets close.

**The connection is visible:** The deer's horns are the source material for the crystal weapons. The party doesn't need a roll to make this — it's there if they look at both things at the same time.

**If the party frees the deer:** The Chitterers scatter, chittering furiously as they go. The deer stands still for a moment. Then the glowing thread appears from it — brighter than before, lasting a half-second longer. Perception check to catch it. Then the deer is gone.

**If the party doesn't free the deer:** The Chitterers eventually return. The deer stays where it is. The thread does not appear.

## The Center

The passage widens. The stone is older here — different cut, different color. Whatever is in the center has been here longer than the labyrinth around it.

*Story item. Develop when relevant — the labyrinth is a trial for the party's bonds. What they find at the center should reflect how they navigated.*

---

## GM Notes

**The Ferryman's exact words are the only verbal hint.** Don't rephrase or expand unless the party asks a direct follow-up. If they push: *"I'm not the one who's been in there."*

**Reward players who check the walls before committing.** The worn side is visible. Don't hide it — just don't announce it.

**The pressure track is cumulative.** It doesn't care why pressure built — noise, rule breaks, panic — it only cares that it did. A party that runs and fights and second-guesses their wall choice will arrive at the center with the minotaurs close behind, regardless of which mistake was "the" mistake.

---

## Open Hooks

**What the Ferryman is waiting for.** He rows parties there without explanation. He doesn't go in. He doesn't look at the entrance while he waits.

**The appearance condition.** He knows when the island surfaces. He has never missed it. The source of that knowledge is not documented.

**The center.** Left open deliberately. Build toward it when the story knows what it needs.

**The bodies.** Some are old. Some are not. At least one has a Vulture's Nest dock badge.

**Why it was built.** The labyrinth is not natural. The rule is not accidental. Someone designed both. That someone is not a thread to pull until the center is decided.
