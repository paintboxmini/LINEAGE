# The Hollow Below Briarwatch

A Tales Untold adventure module. For location context, see `locations/briarwatch.md`.

---

## Tone

Grounded. Damp. Heavy. Overcast. The danger here is not mystical — it is water, pressure, stone, and reaction.

---

## Opening Conditions

The day begins under thick cloud cover. The air carries the smell of wet soil. Fields feel softer underfoot than they should. Fenwool were lost weeks ago when a section of field gave way. A bounty was posted.

---

## Boxed Read-Aloud — Arrival at the Farm

> The sky hangs low and gray over the farmland. The soil under your boots gives slightly, as though it has not fully decided whether to hold. A wide depression scars one of the fields — fenced off with rope and splintered posts. The farmer stands nearby, hat in hand, looking more tired than frightened.
>
> *"Thanks for coming. Was starting to think nobody would. Lost a dozen head when the ground opened up. There's an old well on the property. Leads down into caves. Always knew it was there. Never thought it'd matter."*

## Causality Chain (GM Reference)

Last year: river diverted during drought.
Spring rains returned; subsurface seepage increased.
Minor limestone seams widened.
Borrowers widened their tunnel network over time.
Root-gnawer populations shifted; predator hunting range expanded upward.
Sinkhole formed above Flood Shelf Chamber.

This is hydrostatic pressure plus structural compromise. Not curse. Not omen.

---

## Subterranean Pressure Track (GM-Facing)

You never announce a number. You narrate symptoms.

| Level | State | Symptoms |
|-------|-------|----------|
| 0 | Stable | No structural risk. Predator confined to lower depth. No collapse checks triggered by normal combat. |
| 1 | Softening | Minor debris may fall in unstable zones. Undercut Pillar cracks visible. Combat near weakened seam may justify a Structural Event check. |
| 2 | Active Instability | Split Seam shifts during heavy impact. Flood Shelf soft floor counts as unstable terrain. Loud combat may trigger a Structural Event roll. Predator range expands upward. |
| 3 | Collapse Risk | Sinkhole widening on surface. Any major vibration may trigger Structural Event automatically. Environmental damage may cause Collapse. |
| 4 | Structural Failure | Partial cave collapse possible without player trigger. Borrower tunnels may seal mid-session. Flood Shelf partially floods. Old Flow Channel destabilizes. Greater Stonecoil may begin relocating. |
| 5 | Major Sink Event | Significant surface collapse. Greater Stonecoil migrates upward. Multiple Structural Events occur during movement. Map geometry permanently altered. |

**Pressure increases by 1:** heavy rainfall, increased irrigation flow, Undercut Pillar damaged, major intentional collapse, Greater Stonecoil barrier destabilized.

**Pressure decreases by 1:** pillar reinforced, drainage cleared, irrigation reduced, water safely redirected, sinkhole stabilized.

**How it feels at the table:**
- Early: *"Something's off."*
- Mid: *"We need to act."*
- High: *"This might go wrong during the fight."*
- Critical: *"The cave might choose for you."*

Narrate through dust falling, cracks widening, Borrowers moving their sleeping mats further back, new soil sag reported by the farmer. Players understand things are worsening — not by how much. That uncertainty is the pressure.

## The Dungeon

### Surface Layer — Watchtower Ruins

An old border watchtower from a prior era. What remains is a cracked circular foundation, scattered blocks half-swallowed by grass, and a well at its center with rope long rotted away.

**Read-Aloud:**
> *Something moves in the rubble. Quick. Low. Pale fur against stone.*

**Encounter:** Briarwatch Jackrabbits — see `bestiary/briarwatch-jackrabbit.md`

Large, territorial, fast-breeding ruin scavengers disturbed by sinkhole formation. Skittish but defensive of nesting hollows. Fast charges. Retreat through masonry gaps.

*Tactical purpose: introduce unstable footing, warm up combat, establish environmental hazard, cost resources without escalating the ecosystem prematurely.*

**Alternate Surface Encounter:** Shifting Burrow — see `quests/shifting-burrow.md`

Borrower Sentries defending exposed burrow entrances at the sinkhole. Teaches positioning consequence and Backline cost before the descent. Use instead of Jackrabbits for a party that needs more mechanical grounding before the Hollow.

**Checks:**
- Body (Easy): Climb broken stair safely; failure causes minor damage; loud impact may raise Pressure if already 2+
- Mind (Easy): Identify nest cluster, predict charge angle
- Soul (Optional): Drive off rather than kill

**Entry:** The well shaft is the primary descent. Old drainage channel runs east. Vertical shaft risky but usable.

---

### Transition Zone — Broken Stair Chamber

Wide chamber (~30 ft across), ceiling 15 ft high. Clear separation possible. One obvious tunnel forward. One narrow Borrower crawl lane, hidden.

*Safe staging area. Introduce dampness. Show mineral streaking on walls.*

**The Heavy Door** — half-buried in debris off this chamber. Thick stone slab, iron-banded, seals into a carved rectangular frame. When closed: reduces vibration, dampens sound, blocks predator access.

**The Living Chamber (Rest Site)** — inside the door: dry, reinforced ceiling, four carved bunks, small hearth alcove, tool hooks, old supply shelves. Structurally superior to surrounding caves. Survives high Pressure events. The old builders reinforced it properly.

---

### Upper Caverns

**Split Seam (First Choke Point)**

Natural fissure widened by Borrowers. 5 ft wide, low ceiling, single-file movement, sharp bend mid-way.

Confined Space Rule active — only one Frontline per side.

Clues: scratch marks at knee height, shed skin fragment, clay bowl listening post nearby.

---

**Flood Shelf Chamber**

Medium chamber. One side visibly damp. Old dry mineral ring higher on wall. Soft sediment floor on irrigation-facing side. Underground trickle audible.

Two exits: narrow predator tunnel, wider Borrower tunnel.

Contains the **Vertical Soil Break** — a rough 4–6 ft diameter failure point with roots protruding through the ceiling. Faint daylight at certain hours. Players can drop pebbles and hear them fall, or bypass watchtower entry entirely using rope anchor from the surface.

---

### Mid Depth

**Borrower Settlement**

Cluster of widened tunnels. Low-roofed communal space. Fungal mats cultivated on walls. Clay bowls at tunnel mouths. Simple barricades. Borrowers move through 3 ft tunnels; players must crouch or go around. No predators here — they avoid this zone unless starving.

---

**Undercut Pillar (Structural Hazard)**

Critical load-bearing seam. Clearly thinned. Hairline cracks above. Small debris falling intermittently. Water drip from above seam.

- Body Check: reinforce, shore up, or stabilize
- If damaged in combat: Partial collapse risk

---

**Unstable Cave — Pressure Trap**

Three visible cracks in the ceiling. Loose shale underfoot. The path forward is rubble — recent fall, still settling. The silence has weight.

**The Pressure Die:** Place a d6 showing 1 on the table. Every loud or forceful action rolls the die — on a 6, the ceiling drops (DC 16 to dodge).

| Action | Pressure Roll? | Notes |
|--------|----------------|-------|
| Inspect ceiling | No | Observe/Sense check reveals crack severity |
| Stabilize cracks | Yes on fail | Slow — DC 13 Body or Mind |
| Dig through rubble | Yes | 3 successes to clear |
| Blast through | Yes + Yes | Two rolls |
| Look around | No | May find buried support beam — DC 13 to place; removes Pressure Die on success |
| Flee | No | Always open |

---

### Lower Depth

**Rodent Burrow Basin**

Shallow bowl-shaped cavern. Evidence of drowned burrows. Fungal patches regrowing. Predator tracks. Fairer terrain for an intentional fight.

---

**Predator Ambush Corridor**

Narrow serpentine tunnel. Twisting S-curve. One elevated shelf, one soft floor zone. Most lethal space in the dungeon. Confined rules fully active.

---

**Deep Stream Channel**

Natural underground stream. Wider. Fast-moving water. Predator avoids strong flow — usable as a safety boundary. Water here originates deeper, not from irrigation.

---

**Old Flow Channel (Deepest Layer)**

Sealed limestone throat, partially blocked long ago by watchtower engineers to stabilize water pressure. Below it: a larger, older, untouched cavern system. And in it — the Greater Stonecoil.

## Factions

### The Borrowers

Small subterranean mole-like humanoids.

**What they do:** Harvest fungal mats for fuel and insulation. Trap cave rodents for protein. Divert water to prevent flooding.

**Current state:** Flooded storage chambers, collapsed nursery tunnels, fungal rot in food stores, predator movement patterns shifting. They widened their tunnel network for survival — each action small and logical, together causing structural compromise.

*Sympathetic. Distrustful. Occasionally losing members. Organized enough to have mitigation systems. Not helpless. Under stress.*

**Borrower adaptations against Stonecoils:**
- **Listening Posts** — clay bowls pressed to stone at choke points; if the bowl rattles, withdraw
- **Soft-Foot Wrappings** — woven fungal-fiber sandals that reduce vibration transmission
- **Decoy Scrapers** — loose stones wedged in cracks to discourage predator ambush points
- **Narrow Crawl Lanes** — tunnels too small for Stonecoils; they don't dominate the space, they survive it

They have a word for the vibration before a Stonecoil strike. They leave food scraps at certain junctions to redirect hunting paths. They tell stories about "the stone that moves."

If the party observes Borrower behavior, they can learn predator patterns without fighting first. Borrowers may refuse to guide players deeper but might offer: a clay bowl tremor detector, a map of "moving stone" zones, a narrow bypass tunnel, or the warning: *"Do not run."*

---

### The Stonecoils

Blind ambush reptilians. Several have grown large due to increased diet of Borrowers and Fenwool.

**Key traits:** Hunt via vibration. Prefer narrow choke points. Low metabolic rate. Territorial but not social. Normally confined to deep caves — increased water flow has expanded their range upward.

Players can avoid lethal engagement by noticing:
- Scratch marks at knee height
- Shed skin
- Rhythmic scraping in stone
- Gnawed cave rodent carcasses near choke points
- Airflow shift in certain cracks

Mind checks can reveal: the predator prefers ambush over open fight; it won't cross heavy water flow; it avoids bright open chambers; it uses the same choke point repeatedly.

---

### The Greater Stonecoil

Scaled mass thick as a cart axle. Slow pulse breathing. Scarred hide. Long dormancy cycles. Deep territorial anchoring.

It is not roaming. It is below. Sleeping or conserving. It has no interest in the surface — until pressure forces it.

**Release conditions:**
- *Primary* — Pressure 4: structural failure in Old Flow Channel destabilizes the barrier
- *Secondary* — Abrupt water diversion: players redirect underground flow too aggressively or seal seepage suddenly; rapid pressure change travels downward, releasing it before Pressure 4

When released, it does not stalk. It moves. Slow, heavy, unstoppable through narrow stone. Bursts through weakened seams. Collapses marginal tunnels. Kills lesser Stonecoils incidentally. Destroys Borrower infrastructure if in path. Avoids strong open water flow. Seeks deeper stable rock.

It is relocating. The damage is collateral.

**Player options:** Hide in reinforced chamber behind the heavy door. Climb vertical shaft. Retreat to surface. Get caught in shifting geometry.

**If players prevented release:** System stabilizes. Lesser Stonecoils remain apex. Borrowers survive. Farmland mostly intact.

**If it passes through:** Split Seam widens permanently. Undercut Pillar destroyed. Flood Shelf partially collapses. New sinkhole opens. Predator ecosystem resets.

---

## Player Pathways

**Mediation Arc** — Broker a water diversion agreement. Help reinforce structural support.
Requires: Mind checks (deduction), Soul checks (trust), Body checks (reinforcement work).

**Surgical Intervention Arc** — Quietly reinforce weakened chamber. Collapse unstable section.
Outcome: sinkholes stop. Borrowers survive. Tension remains unresolved.

**Escalation Arc** — Side with the surface. Clear Borrowers out.
Outcome: surface stabilized. Borrowers displaced.

## The Builders (Background Layer)

The watchtower was built by an old guild of border wardens and structural engineers who understood the limestone was unstable and built to manage it. The tower was abandoned when the border shifted. Maintenance ceased. Borrowers moved in generations later.

Their stonework includes shallow repeating marks carved along stress lines and load vectors. The reinforced chamber's survival is not magic — it is structurally sound. The marks make the stone behave. No glowing. No humming. Indistinguishable from excellent masonry.

Players may notice the recurring mark pattern if they look closely. They do not learn spells. They notice that the marks always follow stress lines — geometry-aware binding disguised as engineering, or engineering disguised as metaphysics.

---

## Aftermath

Days or weeks later: surveyors arrive. Quiet. Stone-scrapers, measuring rods, chalk, line plumbs. They examine the pillar seam, the door lintel crack, the water line in the Flood Shelf, the scoring degradation. They don't accuse. They assess.

If the reinforced chamber held, a nod. If it cracked, a longer look at the lintel.

They repair. Re-score the cracked seam. Slightly modify the irrigation path. They leave.

If the Greater Stonecoil passed through, one of them might quietly say: *"Deep displacement event."* Professional language. Controlled.

Players just intersected a larger system without being the center of it.

## Structural Logic Summary

- Irrigation increases seepage into Flood Shelf Chamber
- Borrowers widened Split Seam
- Undercut Pillar now compromised
- Predator range expanded upward
- Rodent population displaced
- Sinkholes form above Flood Shelf

All physically coherent. No magic required.
