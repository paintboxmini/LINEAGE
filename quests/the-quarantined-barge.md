# The Quarantined Barge

*A Tales Untold adventure module — the first job at Vulture's Nest, and the one the party does not get to decline, because their own freight is inside it. Location context is `places/vultures-nest.md`; the scene that starts it is written beat by beat there, under The Opening Scene.*

---

## Tone

Nobody is lying. Nobody is a villain. Every person in this adventure is doing the reasonable thing from where they are standing, and the sum of all that reasonableness is a boat full of people nobody will feed.

The horror, so far as there is any, is administrative.

---

## Opening Conditions

A People of Promise ritual barge is held at the Nest and the Compact will not release it. The berth it was moored at is impounded entire, and everything else on that stretch of dock went into a Compact warehouse behind a question nobody can answer (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Quarantined Barge).

**The party's freight is in that warehouse.** That is the whole of their motive and it does not need dressing up. Nobody hires them. They want their things back and the man who can release them has a problem he cannot solve.

---

## The Shape of It

**This is a negotiation, not a dungeon.** There is a boat, three people who each need a different thing before it can leave, a man in a cell who will not give any of them what they want, and a clock that none of them know about.

The party's job is to find the one answer that satisfies enough of the Compact at once. There are several, they cost different things, and **two of them are bad for people the party has not met yet.**

---

## The Three Wants (GM Reference)

The Compact is a council of three and they are not aligned. Learning this *is* the first act.

**Bartho wants it gone quietly.** Not resolved — gone. He does not need to know what the disease is, he needs the stretch of dock working and no story attached to it afterwards. He will take a solution that is merely plausible if it is also quiet, and he is the hardest of the three to move with information, because information is not what he is short of.

**Jonas cannot make the paperwork balance.** This is the real lock, and it is worth letting the party discover that the timid one is the immovable one. His ledger is cross-referenced against Quartermaster Voss's intake at Eclipseria South Gate (`places/vultures-nest.md`, Key NPCs). He physically cannot write *unregistered communicable material, origin unconfirmed* and have the system still work — there is no such category, and inventing one is the single thing he will not do, because the ledger's accuracy is the only reason the Nest's whole arrangement is tolerated.

**Give Jonas a true category and the barge leaves.** That is the clean solution and it has a price: a true category means naming what it is and where it came from, in a book the Regency reads.

**Harlock does not care, and now does.** He has been indifferent to the barge from the start — right up until the impound shortened his frontage, he put the Arcadia into the dock, and Bartho sent Jonas aboard to count his hold (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Opening Scene). **He is now the most motivated man on the council** and he wants the fastest dirty answer available. He is an ally of convenience and a genuinely dangerous one: every solution he proposes works, and most of them get somebody killed.

---

## Corvel

Held, not mistreated. He will talk to anyone who comes, at length, warmly, and he will give them almost everything.

**He will not recant** — there is nothing he considers wrong to admit to, so there is nothing to extract, and an interrogation gets a theology lecture delivered by a man who is pleased you asked.

**He will not say where the barge came from,** and this is not stubbornness. Naming Pneum sends a port that has already decided the word is *cursed* to an island of people whose entire faith requires their symptoms to be **visible** (`places/pneum.md`). He knows exactly what the Nest would do with that address. He is not protecting a secret. He is protecting people from a misunderstanding he can see coming and cannot correct fast enough.

**A party that works that out has the real lever on him.** He will name Pneum to somebody he believes will get there first and explain it properly. That is the thing to earn, and it cannot be bought.

---

## What Nobody Knows

*This is the spine I would run, and it is a proposal rather than canon — the adventure works without it as a pure negotiation, just smaller. Everything below is severable.*

**One of the people on that barge is genuinely sick.**

Not collected. Not chosen. Something they picked up on the river like anyone else picks something up, and it is killing them, and **nobody aboard can tell the difference** — because the congregation's entire diagnostic framework is *rarity is prestige and symptoms are witness*. A body producing new and dramatic signs is a body doing well. The sicker this person gets, the more admired they are.

**Name:** **Sabel**, the youngest aboard. *Rename freely.*

**What this does to the adventure:**

- **It gives the Compact a real reason to be right for the wrong reasons.** They were wrong about why the barge is dangerous. They were not wrong that something aboard it is.
- **It makes the clock real and invisible.** Nothing on the dock is getting worse. One person on the boat is, and only a party that actually goes aboard and looks at people as patients rather than as a category will notice.
- **It refuses to let anyone be right.** The port's fear is bigoted and correct. The faith is sincere and is killing a child by mistaking a fever for a blessing. The party is the only group present with no theory to protect.
- **It is the honest cost of the theology** rather than a punishment of it. These people are warm, communal and completely sincere (`places/pneum.md`, Tone). The flaw is not cruelty. It is that a framework which reads all illness as communion has no word for *this one is different*, and so cannot see it.

**How the party can notice.** Nothing arcane. Sabel is the one whose symptoms the others are proud of and who is not proud of them. The congregation will happily point them out as the most blessed person aboard. A party that talks to Sabel alone gets somebody frightened who has not got the vocabulary to say so, in a community where being frightened of it is the one thing that cannot be admitted.

---

## Pressure — The Dock Decides To Help

**Somewhere in the middle of this, the Nest stops waiting.**

A quarantine that goes on long enough stops being a Compact matter and becomes a neighbourhood one. Dockhands who are being paid to not unload something have had days to talk about it. Somebody's cousin coughed. Somebody heard the word *cursed* enough times that it turned into a plan.

**They come for the barge with pitch and a lit torch, at night, in a group.**

**The encounter.** Six frightened dockworkers, **Mind 1 / Body 1 / Soul 1, HP 6 each**. Against three starting characters that is an even fight — a real one, roughly a coin flip (`rules/gm-guide.md`, How many of them). It is deliberately not a monster: **the hardest fight in the adventure is a crowd that has decided to solve the problem itself**, and every one of them is somebody the party bought fish from that morning.

**Scale it by headcount, not by stats.** Four is a fight the party wins and remembers; eight is a wipe. Add or remove neighbours.

**And it should be losable in the useful direction.** The party can talk them down, block the gangway, wake Bartho, buy them off, or simply stand in front of it. **If the party wins by hurting six locals, they have solved tonight and made every later conversation in this town harder**, and the GM should let that land without comment. Bartho will not say anything about it either. He will simply be different afterwards.

## Ways It Ends

Each of these closes the job. They are not equally good and the party should not be told which is which.

**The clean one — give Jonas a category.** Learn what it actually is, name Pneum, and let it into a ledger the Regency reads. The berth opens the same afternoon and the party's freight comes out. **Cost:** the Regency now has the address of an island of people who display disease on purpose, and the party handed it over. Nothing bad happens immediately. That is the point.

**The quiet one — Bartho's answer.** The barge leaves under escort, at night, upriver, with somebody credible vouching that it is going home and not stopping. **Cost:** nothing is recorded, so nothing is learned, and if Sabel is aboard and untreated the party will not find out how that ended for a long time — or ever.

**Harlock's answer.** It leaves tonight, no paperwork, and he owes the party a favour worth having. **Cost:** he does not much mind whether it arrives, and the terms of his help are not the terms he states.

**Someone burns it.** Either the mob succeeds or a party member decides they are right. This resolves the Compact's problem completely and permanently and is the only ending nobody in the town objects to, which is the most damning thing about it.

**The one that costs them.** Get Sabel off the barge and to actual care, which means admitting to the congregation that one of their number is not in communion but simply ill — a thing they have no framework to hear and will resist warmly, at length, without ever raising their voices. **This is the hardest ending and the only one where somebody lives who otherwise does not.** It also delays everything else, and the party's freight stays in a warehouse the whole time.

## What It Leaves Running

- **Pneum.** Whichever way it ends, the word *cursed* points upriver, and whoever follows it meets people who chose this, are proud of it, and will explain warmly why he has it backwards (`places/pneum.md`). For Pat this is the first lead of his actual search and the first lesson in what the search is really made of (`campaign/session-1-convergence.md`).
- **Corvel.** If he is released, he goes home and remembers who did it. If he is not, he stays exactly as pleasant as he was on day one.
- **Harlock's hold.** Jonas is still counting the Arcadia the entire time this is happening (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Arcadia's hold). The barge job is the foreground and the crate is the background, and they are competing for the same afternoon.
- **Erubeth**, who watched the party handle all of it and is deciding whether they are worth talking to (`characters/erubeth.md`).

## Not Yet Set

- **Whether Sabel lives**, and what the congregation does with the fact afterwards — whether it cracks anything in the theology or gets absorbed into it. The second is more likely and more interesting.
- **What Sabel actually has.** Deliberately unnamed: it should be ordinary, curable, and unremarkable, which is the whole joke.
- **Who escorts the barge upriver** in the quiet ending, and whether the Night Ferryman would do it (`places/vultures-nest.md`, Notable Roles).
- **Whether the dock mob has a face.** A named dockhand the party met earlier makes the fight much worse and much better. Left open so a GM can pick whoever the table actually liked.
