**[2026-08-17] `agent-tools/conserve.py` — the second stated-but-unchecked invariant is now enforced, and the old verification method turns out to have been unable to catch the bug it was aimed at.** *Content is conserved across a restructure: a pure move loses no content line and duplicates none.* It had been verified once, by hand, on the entry-sorting pass — 1,591 lines before, 1,591 after. **That proves the totals matched; it does not prove the same lines came out the other side.** Injecting a deliberately botched move into `bestiary/skeinwing/` — two lines dropped, one block copied, exactly the shape of the reference-rewrite bug that hit three separate times in one session — produced **2,433 content lines before and 2,433 after**, identical, with one line lost outright and another silently duplicated. Two faults in opposite directions cancel perfectly in a total. `conserve.py` compares the multiset of normalised content lines instead, and named both. **It cannot live in `verify.py`**, because conservation is a property of a *transition* rather than a state — it needs a before as well as an after. So it is two commands run either side of the move: `snapshot` over the scope about to change, then `check`. Lost lines and duplicated lines both fail; newly written navigation is reported and never fails, because a restructure legitimately adds it. **Headings are exempt from the duplication failure on purpose** — splitting one file into four legitimately repeats `## Contents` four times, and duplicate headings *within* a file are already `check_entry_structure`. **The negative test caught itself failing to fire.** The first injection picked a file too small to hold the fault, raised `IndexError` before writing anything, and the check that followed reported a clean PASS — accurately, since nothing had changed. Only retrying against a large enough file, with an assertion that the injection actually landed, produced the finding above. **Fifth instance this session of a check reading identical whether or not it exercised anything**, and the first one caught by the discipline itself rather than by luck. `conserve.py --selftest` now asserts all four behaviours: deletion caught, duplication caught, heading exemption honoured, unchanged corpus clean. `agent-tools/invariants.md` gains an **Enforced on demand** section; only the exile gap remains stated-and-unchecked. 16/16 verify.

**[2026-08-17] Invariants moved out of `rules/`, rewritten as an index — and testing one found a live engine bug.** Drew, on why he'd stopped tracking them: *"I couldn't make a clear distinction about what they actually are"*, then *"pull the invariants out of rules that's the wrong home for it."* **The file's own empty Confirmed section was the diagnosis.** It had sat empty since creation while prose candidates accumulated above it, catching nothing, in a session where executable assertions found eleven real errors in an afternoon. **Now `agent-tools/invariants.md`**, beside the checks it indexes rather than among rules it isn't — a rule tells a player what happens, an invariant tells a builder what must never stop being true. It states the two tests that separate them (*can you violate it and have nothing be wrong?* and *can it fail?*), lists all **17 enforced invariants against the check that enforces each**, and names the two that are **stated but unchecked** rather than letting enforced and unenforced look alike. **Testing a candidate instead of assuming it found a real bug.** The candidate read *"derived stats are computed live, never cached"* — stated as an implementation, and simply false: `max_hp` was stored and patched on every stat change. Restated as the property that matters — *max HP tracks current stats* — it failed **154 of 1,447 random stat changes**. `eff()` floors a stat at 0 while `adjust()` debited max HP by the raw delta, so a stat driven under-water and back up left max HP wrong, self-correcting often enough to never get caught. A first fix (patch by effective delta) cut it to 2, the remainder being `max_hp`'s own floor of 1 losing history the same way one level up. Fixed properly by making `max_hp` a computed property over a fixed baseline: **zero divergences across 8,894 stat changes on 2,000 combatants**, bespoke boss HP still tracking correctly. **And the first version of that test was itself a false pass** — it guarded on `hasattr(c,'adjust_stat')`, a method that doesn't exist, so it applied no stat changes at all and reported clean. Third instance today of a check that doesn't exercise its invariant reading identical to one that does. 14/14 verify.
