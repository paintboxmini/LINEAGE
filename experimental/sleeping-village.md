# The Sleeping Village

*Name pending.*

An island. The party arrives to find a single large building. Inside: rows and rows of beds. An IV in every arm. One old man moves between them, changing the drips. He is unhurried. He has been doing this for a long time.

Everyone is asleep.

---

## The Two Layers

**The waking island** — the building, the beds, the old man, the equipment. Sparse. Functional. The shell.

**The dreaming island** — the same place, but the real one. Bigger. Space and distance don't hold consistently. The people live their entire lives here. They were born in it. They'll die in it. The waking world is just where their bodies are kept.

To visit the dreaming island, you go under. The old man can put you there. He says it plainly: *"The only way to the real island is to go under."*

---

## Inside the Dream

Casually strange. Not dramatic — just slightly wrong in the way dreams are wrong when you're not quite lucid enough to catch it.

- A woman washes clothes in the river. The clothes keep trying to roll in the mud on the bank, like a dog that just got a bath. She sighs and pulls them back.
- A fully roasted pig on a fire, slowly spinning, chewing the apple in its own mouth.
- Buildings that are further away when you walk toward them, closer when you stop trying.
- Conversations that started before the party arrived and will continue after they leave.

The people are not confused. This is normal to them. The party is the strange thing.

---

## When Night Falls

The village stays warm and lit. The outer landscape does not.

When darkness comes, what's beyond the edge of the village turns nightmarish. Things move in it. The villagers don't go out after dark — not because they were told not to, but because everyone learned not to a long time ago.

It may not have always been this way. That may be why the party is here.

## The Old Man

Sole waking resident. Caretaker. Has been maintaining the IVs long enough that he doesn't think about it anymore. He knows the dreaming island — he has been under, once or twice, years ago. He doesn't go anymore.

He will send the party under without ceremony. He'll tell them one thing before they go: *"Night comes there too."*

## Tone

Pan's Labyrinth. A mythological journey where the logic is internal and consistent even when it makes no surface sense. The dream isn't random — it has rules. Learning the rules is how you survive the night.

The casually strange details should feel lived-in, not performed. The villagers are not putting on a show. This is Tuesday.
