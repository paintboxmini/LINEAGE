# Canille

A small island village in the Unheld, northeast of Vulture's Nest. The water around it is unusually still. Ships stop here if they know it's there.

It was founded as a research colony. Three generations later it is a village, and the research is still ongoing — in the way that something becomes ongoing when the original question gets quietly replaced by a different one.

---

## The Place

Stone buildings, low to the ground. Gardens that run toward the water. A central square with benches sized for people and, along the lower edges, worn smooth spots in the stone the right size for something else to rest.

Hooks are mounted beside every door. This is true of the inn, the kitchen, the hall, the workshop, the homes. The hooks hold masks.

The masks are white. Each one smiles.

---

## The Cubs

Quadrupedal. Pug-faced with proportions that are almost human — wide-set eyes, flattened features, something in the skull that reads wrong in a way that takes a moment to name. Fully furred. Around 120 pounds. They move low and fast when comfortable, slower when watched.

Three generations. The oldest are grey-muzzled, patient, deliberate. The middle generation grew up in the village. The youngest have never known anything else.

They speak. They reason. They have preferences and moods and opinions that show clearly on their faces — when their faces are visible.

**What they know:** The oldest first-generation cubs have the clearest sense that something is off — that the bar keeps moving, that the masks cover something the villagers won't name directly. Most don't have the language for it. One or two do.

The youngest mostly don't ask. The masks have always been there. The warmth has always been there. The two feel connected and they don't yet know they aren't.

---

## The Masks

White. A simple curved smile. Black oval eyes.

Each building has its own set — different widths of smile, different eye shapes, but always smiling. A cub entering the workshop puts on the workshop mask. The inn has its own. The hall has its own. Private homes vary by family, chosen by preference.

The cub does not choose the mask. They choose the building. The mask is already there.

Outside — the open paths, the waterfront, the untended spaces between structures — there is no mask requirement. This is not formally stated anywhere. It is simply understood.

**What the masks do not do:** the cubs cannot pass as human. These masks are not disguise. They are ceremony. The village needs the performance of personhood even knowing it's a performance. The smile at the door is a transaction: *I will behave appropriately in your space. Your space will treat me accordingly.*

No mask shows anything other than happy.

---

## The Villagers

Three generations here as well. The original scientists are old now — some sharp, some softened, all certain they acted with good intent. They have spent decades finding new reasons why the cubs haven't quite met the threshold yet. The threshold is not written down. It never has been.

Their children grew up alongside the first-generation cubs. They have never thought of the arrangement as strange. They are the warmest generation — genuinely fond, genuinely convinced it is kind. They taught their children the mask customs the way you teach any custom: without explanation, because explanation implies it could be questioned.

The third generation is the most comfortable. The cubs have always been there. The hooks have always been by the door. Some have a particular cub they grew up alongside, speak to easily, would be genuinely upset to lose. The thought that something is wrong has never formed. The pieces are all visible. They don't assemble.

---

## Key Locations

**The Founding Hall** — the original laboratory, now the village's common gathering space. The equipment is long gone. The bones of the place remain: the layout, the venting, the reinforced floor that was built for containment. The current generation doesn't know what it was. Nobody told them.

**The Kennels** — a long, low building on the eastern edge of the village. Warm, well-kept, no locks. The village records call it the Residence. The word used in conversation is the Kennels. Nobody considers this a contradiction.

**The Waterfront** — the one place on Canille where masks are never worn. Cubs move here freely. Some villagers have noticed the cubs behave differently at the waterfront. None have asked about it.

**The Old One's House** — the oldest living scientist, at the edge of the village. She doesn't come to the hall much. She watches the cubs from her window. She has a name for what they are — the real name, the answer to the original question — that she has not said out loud in thirty years. She is the last one who knows it.

*Name pending.*

---

## Tone

The village is pleasant. The people are kind. The masks are on the hooks and have always been on the hooks.

The wrongness of Canille is not cruelty. It is the warmth with which something extraordinary was made small, and kept there, by people who genuinely loved it.

---

## The Question

The original question was: *can we create a new sentient life?*

The village stopped asking it. That is not the same as answering no.
