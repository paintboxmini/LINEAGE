# Kevin's Ingredients

What actually loads into the pepper grinder, and what goes into the beverages. The whole point of an artificer-by-cooking build: what he can make is gated by what he's actually gotten his hands on, not a free list. Staged here, alongside `experimental/kevin.md`.

## The loads

Each seasoning carries one bonus, and that bonus is added to GRIND SHOT's Effect when the grinder is loaded with it (`experimental/kevin-cards.md`). One load at a time; spent when the shot resolves.

| Load | Bonus added to GRIND SHOT's Effect | Source |
|------|------------------------------------|--------|
| **Plain** | None. It fires; that's all it does. | Anywhere |
| **Sapphire crystal** | Defender gains Vulnerable | `bestiary/sapphire-ant.md` — 2 copper at Senshi's counter |
| **Cinder flake** | Deal +3 damage | `bestiary/scorchback-beetle.md` — the Cindersink |
| **Hush petal** | Defender gains Rooted | `bestiary/hush-bloom.md` — the Turnroot Weald |
| **Quill grind** | Apply Initiative Shift -2 to the defender | `bestiary/shockquills.md` — the Glasslight scree |
| **Bellows dust** | Push the defender to the Backline | `bestiary/blowback-beetle.md` — the Ashfall scrub |

**Ingredients don't have a colour.** An early draft coded them Red/Blue/Green to match the stat colours, and that was a mistake — it implied a seasoning could only go on a card of its own colour, which is not how any of this works. A seasoning is a seasoning. What it does is what it does, and it does that regardless of what card it rides on.

Deliberately one bonus each, and deliberately small. GRIND SHOT is already a d8 on Kevin's best stat; the load is a rider on a good card, not the reason the card is good. The spread across Vulnerable / damage / Rooted / initiative means the choice of load is a read on the fight rather than a strict upgrade path — cinder flake is not simply the best one.

**Bellows dust does not separate you from the target — it relocates the fight.** GRIND SHOT already moves Kevin to the Backline, so a bellows round sends both of you there together. That reads like a bug and isn't one: it is how he peels a single enemy off the party. He walks into the Frontline, shoots one thing, and the two of them arrive in the Backline while everyone else keeps fighting where they were. The load is for extraction, not for making space.

**Plain rounds are the floor and they matter.** He is never out of ammunition, only out of *charged* ammunition, which is what makes restocking a story rather than a bookkeeping problem (`experimental/kevin.md`, Supply and restocking).

## The beverages

Prepared drinks, handed over by SERVE (`experimental/kevin-cards.md`) or drunk by Kevin himself. Same craft as the rounds — a rare ingredient worked into something that keeps — and the same supply model: made in downtime, gated by what he's actually got.

The effect lives here rather than on the card, so a new recipe never needs SERVE reprinted.

| Drink | Effect on the drinker | Charge |
|-------|-----------------------|--------|
| **Bitter Shot** | Gain Quick | Plain — no rare ingredient |
| **The Early Cup** | Apply Initiative Shift +1 to yourself | Plain — no rare ingredient |
| **Still Water** | Gain Ward and heal 3 HP | Hush petal |
| **Second Wind Cider** | Gain Resist and draw 1 | Cinder flake |
| **Hair of the Quill** | Apply Initiative Shift +2 to yourself and gain Quick | Quill grind |

**The two plain ones are the floor**, the same way plain rounds are. He can always hand someone a Bitter Shot; what he can't always do is hand them a Hair of the Quill. Anything above the basic tier costs a rare ingredient and a session of work, and yields **two servings** per measure — drinks are cheaper per unit than oranges and dearer than rounds, because a mouthful goes further than a shell and less far than a seed.

Stronger recipes than these presumably exist and get worked out at the table as he actually makes them (`rules/items.md`, Crafting).

## Already in canon — no invention needed

**Sapphire Ant Seasoning Crystals** (`places/capital/gilded-tusk.md`, `bestiary/sapphire-ant.md`) — dried, ground azure ants. Already established: genuine umami, and a **blue shimmer** on whatever it's added to. That blue shimmer is sitting right there as a Blue/Mind-coded seasoning already, cheap (2 copper, "add to any dish") and sold at Senshi's counter. First real candidate for pepper-grinder ammo that needs nothing new written — just picked up.

**Senshi himself** (`places/capital/gilded-tusk.md`) — cook, Thessian, runs the Naturalist Collection: an entire menu of monster ingredients with real mechanical properties already attached (Gene-Thief Tartare grants damage-type resistance based on what the creature absorbed; the Bicolor Spider's two halves have different flavor profiles). Not yet decided whether he's Kevin's supplier, teacher, rival, or something else — but he's the obvious connection point once Kevin reaches the Capital, and his existing menu is the proof of concept for everything Kevin's trying to do.

## New — proposed, not yet built

**Shockquills.** **Built** — `bestiary/shockquills.md`, and the only one of the four that is a combat encounter rather than a harvest. Keratin quills on dry quartz scree, charged by friction and nothing else. Ground into **quill grind**, which makes a drink or a grinder round arrive before it should.

Its gate is the sharpest of the four: the charge sets in the quills at the moment of death, so **quill grind only comes off a Shockquill killed at 3 or more Charge**. Kill it fast and safe and you get fletching. Getting the ingredient means letting a small dangerous animal become a large dangerous animal first, on purpose, and then handling it at full charge.

**Scorchback Beetle.** **Built** — `bestiary/scorchback-beetle.md`. Stores volatile plant oils in an abdominal gland and vents them under pressure, the same mechanism a bombardier beetle uses. Dried and ground into **cinder flake**: capsaicin-grade burn, faint ember-glow, the hot counterpart to Sapphire Ant's cool blue shimmer. Lives on the warm ground at the Cindersink margins. The gland has to come out whole, which means either a torpid beetle before dawn or accepting a near-boiling spray — the harvest is the gate.

**Hush Bloom.** **Built** — `bestiary/hush-bloom.md`. A pale flower of the Turnroot Weald's deep shade that only opens in genuine stillness and closes at the first vibration. Dried petals keep indefinitely and impart a calm, numbing-sweet flavor with a soft green luminescence in liquid — the base of Kevin's better drinks as much as a grinder load. Its gate is patience rather than danger, which is the exact inverse of the Scorchback's, and a cook who reliably produces both is proving something.

**Blowback Beetle.** **Built** — `bestiary/blowback-beetle.md`. The Scorchback's dog-sized cousin: same volatile oils, swallowed instead of vented, held in a bellows organ behind the jaw and expelled forward through the mouth. It does not burn you, it hits you and you are somewhere else. Ground, the organ yields **bellows dust**.

Its gate is the bluntest of the five and the only one that is simply a fight: nineteen HP behind Armour 2 that shoves your front rank into the back rank every time it connects. No dawn timing, no sitting still, no charge threshold to judge — you just have to beat it.

All five are real now, and each is gated a different way: Sapphire Ant is **bought**, the Scorchback is **dangerous to harvest**, the Hush Bloom is **slow to harvest**, the Shockquill is **dangerous to harvest correctly**, and the Blowback simply has to be **beaten in a fight**. Five ingredients, five different reasons a party might not have one — and between them the loads now cover damage, Vulnerable, Rooted, initiative, and forced movement, which is the whole vocabulary a shot can speak in.
