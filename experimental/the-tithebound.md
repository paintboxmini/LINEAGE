# The Tithebound

*The Shunka's ancient enemy, and the people responsible for the royal curse — kept in `experimental/` alongside `experimental/the-shunka.md` until ready for `world/races/`.*

## What's Settled

**Why they invaded.** Their own ground was failing them — worn thin, unable to hold them, for reasons the Shunka never fully understood and the Tithebound never explained. Not glory, not tribute. They needed somewhere else to stand, and the Shunka had ground worth taking. The full war and the curse that came out of its failure: `experimental/the-shunka.md`.

**Appearance.** Animalistic, lizard-folk — a heavier build than a snake, closer to a Komodo dragon but with sharper, more angular features than the real animal's blunt lines. Bipedal on land, walking hunched rather than upright — a posture that reads as awkward, almost labored, right up until they're in water. **Far more dangerous submerged than on land** — whatever makes the land gait look uncertain disappears entirely once they're swimming. The hunch is a body built for something else, not a body being bad at walking.

**Ecosystem.** An island people whose whole economy runs on the sea, not the land — almost entirely aquatic diet, built around what open water and reef provide rather than what soil grows. That's the actual shape of "their ground failing them": not dirt going bad, but a fishing people whose sea stopped feeding them enough. A smaller, harder island was always survivable for a people this at home in water; a smaller island with a collapsing catch wasn't.

**Belief system — the Tithe.** Everything alive owes something for the fact of being alive at all, and the Tithebound are the one people in this history who built that fact into an actual religion instead of just living inside it unspoken. Regular, ritual payment — of catch, of something dearer when the catch wasn't enough — kept the debt serviced with whatever the sea actually is to them: provider, creditor, the thing that gave and could just as easily stop giving. Most peoples treat Price (`world/creation-myth-the-three-cuts.md`, the Second Cut) as a background fact of existing. The Tithebound treated it as a transaction they could see and negotiate with directly — which is exactly the theological groundwork that made the curse-worker's final act thinkable at all. If debt can be paid to the sea, the horrifying next thought is whether debt could be forced onto someone who never took the loan out. She was the one who found out it could.

**Survivors exist.** Not extinct — a diminished, scattered remnant, presumably still holding whatever's left of their home islands (the smaller, fragmented landmass shown southeast of the Shunka homeland on the map).

## What's Open

**Whether the curse-technique survived her.** The original curse-worker paid her own existence to force the Price onto the Shunka queen's line (`experimental/the-shunka.md`) — she's gone, not just dead, unmade entirely. Whether what she knew died with her, or whether other Tithebound can still do what she did, is a real open question with real campaign weight: a lost, one-time horror versus a standing threat.

**What they actually tithe to.** Called "the sea" here because that's the honest read given an aquatic economy, but whether that's literal, personified, a named entity, or something else again isn't decided.

**Current relationship to the Shunka, and to outsiders generally.** Not stated either way — reclusive and licking old wounds, actively hostile, indifferent, all still on the table.
