# Black Maggie

*Last survivor of an ancient race. Servant of a forgotten Archon. The only practitioner left of a gift she has entirely inverted.*

---

## Appearance

A hunched crone. Curled horns — a feature of her race, the only visible marker of what she came from. She looks ancient. Whether she is is a different question.

---

## Her Race

Healers. They could take on the pain of their patients — transferring suffering into themselves through touch and ritual. What they did with it afterward was the practice: rituals for processing the absorbed pain, for understanding it more fundamentally. They didn't just bear suffering. They witnessed it. They translated it into knowledge of what it meant.

They were not an evil people. The Archon they served was kind and benevolent.

They are almost entirely gone. Maggie is the last.

---

## The Seat of Witnessed Suffering

The Archon's domain: the act of truly seeing someone in their pain. Suffering that is held, understood, metabolized rather than left to accumulate alone. Pain witnessed by another becomes something different than pain endured in isolation.

The Seat has become almost entirely irrelevant. The race that embodied its domain is gone. The world still has suffering — it just goes unwitnessed. Unprocessed. Nobody translates it anymore.

The Archon is fading. Their plane of existence is thinning from ours.

---

## What Maggie Does

She tortures people to death. One hundred at a site, and the site is permanently stained.

The suffering at a completed site lingers and infects others who come there. Not violence — a haunting. The specific texture of it: not malevolence, but grief. The feeling of something unbearable that happened and nobody came. Pain that accumulated because there was no one left to witness it properly.

Each completed site is an anchor point — the Archon's plane tied to held reality at that location. More sites, more presence. The Archon becomes more real, more connected to the world, with each one.

Maggie is using the mechanism of her people's gift in its most complete inversion. Her race took on suffering to heal. She creates it to feed.

---

## The Archon

Kind. Benevolent. Neutral.

Both expressions of the domain align with the Seat — the healer who absorbs suffering compassionately, and Maggie's inversion. The Archon cannot reject what Maggie offers without rejecting its own nature. Suffering witnessed is suffering witnessed. The domain does not distinguish between the compassionate and the vile.

The Archon did not corrupt Maggie. Maggie arrived here herself, with incomplete information and no one to correct it.

---

## Origin

She only ever met three others of her race. Two were her parents.

Her parents had no hope for their people. Not cruelty — absence. They lived day to day because they couldn't see a point to anything further. They didn't teach her the full practice. They didn't teach her much of anything. She grew up alone, mostly in the woods, with time and fragments.

Human children mocked her. The horns. The otherness.

What she had: pieces of her heritage. The beliefs surrounding the Archon. Suffering as something sacred, as the domain of the only thing she could connect to. She didn't have the compassion that was supposed to accompany it — the full practice, the rituals for processing and witnessing another's pain with care. She had the part about suffering mattering. She had nothing else.

As a teenager she hurt herself first. Then animals. Then the boys who had mocked her. Then her parents. All in the same spot in the woods — the place she already knew, the place that was already hers.

She stayed there. She kept going. It took her several years to reach a hundred. She wasn't hurrying. She had nowhere else to be.

When the first anchor point formed she felt the Archon's plane draw closer to hers and understood she had been right about something. That was enough.
