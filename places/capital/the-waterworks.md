# The Waterworks

*A citywide utility layer, spun in directly beneath the capital — not a room under the mine, a floor under the whole city. The Underground Bazaar is a small, hidden pocket inside it, not a layer of its own.*

For the Bazaar and Tavern hidden within, see `places/capital/underground-bazaar.md`, `places/capital/underground-tavern.md`.
For the mine shaft that descends through it, see `places/capital/the-wallows.md`.
For the guild that runs it, see `factions/the-cellar-custodians.md`.

## What It Is

Where the Wallows is a mine — a shaft, a single vertical throat down to Aurora — the Waterworks is a floor. It runs under every district the capital has, low vaulted chambers and channels cut straight into the bedrock, gates and cisterns laced through it like a second, buried city built to a different plan. Built to a different plan because it was — the oldest chambers and channels predate Eclipseria entirely, cut by a kingdom that stood on this ground long before this one and left no name anyone living remembers. The Custodians inherited the bones of it; they didn't build them. What's actually theirs is the gatework, the cisterns, everything that moves water today. What came before, and what happened to it, isn't something anyone currently alive has an answer to. Most of Eclipseria has no idea it's this large. The people who maintain it prefer it that way; a system nobody thinks about is a system nobody interferes with.

Officially, it's water infrastructure. No indoor plumbing runs to any building in the capital — water comes from public wells, fountains, and bathhouses, fed by gravity from cisterns the Custodians fill on the tide's own schedule, not the city's. The tide pushes inland; the Custodians catch that pulse, gate it into the cisterns, and seal the gates before the outflow can take it back. That much is true, and it's most of what's actually down there. The rest is quieter: a handful of deep cisterns that condition and distribute a second set of ingredients through the same channels — a cooled, diluted form of the same binding force the Weavers carry in thread form, moved through open water instead of wire.

The two systems share tunnels because they were laid the same way, for the same reason, by people who never had cause to compare notes. See `factions/the-weavers.md`.

## The Rivers

Four rivers feed the capital's intake, part of the same continent-wide network described in `world/geography-overview.md` — tributaries the surface city rarely names, because it never has to think about where its water comes from.

**The Vein.** The primary intake. Broad, reliable, heavily engineered — most of the city's official water supply runs through here first. Older Custodian logs sometimes call it the First Vein.

**The Mourn.** Colder and deeper, approaching from the higher ground to the north and northeast. Known for sudden temperature drops and fine grey silt. Some of the oldest cisterns still prefer Mourn water, for reasons the Custodians no longer explain to anyone outside the guild.

**The Ledger.** Slower and browner, coming in from the agricultural districts. Used for volume more than quality — the Custodians call it "honest but coarse" and mean it as a compliment.

**The Quiet Arm.** A smaller, partially subterranean tributary that joins the Vein under the oldest parts of the city. Officially just another intake. Unofficially, the most restricted conditioning stations sit on the Arm, because the water there behaves strangely before any treatment is applied to it at all.

## The Deep Stations

Low, vaulted chambers with heavy stone walls, sluice gates set into channels cut straight through old stone. The air is cold and damp, thick with wet rock and standing water. The gates themselves are massive, iron-bound, some ancient, some newer, none idle at the turn of a tide — they groan, thud, and hold against the full weight of an inland pulse the moment they're sealed. A crew that mistimes a gate by even a few minutes either loses the surge entirely or takes on more pressure than the seal was built for.

In the coldest or stillest cisterns, the water carries a faint, cold blue-white glow — the same phenomenon as the Weavers' purple shimmer, subdued and diluted by the medium it's traveling through, but unmistakably related to anyone who's seen both.

## The Hidden Pocket

Tucked into a stretch of the Waterworks that reads as unremarkable maintenance access — a service junction like a hundred others — is the entrance to the Underground Bazaar and, past it, the Underground Tavern. Neither takes up meaningful space against the scale of the Waterworks around them; from a Custodian's maintenance log, it's a few chambers that draw slightly more foot traffic than the rest of the level and get a wider berth during shift changes.

The Bazaar predates most of the current Waterworks infrastructure and was simply built around, not into — the Custodians route channel and cable past it the way a city routes a road around an old building nobody wants to demolish. The mine shaft to the Wallows begins at the Tavern floor, same as it always has. See `places/capital/underground-bazaar.md`, `places/capital/underground-tavern.md`, `places/capital/the-wallows.md`.
