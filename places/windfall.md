# Windfall

*An island village within a day of Vulture's Nest. Warm, hospitable, entirely sincere, and there is nothing here to fix.*

---

## The Principle

**Cause no death.**

That is the whole of it, and they hold it further than anyone the party has met. Not a dietary rule and not a prohibition on murder — **a refusal to be the reason anything stops living.**

So: nothing is hunted. Nothing is fished. Nothing is farmed for slaughter. And the rule runs past animals, because a plant is alive too — **they do not pick.** What Windfall eats is what has already been let go of: fruit that has fallen, nuts on the ground, things the sea puts on the beach, animals found dead of age or accident or another animal's business.

**Take only what has already been given up.** They did not arrive at this by argument. It has been the shape of the place for as long as anyone there can tell you.

---

## What That Produces

**A found meal is sacred**, because it is rare and because nobody was harmed to make it. A deer discovered dead at the treeline is an event. Nobody is casual about it. There is a way it is carried in, and a way it is divided, and everybody knows both.

**And there is one source of already-dead meat on that island that never runs out.**

**They eat their own dead.** All of them, as a matter of course, without exception and without ceremony beyond the ordinary care taken with any found meal. A body that goes into the ground is food that was wasted, and waste is its own kind of harm.

**This is not a secret, a shame, or a thing they conceal from visitors.** Ask and they will tell you, plainly, in the same tone they would use about the tide. It has never occurred to them that the answer might land badly, because it has never landed badly on anybody who lives there.

**There was no murder.** Not once, not historically, not quietly. **Killing a person would break the only rule they have** — the entire practice exists downstream of a refusal to cause death, and a village that killed its old people would have abandoned the principle to keep the diet, which is backwards.

---

## The Volunteers

**When someone is very old, or sick past recovering, they may volunteer.**

This is a decision about *timing*, not about whether — everyone on Windfall is eaten eventually. A volunteer is someone who has decided to stop.

**Plenty never volunteer**, live on as long as they live, die of whatever takes them, and are consumed exactly the same way. Nobody thinks less of them. There is no pressure, no expectation, and no word for someone who declines — because declining is not a category. It is simply what most people do.

*What volunteering actually consists of: they stop eating. They are cared for, sat with, talked to, and not left alone, and it takes as long as it takes. **Nobody lays a hand on them.** This is my reading rather than Drew's stated fact — it is the only version I can find that keeps "they volunteer" and "there was no murder" both completely true, and it matches how the rest of the place thinks. If you'd rather volunteering just means declaring your intent and letting nature finish it, that works too and changes almost nothing.*

## What the Party Actually Experiences

**Get them there on ordinary business.** An escort job — somebody from Windfall wanting to go home, or a passenger who needs seeing to the island. Or a cargo run, hired out of Vulture's Nest, possibly by Bartho himself (`places/vultures-nest.md`). **It should not be a mystery. It should be a delivery.**

**They are greeted warmly.** Genuinely, immediately, without suspicion. Windfall gets few visitors and likes them. There will be an offer of dinner and an offer of a bed for the night, and both are meant.

**And the party is not fed people.** Not as a deception — it simply would not occur to them. A guest is given a found meal: something washed up or fallen or died on its own, cooked carefully, served with the care due to something rare. **What the party eats at that table is exactly what they are told it is.**

**The tell is the volume.**

Not the meal — the village. Everyone here is eating meat. Constantly, at every meal, in a quantity that makes no sense for a small island where nobody hunts and nobody keeps animals, and there is very little of anything else on any plate. A party paying attention will notice that the arithmetic is wrong before anybody says a word to them.

**If they ask, they are answered.** Immediately, fully, without evasion, and probably while the conversation is still going on around them. There is no reveal to stage and no lie to catch. **Anyone who asks a direct question gets a direct answer**, and the discomfort is entirely on the asking side of the table.

---

## GM — There Is No Problem Here

**This is the point of the place and it should be defended.**

The instinct — mine included — is to put something under it. A coercion, a faction that pushes the old toward volunteering, a headman with a quota, a body that was not found dead. **Do not.** All of that is available and all of it is worse, because it converts an unbearable idea the party has to sit with into a villain they can punch.

**Nothing is wrong on Windfall.** The logic is consistent, the consent is real, the hospitality is sincere, and the arithmetic adds up. **If there is a problem, the party brought it** — and that is a legitimate outcome. A party that decides this is an abomination and acts on it is making a choice in a village of people who will not understand what they did wrong, and that is a real scene. It is just not a scene the island prepared for them.

**Pair it with Pneum on purpose.** Both are small isolated villages with a strange practice around bodies, and they are opposites. Pneum is sincere and *factually wrong* — their whole theology rests on a mechanism that does not exist (`places/pneum.md`). Windfall is sincere and *not wrong about anything*; the reasoning is airtight and leads somewhere most people cannot follow. **A party that visits both learns that "strange village" is not one thing**, which is worth more than either village alone.

**Tone: no horror staging.** No lingering camera, no ominous music, no villager smiling too long. These are farmers-who-do-not-farm having a nice evening with visitors. Play the warmth completely straight and let the facts do their own work.

## Not Yet Set

- **What race they are.** Deliberately open. Nothing above depends on it.
- **The island's own name for itself**, and whether *Windfall* is what they call it or what the Nest calls it.
- **What happens if a guest dies there.** The logic is not ambiguous — a body is a found meal and wasting it would be harm — and a party that works this out before anyone gets hurt is having a much better evening than a party that works it out afterwards. **Telegraph it; never spring it.**
- **Whether anyone has ever left.** A person raised on this principle, living in a port that kills things for a living, is a character and nobody has written them.
- **Where exactly the island sits.** Within a day of the Nest; no bearing fixed yet (`world/geography-overview.md`).
