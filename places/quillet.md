# Quillet

*A farm village on the Kings Road's Vulture's Nest spoke, about a day inside the Kings Road Inn on the capital side (`world/geography-overview.md`). Roughly two hundred people. **The only place in the Regency's practical reach that refused the levy and is still standing.***

*A **quillet** is a small parcel of worked land. The village has never once acknowledged that the word also sounds like a pen, and visitors who point it out get a long patient look.*

---

## The Place

Low, wet, orderly. Fields in narrow strips rather than blocks, hedged with hawthorn, each strip walked by the same family for longer than the Regency has been a council. Stone barns with slate roofs and good doors. A mill. A long house at the centre with more windows than anything else in the village, because of what's kept in it.

**Everything here is in better repair than it should be and slightly out of date.** Nothing has been built new in eleven years. Everything standing has been maintained past the point where maintenance stops being thrift and starts being a statement.

The road into the village is excellent right up to the boundary stone and ordinary past it, which is the first thing a traveller notices and the last thing anyone will explain.

---

## The Custom — Even Weight

**Nothing leaves Quillet without something coming back, and it is written down.**

Not a spiritual practice. Not a religion. **Bookkeeping**, kept as a moral discipline and taught to children alongside letters. Every transaction the village makes is entered in a double column: what went out, what came in, and the date each side was settled. A trade that balances on the day closes on the day. A trade that doesn't stays open, in ink, until it does.

**A debt is not an insult here and nobody is ashamed of one.** The columns are not about trust or honour. They are about the plain fact that a field which gives and is never given to eventually stops giving, and that this is as true of a village as it is of soil. Even Weight is soil management applied to people, by people who have watched what happens to ground that gets taken from forever.

**The Ledger** lives in the long house, which is why the long house has the windows. It is nine volumes now. Anyone may read it. Anyone may check their own entry. The current keeper is whoever the last keeper trained, and the training takes years and is mostly about handwriting.

---

## The Refusal

**They did not refuse out of anger and they have never claimed a grievance.**

The Regency's levy is assessed, recorded, and collected — food out of Quillet and into the capital, every season, lawfully. Quillet entered each collection in the Ledger the way it enters everything: what went out, in weight, dated, signed by the collecting officer without his ever understanding what he was signing.

**The other column stayed empty for sixty years.**

Roads were the Regency's own obligation. A garrison that never came was not a return. There was nothing to enter, so nothing was entered, and the open balance grew across three volumes.

**Eleven years ago the keeper ran out of page.** A single running entry had reached the end of a physical book and could not be carried forward without starting a fourth volume on an unclosed account — which, by the discipline as taught, is the point at which you stop trading with that party until they settle.

So they stopped. They told the collecting officer exactly that, in those words, and offered him the books to look at.

**Nobody in Quillet thinks of this as a rebellion. They think of it as closing a bad account**, and are quietly baffled that anyone reads it as anything else.

---

## The Regency's Answer

**No soldiers came. None are coming.**

The response was administrative and took about a month: **Quillet was struck from the register.** Not outlawed, not condemned, not punished in any way a court would recognise. Simply removed from the list of places the Regency administers — the same instrument that made the priced out (`places/capital/capital.md`, Where the Food Comes From), applied to a whole village at once.

What that actually means, in order of how long it took to hurt:

- **The road stops.** Regency road crews maintain to the boundary stone and no further. The excellent road into Quillet was the last thing they finished before the striking took effect, and they finished it, because it was already assessed.
- **The Kings Road Inn is not an option.** A waypoint is Regency infrastructure and Quillet's people are not registered travellers. They sleep outside like anyone else.
- **The Cartographers Guild stopped surveying the spur.** Route walks cover registered routes (`factions-and-races/the-cartographers-guild.md`), so the road to Quillet ages out of every atlas printed after that year. On paper the village is drifting toward not being anywhere.
- **Nobody may lawfully buy from them.** An unregistered seller cannot be entered in a Marketplace Warden's book, so a capital merchant who trades with Quillet has broken a commercial rule rather than a criminal one — which is worse for him and no help at all to them.
- **The Adventurers' Hall cannot post a job there.** Whatever Quillet needs, the Hall has no mechanism to hear about it (`places/capital/adventurers-hall.md`).

**And the debt closed itself.** A body that is not registered cannot be owed by the Regency, so the balance that was the entire point of the refusal was not settled, not disputed, and not denied. It simply stopped existing everywhere except in nine volumes in a long house.

**Nobody in the capital did anything cruel.** Every step was correct. That is the horror of it and it should be run that way — comprehension, not menace (`world/tonal-bible.md`).

---

## Eleven Years On

**They are still farming and they are not starving.** The strips are good, the barns are sound, and there are two hundred people where there used to be three hundred.

**Their surplus moves through Vulture's Nest**, because the Nest is the one market that has never cared whether a seller is on a list (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Tideward Compact). The Compact takes its percentage and asks nothing. Quillet is entered in the Ledger as a balanced account and in the Compact's books as a reliable supplier of unregistered grain, and both of those are true.

**The village is not of one mind about that.** Roughly:

- **The ones who think they won.** They are trading freely, keeping their whole yield, and paying a percentage instead of a levy. By the numbers this is correct.
- **The ones counting the hundred who left.** Mostly the young, mostly to the capital, mostly having discovered that being from an unregistered village makes work hard to get and registration hard to obtain. Several of them are in the Underground Bazaar now, and that is not a coincidence (`places/capital/underground-bazaar.md`).
- **The ones who want to reopen the account.** A minority, growing slowly, who have worked out that the discipline cuts both ways: the Regency cannot settle a debt to a party it does not recognise, so the price of ever being paid is asking to be assessed again. The keeper considers this sound bookkeeping and unforgivable.

---

## Running It

**Nobody here is a rebel and nobody makes a speech.** These are careful people doing arithmetic. A party expecting an uprising will be bored, and a party that sits down with the Ledger will not be.

**They will show anyone the books.** Immediately, without being asked twice, with the specific eagerness of people who have never once been argued with on the merits. The Ledger is legible, meticulous, and correct, and reading the open column is the encounter.

**They do not want rescuing and will say so politely.** What they want is the account settled, which no party can deliver, which is the point.

**What a party can actually do here, none of it clean:**

- Carry the balance to somebody in the capital who could act on it. Elara's Wardens can't — an unregistered account isn't a document to them. Isabella's court hears what's brought and paid for (`world/the-regency.md`). Percival keeps records and says nothing. There is a route; it isn't short.
- Get the road surveyed again, which requires the Guild to have a reason and a registered route to walk.
- Settle a piece of the balance themselves, which the keeper will enter, by name, in ink. That entry is permanent and it is a relationship.
- Find the hundred who left. Several are findable.

**Do not have soldiers arrive.** The Regency has already won and knows it. Any GM who reaches for a raid has swapped a hard problem for an easy one.

---

## Open

- The keeper. Unwritten — name, age, temperament, and whether they were the one who ran out of page or inherited the consequence.
- The collecting officer who signed the last entry. He is named in the Ledger. He may still be alive and he may not know what his signature is doing in a book in a long house.
- Whether any other village has done this, or whether Quillet is the only one — and whether the Regency's silence is confidence or embarrassment.
- What the Compact thinks it has. A village entirely dependent on one market is leverage, and Harlow at minimum will have noticed.
