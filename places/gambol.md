# Gambol

*An island in the web within reach of Vulture's Nest. Nobody here is over about fourteen, except the ones who are, and everybody agrees those are children too.*

---

## The Belief

**Every wrong thing a person does comes out of being afraid to die.**

That is the whole doctrine and they will state it in one breath. Cruelty is fear. Hoarding is fear. Lying, cheating, striking somebody, refusing to share, holding a grudge across years — all of it is a person trying to make themselves safe, and nobody tries to make themselves safe until they have understood that they are going to die.

**Children have not understood it yet.** That is not innocence in the sentimental sense; it is a specific, temporary, precious condition, and it is the only condition in which a person is actually good. So: **a life should end while it is still that.**

**Growing old is the corruption.** Not the years — the fear the years bring. Gambol is not trying to die young. It is trying to never become the thing that fear makes of people.

---

## Adulthood Is a Diagnosis, Not an Age

**This is the mechanism that makes the island work, and it should be laid out plainly for a GM because everything else hangs on it.**

**You are an adult on the day the others decide you have become afraid.** Not at a birthday. Not at a height. There is no ceremony of years. **Somebody notices, and then other people notice, and then it is simply true.**

**The word is "careful."** *He's gone careful. She's been careful a while now, hasn't she.* To be careful on Gambol is the worst thing you can be, and an adult virtue everywhere else in the world is here the name of a terminal condition.

**They say they can smell it**, and they say it literally — a thing on the breath, a sourness. **Whether they actually can is not the point and probably not true.** What is true is that these are children who have spent their whole lives watching each other for exactly one sign, and children are already the most acute readers of each other alive. They are almost never wrong. When they are, nobody finds out.

---

## Why It Is Almost Always the Parents

**Having a child is what makes people afraid.** That is the island's observation and it is not a bad one.

You can be reckless with your own life easily. The night you have something you cannot bear to lose, you start calculating — and calculating is carefulness, and carefulness is the end. **Nearly everyone who has a baby on Gambol is careful within a year or two of it**, and everybody expects that, including them.

**So the babies are raised by the children**, who are the only people left. A nine-year-old with an infant on her hip is the most ordinary sight on the island, and she is competent at it, because she has been doing it since she was six and so had everyone who taught her.

*This is why the island reads as having no adults. It is not a rule against adults. It is that the reliable route into fear is parenthood, and fear is what ends you.*

---

## The Seeing-Off

**It is a good day.** The best day. There is a feast of whatever the island has, and the person leaving is the centre of it, and they have been looking forward to it — genuinely, not bravely — for weeks.

**They agree.** That is the part a visitor cannot get past. Somebody who has gone careful believes the doctrine as completely as everyone else does, can feel the thing in themselves, and is frightened of what they are becoming in a way that is much worse than being frightened of the end. **They are not dragged anywhere.**

**The older children see them off**, and it is done quickly and by people who love them, and that is as much as this file is going to say about how. A GM does not need more and a table certainly does not.

*The horror here is not cruelty. There is none. The horror is complete, warm, mutual consent inside a premise that is false.*

---

## The Ones Who Bury It

**Doubt is fear wearing a coat**, and everybody on the island knows it.

So a child who has started to wonder whether any of this is right has to put it away, completely, where nobody can see — because the doubt itself is the symptom, and being found out doubting *is* being found out afraid. **The only way to survive having a question is to stop having it.**

**That trap is the cruellest thing on the island and nobody built it.** It is just what the belief does when you run it on a real person. A child worrying about whether they are becoming careful has, by worrying, become careful.

**Most bury it successfully.** They are children, and children are very good at deciding not to think about something. A few bury it badly, and are seen, and there is a feast.

---

## The Old Children

**Some people simply never become afraid.**

They are rare and they are not hiding anything. The thing the island watches for never arrives in them, decade after decade, and so it never becomes true of them, and **so they are children.** Everyone agrees. It is not a polite fiction or an exemption granted out of respect — the islanders have a definition of adulthood and these people do not meet it, and that is the end of the question.

**Mostly they are people who never had a child.** Very occasionally it is somebody who did and was, genuinely, still not afraid afterwards, and those ones are quietly regarded as the luckiest people who have ever lived.

**And they are the entire memory of the island.** They are the only ones who have lived long enough to get good at anything — the boat-builder, the one who knows the channel, the one who can set a bone, the one who remembers what to do in the year the fish do not come. **Nobody on Gambol thinks of them as elders**, because they are not elders, they are children who happen to be sixty and know how to do a lot of things.

*An eighty-year-old sitting in the dirt playing a counting game with five-year-olds, being beaten at it, and taking it seriously. That is the image. It should land as wonderful before it lands as anything else.*

---

## What the Party Walks Into

**They are welcomed, and they are pitied.**

Every adult who steps off a boat onto that beach is, by the island's own definition, a walking example of the thing it exists to prevent. The party smells of it. Not metaphorically — the islanders will say so, plainly, the way children say things.

**Nobody is hostile.** Nobody threatens them and nobody is going to try to see anybody off. The party are guests and outsiders, and outsiders are not islanders and the rules do not run that way.

**They are treated gently and a little sadly**, which is far worse. A twelve-year-old will be kind to a grown adventurer in the specific way you are kind to someone who has already lost something, and will not think they are saying anything cutting.

> *"It's alright. You didn't know."*

**And they will ask the party questions**, cheerfully and without malice, that are extremely hard to answer. What are you afraid of. What do you keep. Who do you have that you would not want to lose. **Every one of those is a diagnostic and the party will not realise it at the time.**

---

## Is There a Problem To Solve

**Honestly: yes, and I am flagging that as a departure rather than smuggling it past.**

Windfall was built so that nothing is wrong and the only problem is the one the party manufactures (`places/windfall.md`). **Gambol is not that.** People are dying on a schedule, the belief that kills them is false, and the consent — which is entirely real — was manufactured by the same belief. A party that decides to intervene is not inventing a grievance.

**But the intervention has nowhere to land.** There is no tyrant, no council, no priest, no one enforcing anything. **The belief is held by everybody equally and the person most committed to it is whoever is about to die of it.** Argue with a child and you are arguing with a child. Win, and you have given a nine-year-old a doubt, which on this island is a diagnosis.

**So: the party can absolutely do something, and the shape of doing it is horrible.** To save anyone they have to infect the island with the exact thing it kills for, and the first person to catch it will be someone who trusted them.

*That is the design. Not a fortress to storm — a belief where every tool the party has is also the weapon.*

**What I would not do** is give it a villain, a secret cabal of adults running it from somewhere, or a revelation that it is a scam. Every one of those is available and every one is smaller than what is there.

## Not Yet Set

- **Whether the party can actually save anyone**, and what happens to a Gambol child raised elsewhere. Wide open and probably the best long-term thread here.
- **What the babies are, exactly** — whether the island takes in foundlings, whether outsiders' children ever end up there, and what the Nest thinks it knows about that.
- **Whether anyone has ever left on purpose.** An adult who left before they were seen off would be the only person alive who knows what Gambol looks like from outside.
- **Their race or races.** Unset, same as Windfall was. Mixed is the likelier answer and nothing depends on it.
- **How the island feeds itself**, given that nobody keeps a skill for more than a few years except the old children. It should be visibly precarious.
- **Where exactly it sits.** Within reach of Vulture's Nest; no bearing fixed (`world/geography-overview.md`).
