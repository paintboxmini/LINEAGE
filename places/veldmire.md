# Veldmire

*A quiet village. People here speak less than you'd expect. Conversations are short, precise. Gossip dies fast. The silence isn't unfriendly — it's careful.*

---

## The Stones

Every adult in Veldmire carries or keeps at home a personal black stone — palm-sized or larger, polished to a mirror finish on one face. They spend hours each day chiseling, grinding, and buffing with finer and finer abrasives until the reflection is flawless.

The act is meditative. The clearer the mirror, the stronger your inner conviction is said to be. A dull or scratched stone signals doubt or weakness. A perfect one signals clarity of will.

People rise early to work their stones. It is not vanity. It is preparation.

A farmer spends longer on his stone during planting season, knowing water rights could come up. A widow polishes obsessively after her husband's death, terrified her grief will dull her reflection when inheritance is contested. Children start with small, rough stones and are taught to polish without being told why — the reason comes at adolescence, when their first dispute is witnessed.

---

## The Ritual

When a dispute arises — land boundaries, theft, inheritance, moral questions — the community gathers in the central plaza at dusk. No speeches. No witnesses. No evidence presented in words.

1. Anyone with an opinion steps forward and holds up their stone, angled so the fading light catches it.
2. They stare into their own reflection and speak their version of events aloud — calmly, factually, without exaggeration. The words are secondary. The real argument is the steadiness of the gaze and the unwavering hold of the hand.
3. The stones reflect the speaker's face back, but also catch glints from the setting sun and from every other stone in the circle.
4. The crowd watches. They do not vote. The interpretation that produces the strongest, most sustained glint — the one that holds the light longest without wavering — becomes the official truth. The loser's version is quietly discarded. Sometimes their stone is retired: buried, or given to children to re-polish.

It is not literally willpower bending light. It is social consensus masquerading as physics. The person with the most unshakeable self-belief wins. Charisma, mental discipline, sheer stubbornness, or sociopathic certainty can all tip the scales. Doubt flickers. Conviction shines.

---

## Social Texture

**Silence as strategy.** Why risk muddying your inner clarity? A rumor you repeat, you may have to defend. People say less. They mean more of it.

**Hierarchy of shine.** Certain families become known as bright ones. Their interpretations win more often, so people defer to them preemptively to avoid the ritual. It isn't formal power — but everyone knows whose stone will outshine theirs.

**Outsiders.** A traveler's interpretation is rarely accepted unless they can produce a mirror-quality stone on the spot. Most can't. Outsiders are heard but not reflected.

**The system's cruelty.** It favors the emotionally armored. Trauma survivors who have learned to suppress doubt can dominate. Empathetic people who second-guess themselves lose repeatedly and withdraw from communal life. Suicides are rare but hushed — someone whose stone no longer shines might simply stop polishing and fade.

---

## The Dark Mechanic

Might doesn't make right here. It polishes the mirror until right looks like whatever refuses to blink.

The visitor who witnesses the ritual for the first time will see something that looks like justice. Order. Dignity. It takes longer to see that the winner is not the most truthful — only the most certain. And certainty is not equally distributed. It pools in particular kinds of people.

*Kino-style scenario:* A dispute arises over whether to allow a sick outsider to stay and be healed. The compassionate argument flickers — the speaker has lost loved ones to illness before, and the doubt is real. The fearful argument holds rock-steady. The fearful interpretation wins. The sick outsider is turned away at dusk, stone reflections glinting coldly on the path out.

The visitor leaves wondering: is this justice by conviction, or simply the loudest inner voice winning because it never questions itself?

---

## Open Hooks

**The bright ones** — which families hold shine across generations, and what did they do to get it? Is it discipline, or is it something they stopped feeling?

**The retired stones** — buried stones are not destroyed. Someone knows where they are. Someone has been digging.

**An outsider who wins** — a traveler who produces a flawless stone and speaks at the ritual. The village has no framework for this. What happens when an outsider's conviction outshines the bright ones?

**What Veldmire decides next** — the village is currently debating something. The players arrive mid-deliberation. What the question is determines everything about who they meet and whose stone is trembling.
