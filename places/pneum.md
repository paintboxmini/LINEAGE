# Pneum

*Pronounced: new'm*

A lake island northeast of Vulture's Nest. Part of a loose People of Promise island group scattered across the lake — a subgroup that left the Glasslight congregations to practice their beliefs in peace, far from the coast but never out of reach of it: their ritual barges run the rivers down to the grey water and back.

---

## The People of Promise

The Promise: everything returns to the Unheld eventually. Held reality is temporary. The Unheld is the destination.

This subgroup's interpretation: disease is the Unheld asserting itself through the body. Deliberate infection is communion — an invitation for the Unheld to take root in held flesh. The rarer the disease, the deeper the contact.

They do not see themselves as suffering. They see themselves as the most devoted practitioners of an obvious truth.

---

## The Practice

Diseases are collected. Rarity determines prestige — and prestige is sacred, because the social witness of your collection is the act of communion made visible. Collecting without witnesses isn't collecting.

Symptoms display publicly. The body is the trophy case.

The diseases must be visible. This is not negotiable in their theology.

## Wild Growth — A Reflection, Not a Seat

Wild Growth was never its own Seat. Calling it one was bad design; corrected here. What the People of Promise's theology actually rhymes with, the same accidental way every local religion in Eclipseria rhymes with a Seat nobody knows exists (`world/seats.md`, Love/Binding — the Temple of Unity's own version of this), is **Change** — Elias's Seat (`world/seats.md`, `characters/elias/README.md`). They don't know that. They've built an entire theology around disease and proliferation as "the Unheld asserting itself in held reality," and what they're actually circling, however imperfectly, is a domain that reshapes what a thing is without asking its permission first. Elias's own entry says it plainly about himself: "he is not wrong about what he does. He is wrong about what he is." The People of Promise are wrong about what they're worshipping in exactly the same shape.

Disease read as sacred proliferation, "too much life, something about to burst" — that's their metaphor, not a mechanic. No separate Archon. No Unheld literally taking root in a living host. Whatever inspired that image is theirs to have imagined, not a GM-facing truth about how any of this actually works.

## Tone

These people are warm, communal, and completely sincere. The wrongness isn't cruelty or deception — it's a theology that arrived at a genuinely strange place by following its own logic all the way down.
