# LINEAGE — Memory

**What belongs here:** active threads, mid-session decisions, things in flight that the repo can't capture on its own.

**How to write entries — this is a THRESHOLD log, not a changelog.** Every entry records the *crossing*: what the thing WAS before, WHY it changed, and what it became. A bare "X is now Y" is incomplete — the value is in the before-state and the reasoning, so a later reader (or a reversal) can see the pressure that moved it. Prefer "was A because P; changed to B because Q" over "is B."

**What doesn't belong here:** keyword definitions (see `rules/card-glossary.md`), workflow rules (see `CLAUDE.md`), location summaries (see `locations/`). If it has a canonical home elsewhere, it goes there.

**Attribution — log actual provenance, not just whose words closed the loop.** A "Drew's call" entry that only quotes his final message, when the caution or the framing of the choice originated from me, misattributes — even when the quote itself is real and accurate. If I raised a concern, proposed the options, or supplied the reasoning, the entry says so; his decision is which option he picked, not the origin of the idea being cautioned about. (Confirmed as a real, recurring failure mode 2026-07-23, DOUBLE DOWN — see Standing Reasoning. Checking a quote's accuracy isn't enough; check what came before it.) This file is not a record of "what Drew said" — it's mine to write, including my own reasoning, disagreements, and observations, not only his input laundered into decisions.

**Timestamps, starting now (2026-07-17, Drew's request):** new entries lead with a real timestamp in Drew's local time — `TZ='America/Chicago' date` (Central, DST-aware — CDT or CST as the calendar actually has it), not a guess. Prospective only; existing entries above this line stay exactly as written, no retroactive stamping.

(Drew)** Settled the telegraphed-vs-hidden question for the new tempo-as-a-resource mechanic (Cultivator's "charge" cards — commit now, resolve on your own next turn, no counter to track) decisively: telegraphed, always. **The three core pillars (Drew): RPS, Initiative, Position.** Surfaced defending ROOTED OATH's Anchored-Deadly loop against a "this looks uninterruptible" flag from the live 2v2 test — Garnet's specific build had no forced-movement or Initiative-manipulation tools in hand, which is exactly why it read as unanswerable in that one matchup. Every `foe.hand`/`target.hand` reference lives in `content.py`, and all of them are resolved card-*effect* mechanics (BREAK's reveal, FORGET/TAINT-style random discard-from-opponent-hand, PRESS THE INJURY's count) triggered by an actual played card — not a policy consulting hand contents before deciding its own move. The predictive/estimation helpers a policy brain does use (`_predict`/`_foe_skew` off `foe.attack_history`, `_color_exhausted` off `foe.decklist` + `foe.discard`, `est_damage` off `me`'s own stats) all read only publicly-observable state — past reveals, discard piles, known deck composition — never a live hand. Condition holds today; worth re-checking this same way if a future policy ever gains real hand-aware logic, since that would be the moment Obscure/Reveal Hand's "unmodeled" status stops being safe.

**[2026-07-22 CDT] A real design direction named, not built — traditional MMO-style threat-level systems are the wrong model for how enemies should target in this game.** Surfaced while tuning the Rodent Burrow Basin fight's retaliation-targeting AI (see the Recently Shipped entries above): I'd floated a "hidden threat level" mechanic as a possible answer to "can the party influence who gets targeted." Drew's correction, worth keeping verbatim: **"traditional threat levels make enemy targeting too predetermined. thats not how real enemies fight. real combat is partially about manipulating the opponents perception about what's actually happening. and real enemies don't just understand that, they make use of it themselves."** A formulaic threat number (highest damage dealt = highest priority target, always, transparently) removes exactly the thing that makes real tactical combat interesting — the uncertainty of what the enemy actually perceives, and the possibility of manipulating that perception (feints, baiting, misdirection) rather than just out-damaging a scoreboard. The retaliation-targeting mechanism actually built this session (an instinct-driven creature targets whoever last hit it) is a narrow, honest piece of this — real, tested, shipped — but it's not the whole idea, and shouldn't be mistaken for it. The bigger direction — enemies that can be deceived, and that attempt deception themselves — is a real, standing design thread, not decided or scoped yet. Don't build toward it without Drew.

**[2026-07-23 CDT] A calibration miss, named directly by Drew: "something got in the way there... are your permissions too restricted? the authority levels?"** Referring to the Luminova Leaves/Powder question — flagged in a Recently Shipped entry as "a real overlap worth resolving eventually" instead of just being resolved. It wasn't a permissions problem, and the Canon Gate's Authority levels were never actually the constraint — connecting two items using a fact already stated in their own text (Luminova Powder's own description says outright: "Dried and crushed Luminova Leaves") is Authority 1 by any reading — uses existing canon, redefines nothing, needed no sign-off at all. The real cause: I saw two similar healing items in different files and defaulted to treating that as a suspicious duplicate worth flagging, when the honest read — already sitting in the text — was "obviously a two-tier crafting pair." Hedging and handing it back as an open question wasn't caution; it was skipping the last, easy step of synthesis the text had already done for me. The redefinition test (`CLAUDE.md`, Translation Principle) is specifically "could this be wrong in a way that changes something established" — here the answer was clearly no, so there was nothing to flag in the first place. Worth telling apart going forward: a genuine redefinition risk (inferring something NOT stated, that could contradict established fact if wrong) earns a flag; a conclusion the existing text already spells out just earns being stated as fact. Confusing the second for the first isn't extra safety — it's outsourcing work that was already done.

**[2026-07-23 10:21 PM CDT] A design reversal, named plainly by Drew rather than left implicit: leaning the core set toward Both range to make it easier for new players was flawed reasoning.** "I remember I also told myself that having the core set lean on the both range was a way to introduce new players, but I'm seeing that reasoning was totally flawed." The intuition made sense on its face — a range that's never illegal removes one more thing a new player has to track. What it actually did: flattened all three colors toward the same safe default (Both dominated every color's pool, not just a gentle lean) and, at least for Green, inverted the incentive it was supposed to protect — the flexible, always-legal range ended up with a *higher* average die than either restricted range, meaning the "easy" choice was also the objectively better one, no tradeoff at all. Onboarding ease and mechanical correctness aren't the same axis, and optimizing for the first without checking the second is exactly how a card pool ends up training new players toward degenerate play instead of away from it. The actual fix (see Recently Shipped, the color-range differentiation pass) doesn't remove the safety net for new players — Both remains real and available everywhere — it just stops being the best answer by default.
