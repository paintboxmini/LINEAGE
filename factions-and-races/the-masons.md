# The Masons

A known order with an unknown purpose. Nearly everyone in Eclipseria has heard of the Masons, and most people have stood next to their work without being told what they were looking at: a line of squared posts along a field edge, a glyph cut into a bridge footing, an arch still standing where the rest of a structure isn't. The order's existence has never been the secret. What they are doing at any given place is, and since nobody in the order has ever explained it, everybody outside the order has.

**Their real work is binding.** They find seams — places where held reality presses against something it shouldn't touch — and cut glyphs that hold the seam still. Then they come back on a rotation, indefinitely, because a glyph fades and a binding nobody recuts is a binding that ends. They don't announce the work, they don't warn the people living on top of it, and they don't stay. The places they protect are rarely told they're being protected.

They are also masons in the ordinary sense, and among the finest alive at it. The glyphwork is what the order is *for*; the stonework is what makes the glyphwork possible. A ward cut into a badly set post fails with the post. Nothing they raise is temporary by intent — the fence posts at Briarwatch, the pillars under it, the lodges — and a Mason who can't cut a true joint isn't trusted with a glyph.

## Methods

- Glyphs — carved into stone. To ward, stabilize, and protect. Cut with chisels, not drawn — chisel width and depth aren't a style choice, they're the whole tradeoff. A large chisel cuts shallow but wide, and the glyph it leaves hits hard and fades fast. A thin, sharp chisel cuts deep and narrow, and what it leaves lasts far longer at a fraction of the strength. Chalk and string mark the cut before the chisel ever touches stone.

- Constrained magic — Mason practice is disciplined, systematic, and deliberately limited in scope.

- **Seams have types, and the type sets both what a seam does and what holds it** *(Drew, 2026-10-01)*. A seam presses on one domain, so its effects are that domain's and nobody else's — **there is no such thing as what a seam does in general.** A Mason contains it with the chisel aligned to the matching Seat (`world/seats.md`; Sebastian's seven are `characters/sebastian.md`, The Chisels), and then picks the width on the usual tradeoff between strength and how long it lasts. *Forget-Me-Not Island is a Memory seam, which is why names and faces slip there and why Sebastian brought the Memory chisel* (`places/forget-me-not-island.md`). **A Change seam, a War seam or a Hunt seam would do something else entirely.**

- **Binding** is the order's own word for what a finished piece of work is, and it's a precise one. A binding doesn't remove a threat or close a seam; it holds one in a fixed state so it stops getting worse. Everything in Known Works below is a binding, and every one of them is still holding something that was never solved.

- Maintenance, not installation. A glyph fades. The work is a rotation kept without interruption for as long as the order has existed — walk the line, recut what's thinned, walk back out. Closer to a rite kept on schedule than a chore fit in when there's time.

- The whole method assumes a threshold: something that holds still long enough to be measured, cut against, and checked on later. Everything the order knows how to do rests on that assumption. Where it doesn't hold, they have nothing (see The Coil, below).

## What They Believe

**The Three Cuts are the order's creed** (`factions-and-races/the-masons-three-cuts.md`). Before anything Held there was the Unheld, and an edge called the Knife wounded reality into holding three times — Name, which made a thing legible; Price, which made it costly to keep; Distance, which kept it from swallowing everything else. Where the Cuts hold, reality persists. Where one thins, the Unheld presses close.

**The creed and the trade are the same object.** A Mason finds a seam where something presses on held reality — Distance giving. Cuts a glyph to force a legible shape onto it — Name. Walks the rotation forever, because a glyph fades and nothing stays held without being paid for continuously — Price. Ask a Mason whether the doctrine explains the work or the work explains the doctrine and you will not get a useful answer, because inside the order the question has never come up.

**This is not the world's official cosmology, and the world does not have one.** The Cuts are what one small order believes, held with total conviction, taught to every apprentice, and never once demonstrated to anybody outside. Other peoples have other accounts of the same events — the Lizardkin Tithe rhymes with Price without deriving from it (`factions-and-races/races-lizardkin.md`), Apnea calls the thinness *Out There* and leaves it there (`places/apnea.md`), and the People of Promise worship their way toward something the Masons would describe entirely differently. **Nobody is in a position to check.** The Masons don't argue about it. They don't proselytise, they don't correct anyone, and they have never made the case in public — which is one more reason the superstitions below have had a free run.

**What the doctrine does not do is give them certainty about anything specific.** It tells them what a seam is. It does not tell them what is on the other side of one, and the order is entirely comfortable never finding out.

## How They're Seen

**Common knowledge, uncommon specifics.** Everyone has heard of the Masons. Everyone knows they cut glyphs into stone, that they turn up unannounced, and that they don't explain themselves. Almost nobody knows what a given glyph is for, how the order picks where to work, who pays for it, whether anyone pays for it, or how many Masons there are. That gap is wide, old, and has never once been filled by somebody in a position to fill it — which is where the superstitions grow, and why they've had centuries to get elaborate.

**They answer to nobody.** The order is independent and always has been. It holds no charter, sits under no Seat's authority, takes no commission, and does not ask permission before working. Nobody grants them the right to cut a glyph into a village's stone; they cut it. This is not defiance — the order has no quarrel with the Regency or anyone else, and largely ignores the question of who governs where it works. It is simply that the criterion for where a Mason goes has nothing to do with borders, and never has.

**Reactions run the full spread, and one village usually holds several at once.**

- **Dread.** They're a portent. When a Mason turns up, something follows. Strongest where there's a local story about the last time one came, and those stories are common because a Mason coming is itself uncommon enough to be remembered.
- **Wariness.** The most widely held reading and the most practical one. Don't obstruct them, don't invite them in either. Feed them if they ask, don't ask them why they're here, and be relieved when they've moved on. Most people who deal with a Mason directly land here.
- **Cautious optimism.** A real minority view, held by people who've noticed that the marked places tend to still be standing. Someone competent is paying attention to us, and whatever's wrong, it's being worked on. Held quietly, since saying it out loud invites an argument with whoever holds the first view.
- **Indifference.** Plenty of people have lived a whole life beside a marked post and never once wondered about it. A glyph on a bridge footing is just what a bridge footing has.

The superstitions themselves contradict each other freely and all of them have believers: that the glyphs are a claim-mark, and the ground is being registered to somebody. That the glyphs cause what follows them, and a village that lets one be cut has agreed to what comes next. That the order deals with something and the posts are the terms. That they're undertakers for ground, and a marked field is written off. That the marks are being counted somewhere, and nobody wants to know what happens at the number they're counting to.

**The correlation is real. The causation runs the other way.** The Masons go where catastrophe is close — that's the entire selection criterion for a seam, the only reason a site ever gets a Mason's attention, and the one thing about the order that outsiders have never guessed. So what people actually observe is: a Mason arrives somewhere nobody sent for one, cuts something into stone without being asked, won't say what it's for, and leaves. Within a season something terrible happens nearby, or comes near enough that everyone talks about it. Being right about the danger is precisely what makes them look like the source of it.

Explaining doesn't fix it, and the order gave up trying long before anyone currently alive joined. A Mason who tells a village the truth — *your ground is failing, that's why we're here, and we aren't certain we can hold it* — hands the first group their confirmation, hands the second a reason to obstruct, and hands the third a promise the order can't guarantee. Silence is the only version that treats all four the same and leaves the rotation walkable. That, and not the order's existence, is what the walk-in-work-leave discipline is actually protecting.

**The Regency is the exception.** Whatever the Council does or doesn't share, it holds a far better picture of the order's actual workings than any village does — what the glyphs are for, roughly how the order selects a site, and that the Masons are not to be interfered with. This is characteristic rather than special: the Regency generally knows more than it says about most things. It has never made the order any kind of arm of the state, and couldn't; the Masons don't take commissions. Both parties appear to find the arrangement acceptable (`world/the-regency.md`, The Masons, What the Council Knows).

## Known Works

- **The Vulture's Nest docks** — **not a seam at all** *(Drew, 2026-10-01)*. **The Nest asked**, long ago, for its docks to be made to last as long as possible, and the order cut **Time glyphs** into the dock stone — continuity and persistence, put to work holding a structure rather than a threshold. *It is the one known case of Mason work that was requested, and of glyphs that hold something up rather than something back.* They surface only when the water settles (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Tide). **Why the order said yes, when, and whether anyone still recuts them are not written** — and nothing says anything was paid; the order takes no commissions.
- **The Briarwatch line** — **a Trust and Change seam** *(Drew, 2026-10-01)*, which is the Seat the Weald itself holds (`world/seats.md`, Trust and Change). *It is also why the line is always losing:* Change is the widest, shallowest chisel, the one that hits hardest and fades fastest (`characters/sebastian.md`, The Chisels), so every recut is strong and none of them lasts. One threshold among the many they tend, held to with the same unbroken regularity as all the others. The watchtower ruins and the giant fence at Briarwatch's western edge are one project, not two: a boundary raised against Turnroot Weald's encroachment, carving out the ground Briarwatch now farms. The larder fence is one stretch of that giant fence, not a separate structure. The Masons still visit periodically for maintenance and repair. What they didn't plan for: a section of their plain stone perimeter has been slowly overgrown with thorn-brush the local tollbirds dragged in from the Weald itself, generation over generation, until it became a fence within their fence. Briarwatch has tended the outer larder-fence stretch for generations too, believing it's simply old, without knowing why any of it started. See `places/briarwatch.md`, The Larder Fence, and `places/turnroot-weald.md`. **The same line extends below ground** — the Western Property sits directly against this same threshold, and the Masons' work there was never limited to the surface: a reinforced chamber and at least one support pillar underground, holding back the same pressure the watchtower line holds back above. One pillar has since failed — the actual cause behind the Western Property sinkhole, not just water and limestone alone. The Masons don't know yet. **The physical encroachment was never the whole reason the line went up.** The Masons understood, when they raised it, that a sentient forest's growth left fully unchecked behaves the way any unchecked growth does: parasitically, consuming balance rather than existing inside it. The watchtower line doesn't just hold the Weald's edge back — it holds the forest's entire natural expansion back **on purpose**, and blocked growth has to go somewhere. Redirecting it inward, toward the Root Heart, was the design: a slow, cyclical release valve the order judged a containable cost against letting the whole forest spread unchecked. The Root Heart's dormant-to-Overgrowth cycle, currently peaking aggressive because outward was never an option while the line held, is not a side effect they stumbled into. It's the fence working as intended — Rootstalkers hunting, forest-people dying, and all. See `bestiary/root-heart.md`, The Overgrowth Cycle, for the phases the valve is regulating, and `characters/aege.md`, Story Hook, for what it costs on the ground.

  **The line is losing, slowly, and it always has been.** Every aggressive phase spends itself against the fence, and every time it does the stone gives up fractionally more than the next recut puts back. No single cycle does visible harm. The deficit is cumulative, it runs in one direction only, and the order has been carrying it since the line went up. If the glyphs are ever left unre-established through a full cycle — one missed rotation at the wrong moment, one recut that doesn't happen because there was nobody left to walk it — the line fails, and the Weald takes the expansion branch for the first time in its recorded history. **This is the single largest thing the order is currently losing at, and the number of hands available to walk that rotation is the number in The Order Now, below.**

  **It does fail.** One cycle arrives with the recut incomplete and the push lands. A Mason gets to the Briarwatch stretch in time to reinforce it properly and runs out of time for the rest of the fence — so Briarwatch survives as a held span in a line that broke on either side of it, and the Weald advances north. The order does not get to choose between saving the line and saving the village; it gets to choose which section it can reach. See `quests/turnroot-weald-adventure.md`, The Return — After the Line Breaks, for the state that leaves behind, and which Mason it was.

  **The failed pillar and the sinkhole above it are the one consequence in this whole picture the Masons genuinely didn't plan for and still don't know about** — everything else here (the redirected growth, the Root Heart's cycle) was the design working as intended; that failure wasn't. **The stone for both projects — the underground pillar and the giant fence posts topside — came from the same source**: a small quarry off the Living Chamber itself, cut once and never revisited (`quests/hollow-below-briarwatch.md`, Transition Zone, the Quarry Offshoot). See `quests/hollow-below-briarwatch.md`, Transition Zone, and Surface Layer — Western Property Sinkhole.

- **The Soft Edge glyphs** — held with the **Hope** chisel, Sebastian's seventh — wide like Change, so every recut hits hard and none of it lasts, and the Soft Edge is recut often (`characters/sebastian.md`, The Chisels). **A Hope and Possibilities seam** *(Drew, 2026-10-01)*, which is the light-dance exactly: something trying to form out past where nothing ever finishes forming. Cut into the quartz cliffs at Glasslight Reach, where the northernmost water stops behaving and the light-dance runs along the line (`places/glasslight-reach.md`, The Soft Edge). This one is the order's only purely preventative work. Nothing has come across there. Nothing is pressing on it. But the light-dance reads, to anyone trained to read a seam, like something trying to form — and the order does not wait to find out what finishes forming out past where nothing ever finishes. The glyphs go in against a threshold that hasn't opened, and get recut on the same rotation as every line that has. Cover for this work is the Lodge in the cliffs, and cover is the reason nobody in Glasslight Reach knows the cliffs are warded. The stabilization glyphs are close enough to the surface that a determined person can find them by following the marks — Mirel did exactly that (`characters/mirel.md`). A hostile storm on those cliffs would find them too (`quests/tide-pulls-back.md`).

- **The lodges** — the order's own houses, and the best stonework they do, because a lodge is the one building they raise for themselves and the only one with nobody to hide it from. Hidden by placement rather than by disguise: set into seams and cliff faces, reached by ground that takes training to even read as a way in. The Lodge at the Soft Edge is the one currently kept (`characters/sebastian.md`). **The rest are empty, as far as anyone knows** (The Order Now, below) — how many there are and where is not established, and an empty Mason lodge is the best-built ruin a party will ever walk into.

- **The glyph posts in the In-Between** — **Navigation and Pathfinding seams** *(Drew, 2026-10-01; the Seat's title is still a working one, `world/seats.md`)*, held with the Hunt chisel, which covers pathfinding (`characters/sebastian.md`, The Chisels). The order's least visible and most widely used work, and the only work of theirs an ordinary traveller passes without knowing it. Where a pocket of In-Between sits across ground people actually have to cross (`world/eclipseria-overview.md`), the Masons cut posts to slow the instability inside it: not a seal and not a fix, just enough held structure to keep a pocket from drifting freely and to make crossing it survivable. Same rotation as everything else — walk the line, recut what's thinned, leave. **And they are slowly failing** *(2026-10-01)*: one Mason cannot walk every post on the continent on rotation (The Order Now, below), so pockets drift more each season than they used to, and routes that took four days are starting to take five. The Cartographers Guild surveys the pockets and charts routes between them (`factions-and-races/the-cartographers-guild.md`); the Masons are the reason the ones that can't be routed around are passable at all. Neither order is in the habit of telling the other, and travellers credit the Guild for both.

- **The Coil** — **read as a Vitality and Destruction seam** *(Drew, 2026-10-01)*, the Storm Seat: one force whose effect depends on what it passes through (`world/seats.md`). *Read as one, and never actually one* — see below. Not one of the ones they tend; a failure, not a success. Early enough in the order's history that it survives only in the record, not in anyone's memory: they found something ancient in the deep lake east of Vulture's Nest and read it as a geological threshold like any other seam. It wasn't. It was a carrier — a living thing marked by direct exposure out in the Unheld itself, the same way the old ghost-story sailors came home wrong, except it never stopped being what it came home as. Full history at `places/the-coil.md`, What Happened To It. They tried to stabilize it with their usual methods anyway — the entrance arch survives; whatever else they attempted mostly didn't. It wasn't a boundary that would hold still to be measured. It was alive, restless, and it answered them instead. The order's own record calls it closed — not worth the cost of trying again, filed the way a failure gets filed. Officially, there are no more maintenance visits. Unofficially: Sebastian still goes back. Not sanctioned by anyone left to sanction it, not stopped either — inherited as an old obligation from a line that's had nobody else to check it against for longer than anyone currently walking can say. At least one of the original attempt never left at all. See `places/the-coil.md`, GM Secret, and `bestiary/wall-reader.md`.

## The Order Now

Small enough that the word *order* flatters it. The rotation is still walked, the lines are still recut, and there are not many hands left doing it.

**Sebastian is the only one left** *(Drew, 2026-10-01)*. A Head Mason, last of his line, keeper of the Lodge at the Soft Edge (`characters/sebastian.md`). He tends the Soft Edge, the Briarwatch line, the posts in the In-Between, the new Memory seam on Forget-Me-Not Island, and — off the books — the Coil. **Alone.**

**Mirel is the first new one in a long time**, and she wasn't recruited so much as found already doing it: dreaming a glyph, following the marks to the seams, scratching the shape into rock from memory. Sebastian gives her a chisel and no lessons (`characters/mirel.md`) — **his Time chisel, just before the party reaches Glasslight** *(Drew, 2026-10-01)*. Until then he carries all seven.

**The others are dead or gone.** There were more once, and the lodges they kept are still out there, set into cliff faces and seams — **empty, and worth finding** (Known Works, The lodges). *The Wall-Reader in the Coil was one of them* (`bestiary/wall-reader.md`): that is one way the order loses people.

**So when the Masons become more common as the campaign goes on, that is the party's doing** (How the party meets them, below). **Every new Mason is Mirel or somebody the party brought in.** *The order growing is a story the table tells, not something that happens offscreen.*

**And one man cannot hold all of it.** The Briarwatch line and the Soft Edge are both wide-chisel work that needs recutting often, and the posts in the In-Between run across the whole continent. **Something is always being missed** — which is why the Briarwatch line can only ever be saved one section at a time, and why the roads are slowly getting worse (Known Works, The glyph posts in the In-Between).

## How the party meets them

*Drew, 2026-09-30.* **Few, and rare early. More common as the campaign goes on.** The order stays as small as The Order Now says; what changes is how close the party gets to its work.

**Hermetic, close to druidic, and selfless.** A Mason lives apart, works alone, and walks the rotation out of conviction — trying to make the world safer for people who will never know it was done. *Nobody sends them, nobody pays them, and nobody hands their work out as a job.*

1. **First, a question with no answer.** The glyphs at Vulture's Nest that show only when the water settles (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Tide). Nobody explains them and nobody should.
2. **Second, one with teeth — after Pneum, not during it.** **Forget-Me-Not Island** (`places/forget-me-not-island.md`), set 2026-10-01: a new site Sebastian found recently, fresh glyphs on half a doorframe, and Sebastian arriving to finish them and warn the party off.
3. **Then, company.** A small order walking the same rotation forever is short of hands, and **a Mason the party has actually helped may let them walk the line alongside.** Not a job, not posted anywhere and not paid — it happens between people, on the Mason's terms, and a Mason who does not want company simply is not there in the morning.
4. **And a player can become one.** Mirel was found already doing the work rather than recruited (`characters/mirel.md`), which is the precedent: somebody who has walked the rotation enough times to start seeing seams is somebody Sebastian gives a chisel.

## Relationship to Other Factions

The order is independent of all of them, and of the Regency. It accepts no commissions and asks no permissions; where it works is decided by where the seams are, not by whose territory they fall in.

The Masons and the Final Current represent opposing orientations toward the same phenomenon. The Masons see the Unheld as a threat to be fought off. The Final Current see it as potential to be embraced. They are not openly at war. They are not allies.
