# The Spoken For

*Contracted cursegivers. The order that laid the Shunka royal curse, and the thread `experimental/pat.md` is pulled out into the world by.*

*(Working name. Alternatives worth weighing: **The Last Word**, **The Given**, **The Tally**. "The Spoken For" is doing double duty — the children are already spoken for, and so is every life a Price gets declared onto without asking. Swap it freely; nothing depends on the word.)*

## What They Are

**An order that raises cursegivers from childhood and spends them.** A curse laid the way theirs are laid costs the caster everything — not their life in the ordinary sense but their existence, the Price that was anchoring them coming off and landing on the target instead (`world/creation-myth-the-three-cuts.md`, The Second Cut). It is a weapon that can only be fired once per person, and only by somebody willing.

So the order's actual product is not curses. It's willingness, manufactured over fifteen or twenty years, one child at a time.

**They take orphans.** Children nobody is coming back for, raised inside a thing that tells them what they are for. What the order gives them is not cruelty — it's meaning, certainty, and a place to belong, which is precisely why it holds. A child raised to believe they are the only clean answer to an unpunishable wrong does not have to be coerced into the ending.

**They believe they are doing good, and they are not lying.** The doctrine is that a curse is the last honest justice available — that some people are past any court, past any army, past anything but this, and that a life spent unmaking one of them is a life spent well. A cursegiver walks to their own erasure convinced the world will be better on the far side of it.

**The people who run the order believe something else.** They take contracts. They choose targets by what the contract is worth and dress the choice in the doctrine afterward. Every cursegiver they raise is told the target deserved it; the order's leadership knows who was actually paying, and knows that at least some of the answer is *nobody who deserved anything*.

That gap is the whole faction. Not monsters running a lie — a genuine faith with a procurement department, and the faithful are the ones being spent.

## What They Did to the Shunka

The Tithebound invaded the Shunka and were routed cleanly in the field (`factions-and-races/races-lizardkin.md`, `experimental/the-shunka.md`). With no army left to press it, they hired what they couldn't do themselves.

The cursegiver sent to them laid an unspoken Price directly onto the Shunka queen: every child born of her line would come into the world on four legs, as an ordinary dog. The instant it took, she stopped being real — not killed, unmade, as if she had never been Cut at all. There was no body to find.

**She would have been in her twenties, and she would have believed the queen deserved it.**

## Why This Is Live Now

The curse was never dormant. It was crossing a spiritual distance, and it is only now beginning to reach the Shunka common folk — a thing aimed at one royal line arriving late and arriving wide. The three cursed royals have woken and sent Pat out to find the people who made it, because the people who commissioned it can't undo it and never could (`experimental/pat.md`, `experimental/session-1-convergence.md`).

**Whether the order can undo anything is an open question and probably shouldn't be answered early.** A Price paid in full doesn't undo on request; that's stated cosmology. What the order plausibly has is not a cure but an explanation — how the spread works, what it's doing, whether it stops on its own. Pat is chasing a diagnosis and may not know that yet.

## Finding Them

**They are contractable, which is the only reason they're findable at all.** An order that takes commissions has to be reachable by the kind of person who commissions one. It does not advertise, it has no hall, and there is no place to go and knock — but somewhere there is a way to make an offer, and somewhere there is a person who carries it.

That makes ports the natural place to look, and it makes Vulture's Nest the obvious one: the Tideward Compact tolerates anything that moves through the right channels and stays invisible enough not to force the Regency's hand (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Tideward Compact). An order that needs both discretion and a steady supply of children nobody misses is describing that town's blind spots exactly.

## Open

- The name. See above.
- Where they actually are. Deliberately unestablished — a home would make them a raid rather than a hunt.
- How a contract is placed, and who carries it.
- Whether the cursegivers are the only ones who pay, or whether the leadership is running an exposure of its own that a party could find and use.
- What the order gets out of it. Coin is the assumption and the least interesting answer.
- Whether any cursegiver has ever refused at the end, and what the order does with one who does.
