# Rue

Light-cartographer, formerly of the Soft Edge survey. Glasslight Reach.

She used to map the Soft Edge — one of the ones whose atlases record where the colors stop behaving, and whether the line has moved. One year it moved farther than any atlas could hold steady. She turned in her findings, watched the senior cartographers argue about whether to record the real number or a gentler one, and stopped recording for anyone after that.

Now she walks the fractures in the cliff and names every place the quartz has cracked since — a private atlas nobody commissioned, kept the way the official ones used to be kept, before she decided the official ones were lying by omission.

She does not raise her voice, but Rue's stillness isn't the town's gentle kind. It's controlled the way a held breath is controlled. The anger is in the precision: an exactness that doesn't let anything, or anyone, get away with being vague about what it is. She will correct anyone who calls the Unheld gentle, calmly, completely, and at whatever length the correction requires.

What she does when nobody's watching: re-measures the same three fractures on a rotation, alone, at hours when the streets are empty enough that the singing of wind through the fractures is the only other sound. She has never told anyone this is a ritual. She would object to the word if someone used it.

What she never fully said: Somewhere in her, under the precision, is a sentence. "I will never stop measuring."
