# Marren

*Glasskin. Prism-grinder, lower streets. Glasslight Reach.*

---

Makes the best prisms in the Reach and is almost never kind about anything else she makes contact with. Her gift is real and specific: she names the exact shade of a person's failing the way a light-cartographer names a hue nobody's bothered to write down yet, and she is never wrong about the color, only about whether saying it out loud was necessary.

The town tolerates her for the prisms. Nobody defends the rest of it. She corrects apprentices' color-sense in front of whoever's in the shop, precisely, in a voice that never quite rises — Glasslight doesn't do rising, even Marren, even now — and somehow that makes it worse, not better. A shout would be over quickly. This isn't.

She turns the same eye on herself, without mercy, in private, and it isn't self-improvement — it's the same weapon, pointed inward on schedule, as if fairness demanded she take her own medicine before dosing anyone else. She would call this integrity. Nobody who's watched her do it for long calls it that.

**What she's seen the party hasn't:** what it costs a town this quiet to keep a person like her employed anyway — the small, constant negotiation between "her work is the best we have" and "we all flinch a little when she walks in."

**What she does when nobody's watching:** grinds a prism for hours longer than the commission requires, checking the angle against a memory of a specific hue she's never named out loud to anyone, and has never gotten quite right.

**What was named and never paid for.** Once, a long time ago, she said something true about someone she loved — precisely, the way she says everything — and it landed exactly as hard as the truth always lands when it's aimed at someone who can't defend against being seen that clearly. She has spent the years since trying to make the world smaller than that one sentence turned out to be, correcting everyone else's shade so precisely that nobody notices she's never once corrected her own account of that day.
