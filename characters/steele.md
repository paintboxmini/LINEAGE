# Steele

Combat chef. Runs the orphanage in the capital's outer ring — the one rebuilt on the ground where the first one burned, which he was inside for.

**He is a returning player character.** Steele was Kevin's previous character, brought back as an NPC with explicit permission, and the table wants to meet him. Play him as somebody's finished character rather than a piece of set dressing: he has a complete kit, a familiar with opinions, and a life that continued after his last session without asking anyone's approval.

---

## The Kit

**The fairy dragon.** Rides his shoulder. It does the cooking — not helps with it, does it. Steele sources, preps, butchers and plates; the actual application of heat is the dragon's, and always has been. The two of them have worked as one cook for so long that Steele no longer narrates the division of labour and will look at you strangely if you ask about it.

**The cleaver.** Enormous, worn on his back, and it is a kitchen tool that happens to be the size of a greatsword. It breaks down carcasses. It also ends arguments. He does not own a weapon; he owns a cleaver, and the distinction matters to him more than it does to anyone he has ever had to use it on.

---

## What He Did

The original orphanage took him in. It burned with him in it. Years later he came back to the same ground and built another one, himself, and it is open and operating and takes children with nowhere else to go.

Three choices are stacked in that sentence and none of them were obvious.

**He came back.** Nobody is owed a return. He could have been anywhere.

**He built it rather than funding it.** The place is his labour, not his charity. It exists because he put it up.

**He built it on the same ground.** There is better ground in the capital, and cheaper ground, and ground that is not where a building full of children burned down. He did not use any of it. Whatever the fire took, he was not willing to let it also take the site.

---

## The Chef and the Orphanage Are One Fact

He is not a chef who happens to run an orphanage. The building is full of children and every one of them eats, every day, and he is the one who feeds them. The cooking is not his hobby or his past life. It is the daily mechanism by which the place actually works.

Which is also why the kit is what it is. A cleaver that size is for breaking down a lot of animal quickly and cheaply. A dragon that cooks is a hearth he does not have to pay to keep lit. Read his whole loadout as the equipment of a man feeding forty people on very little, and it stops looking like adventuring gear at all.

---

## Where It Sits

The **outer ring** — the band of the capital just inside the wall, before the Marketplace crowd begins. Cheap ground, and the part of the city where people who could not afford to be closer ended up once the taxes went where they went (`places/capital/capital.md`, Why the taxes are what they are).

That placement is the point. The children with nowhere else to go are already out here. He did not build somewhere respectable and ask them to travel to it.

---

## How He Runs It

No conditions on entry. No selection, no assessment, no waiting on whether a child is the right kind of problem. If they have nowhere else, they are in.

He does not tell the story of the fire to the children, and he does not hide it either. The building is on the site. Anyone old enough to ask gets a plain answer and no more of one than they asked for.

---

## Running Him

He does not recruit and he does not appeal. If the party turns up he is busy — prep, repairs, a queue of small things — and he will answer questions while he keeps working. Expect to be fed. Expect it to be genuinely good, because it is, and expect him not to make anything of that.

He undersells everything. Asked what he does, he says he cooks. Asked about the fire, he says it burned. Asked why he rebuilt on the same spot, he changes the subject or he doesn't answer, and either way he doesn't explain himself.

He accepts help with the work immediately and without ceremony. He is much harder to give anything to.

**The temptation to avoid** is making him wounded or making him a saint. He is neither. He is a man who decided one specific thing a long time ago and has simply not stopped doing it since. Play the ordinariness — the noticing is the party's job, not his.

**One thing for the GM's awareness rather than the table's.** Kevin is playing a combat chef again. Whatever that rhyme means is the player's to notice, not something to point at.

## Not Yet Set

- **The fire.** What caused it, whether anyone was at fault, and whether Steele knows. All open. It is the obvious hook and it should stay unspent until it is worth spending.
- **What it cost him physically.** He was inside. Whether that marked him, and how visibly, is unwritten.
- **The fairy dragon.** Name, temperament, how they met, and whether it talks. None of it set.
- **Everything before the original orphanage.**
- **His stat line is recorded, and it is not what his deck implies.** The archived sheet gives **Mind 3 / Body 4 / Soul 2**, which is HP 21 under the current formula — the sheet said 17, from the retired `(3 × Body) + 6`. His deck ran ten cards at 4 Red / 4 Blue / 2 Green, so it neither matches his stats by colour (4 Blue on Mind 3) nor fits the current cap, which would be 9. Left as found rather than tidied: it is what was actually played, and the cap is newer than he is.
- **One card was printed under a different name.** REPEL appears on Kevin's physical card as EARTHQUAKE — same effect, renamed after the card was made. Worth knowing before somebody rules it a second card.
- **He received PARADOX from the Oracle in his first session**, which is the only advancement on record.
- **The deck itself** is preserved at `experimental/archives/frost-and-steele-decks.md`. Its red half is BLOOD TITHE, PAIN IS FUEL and GAMBLER'S RUIN — a colour of cards about paying with yourself to keep something standing.
- **Any tie to an order or faction.** Deliberately unanswered.
