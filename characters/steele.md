# Steele

The Fog Basin. He runs the orphanage there — the one rebuilt on the ground where the first one burned, which he was inside for.

---

## What He Did

The original took him in. It burned with him in it. Years later he came back to the same ground and built another one, himself, and it is open and operating and takes children with nowhere else to go.

Three choices are stacked in that sentence and none of them were obvious.

**He came back.** Nobody is owed a return. He could have been anywhere.

**He built it rather than funding it.** The place is his labour, not his charity. It exists because he put it up.

**He built it on the same ground.** There is better ground in the basin, and easier ground, and ground that is not where a building full of children burned down. He did not use any of it. Whatever the fire took, he was not willing to let it also take the site.

---

## The Cost Is the Point

A safe place is a place somebody is paying to hold. The basin is permanent fog that reorders its own paths, it is a long trip to bring a child here from anywhere else in the basin, and none of that has been solved — it is absorbed. He carries the inconvenience so the children do not have to be turned away for it.

That is what makes the orphanage a standing answer rather than an open question. Not that it is safe. That someone is on the hook for it, continuously, and has not stopped.

---

## How He Runs It

No conditions on entry. No selection, no assessment, no waiting on whether a child is the right kind of problem. If they have nowhere else, they are in.

He does not tell the story of the fire to the children, and he does not hide it either. The building is on the site. Anyone old enough to ask gets a plain answer and no more of one than they asked for.

---

## Running Him

He does not recruit and he does not appeal. If the party turns up, he is busy — there is always something half-finished, because a building this size run by one man is never finished — and he will answer questions while he keeps working.

He undersells it. Asked what he does, he says he keeps a house. Asked about the fire, he says it burned. Asked why he rebuilt on the same spot, he changes the subject or he doesn't answer, and either way he doesn't explain himself.

He accepts help with the work immediately and without ceremony. He is much harder to give anything to.

**The temptation to avoid** is making him wounded or making him a saint. He is neither. He is a man who decided one specific thing a long time ago and has simply not stopped doing it since. Play the ordinariness — the noticing is the party's job, not his.

---

## Not Yet Set

- **The fire.** What caused it, whether anyone was at fault, and whether Steele knows. All open. It is the obvious hook and it should stay unspent until it is worth spending.
- **What it cost him physically.** He was inside. Whether that marked him, and how visibly, is unwritten.
- **Everything before the original orphanage.** Where he came from, and how he ended up in it.
- **Whether he fights.** He has no stat line. A deck of his was drafted and retired to `experimental/archives/frost-and-steele-decks.md` — ten cards at 4 Red / 4 Blue / 2 Green, implying Body 4, Mind 4, Soul 2 under the current deck-size rule. That deck was archived because it had no character behind it; there is one now, so it is available if he ever needs one. Worth a look before writing a new one: its red half is BLOOD TITHE, PAIN IS FUEL and GAMBLER'S RUIN, which is a colour of cards about paying with yourself to keep something standing.
- **Any tie to an order or faction.** Deliberately unanswered. He reads as a man doing this alone, and that is currently doing more work than an affiliation would.
