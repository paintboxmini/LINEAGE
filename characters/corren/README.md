# Corren

*Bathed once, ordinary, like everyone in the congregation — until one day, decades later, he went back. Dead now. His death is the hidden origin of the Final Current. Nobody but Kaine knows that.*

---

Corren's first bath was the same as anyone's: normal custom, a rite of passage, the community holding the ropes, coming back changed (`factions/people-of-promise.md`, Ritual Bathing). Fluid, in his case — open, unsettled, possibilities still moving in him more than they do for most.

He remembered versions of people that don't exist anymore, after that — the self a friend had before a hard decade, the self a stranger showed him once in the water and never became. He greeted people by those selves. Not out of confusion; he wasn't senile and everyone around him knew it. It was fidelity. He genuinely believed the self he'd glimpsed was as real as the one standing in front of him, maybe realer, and he refused to pretend otherwise for the sake of comfort.

People found him warm, at first. Being seen that generously is rare. It curdled fast for anyone he called by a name they'd buried on purpose — an old cruelty, an old cowardice, a person they'd worked hard to stop being. Corren didn't do this to wound. He genuinely didn't understand why it would. To him, every version was still owed the courtesy of being real.

**What actually happened, decades later (GM Secret — not known to Kaine's followers, never said aloud by Corren while he lived, not confirmed to the table unless a GM decides the moment has earned it).** He broke the norm — privately, without telling the congregation, without the ropes — and went back. He wasn't chasing more of what the first bath gave him. He was chasing the same completion the Last Bath gives the dead (`factions/people-of-promise.md`) — quietly, alone, without ever once calling it that.

**The declaration he made both times.** Before each bath, Corren said the same private words — a version of a vow, close enough to a binding statement that anyone who'd heard both would notice. Whatever answers a declaration like that is, by every account of how it's supposed to work, still owed. Corren treated the second bath as the place he might finally find out.
