# Iron

---

## Appearance & Manner

Hat in hand when he talks to strangers, and he keeps it there longer than the conversation needs. More tired than frightened, and has been for a season.

Something small and matte-black keeps to his heels the whole time, low to the ground, never quite touching him.

---

## What He Owns

The Western Property, bordered by the watchtower ruins. Good ground until it wasn't. A sinkhole opened in one of his fields last season — the first in living memory at Briarwatch — and he fenced it off himself with rope and splintered posts, which is the entire extent of anyone's response so far.

He has been waiting for help longer than he'd like to admit. He will not say so directly, and he will not ask twice.

---

## What He Knows

Glimpses over years, never contact. He has seen enough to suspect something lives under his fields and nowhere near enough to know what, and he is careful to say so in those terms.

He does not know whether that's what caused the sinkhole. He does not know whether it's connected to the Fenwool the farms keep losing. He is not wrong to wonder about either, and he will not pretend either is settled.

**His suspicion is a lead, not a briefing.** He has bounded knowledge and he stays inside it — a party that asks him for more gets an honest "I don't know," not a hook.

---

## The Neighbour's Field

He keeps the bundles up at the abandoned homestead down the road (`places/briarwatch.md`, The Abandoned Homestead). Two new ones every autumn, since the family left. He has never told anybody and he does not bring it up.

Asked who ties them, he says he does. Asked why, he says somebody should. Asked anything past that, he doesn't have more — it is not a story he has, it is a thing he does on the way back from his own fields.

He works that yard and nothing else. He has never been inside the house and has no reason to be, so he does not know what has taken up the doorway.

---

## The Duskwick

It started showing up a season back and never left. He doesn't feed it. It doesn't seem to need him to.

Asked whose it is, he'll say it isn't. Not his, exactly — it just hasn't gone anywhere. See `bestiary/duskwick.md`.

---

## Running Him

He is what the party is being asked to help, and the encounter works better the less he performs it. No speech, no plea, no crisis in his voice. A man standing at the edge of a hole in his own field with his hat in his hands, answering questions accurately.

If the party has already passed the abandoned homestead (`places/briarwatch.md`, The Abandoned Homestead), they have seen what the end of this looks like, and nobody has to point at it.

**The bundles are an answer, not a scene.** If someone works out that the fresh cuttings at an empty house and the farmer down the road are the same fact, let them ask and let him say yes. Flat, one sentence, no weight put on it. It is the smallest possible version of what he is, and building a moment around it makes him perform something he would never perform.
