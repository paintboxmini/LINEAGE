# Harlock

*Captain of the **Arcadia**. Third seat on the Tideward Compact, and the only one of the three who would burn it down.*

**Cards:** `cards/harlock.md`

**His position at the Nest is `places/vultures-nest.md`** — the Compact, the crash, the count, and what is in his hold. This file is the man and the fight.

**Mind 4 / Body 6 / Soul 3 — HP 31**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 13

---

## Appearance

Big through the shoulders and heavier than he looks, because most of it is under the coat. Weathered past his age. Hands scarred across the backs rather than the palms — the marks of someone who has punched more things than he has gripped.

Never armed obviously. There is always something within reach, and he has never once had to look for it.

---

## Behavior

**The temper is real and it is famous, and it is also the least interesting thing about him.** Everyone at the Nest can tell you a story about Harlock going off. Nobody can tell you a story about him going off when it cost him something. That is not luck.

He'll tell you loyalty is a transaction and nothing more, and say it like he's saving you the trouble of finding out the hard way yourself. He was loyal to someone once, for free, before the Nest — the story ends there whenever he tells it, which isn't often. He calls the doctrine clarity. Nobody who's heard where the story actually ends calls it that.

**He deals honestly, which surprises people.** A transaction is a transaction; welching would make his word worth less and his word is the thing he sells. What he will not do is anything for free, or anything on the strength of having done it before.

**Soul 3 is the lowest number on his sheet and it is the exact thing his doctrine is about.** He is hard to frighten, hard to out-fight, and unusually easy to reach through a bond — a person he owed something to, a name from before the Nest, an appeal that is not an offer. A party that tries to buy him gets a fair price. A party that gets under the doctrine gets something he did not intend to give.

## Trait — Running Hot

**This is a Trait, not a Passive** (`rules/character-creation.md`, Passives and Traits) — it costs no Action, is never played, and has no die.

**Each time Harlock takes damage, he gains 1 Deadly** (`rules/card-glossary.md`).

Stacks the way Deadly always does: each stack adds a d6 to one future damage roll, not extra dice on the same roll. Hurting him makes him hit harder, every time, with no cap and no cooldown.

**What this does to the fight.** A party that burns him down fast is fine. A party that grinds him is feeding him, and by the fourth round they are in a fight with something much worse than they started. He is a race, and the table should figure that out the hard way.

---

## Trait — Cuts Losses

**Harlock does not fight to the death. Ever.**

When a fight stops being worth what it is costing him — his own read, not a threshold — he leaves, and he leaves properly: cover, a direction picked in advance, no parting line. He has never once stayed in to finish something on principle, because principle is not a thing he believes in.

**A GM should not override this to give the party a kill.** It is the same doctrine as the rest of him, applied to himself, and the correct consequence is that **Harlock cannot be beaten in a straight fight — he can only be trapped.** A party that wants him finished has to take the exit away first, and that is a plan, not a combat.

*If he leaves owing the party a hiding, he pays it later. That is also a transaction.*

---

## Combat Identity

High Body, real Mind, no Soul. He hits like the front of a ship and he is not stupid about it.

- **Opens at range or holds position**, reads a round, then closes. BOARDING ACTION is the pivot and everything before it is setup.
- **Wants to be hit slightly**, and the table should feel him welcome it. Running Hot means the first two exchanges going badly for him are going badly for the party.
- **THE PRICE is how he fights people he can't punch** — it hands the target a choice and both branches cost them. He enjoys that more than damage.
- **CUT THE LINE is him shedding a problem**, in combat exactly as out of it.
