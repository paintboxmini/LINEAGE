# Wren

*Promise community, Glasslight Reach. Steve's parent (`characters/steve-and-pip.md`).*

---

Believes, completely and without any sense of contradiction, that love means keeping the people you love safe from the Final Current.
It doesn't look like control from the inside, or even from most angles outside. Wren speaks fluently in the language of care: *not tonight, you're tired* / *let's wait until you're steadier* / *I just don't think you're ready, and I'd rather be wrong about that than wrong the other way.* Every individual sentence is reasonable. The pattern, over years, is a slow closing of a door that was never announced as closing.

Steve is who this is about, and has been his whole life. The household is, by most visible measures, well cared for. He is also not, and has never been, the one who decides when he's ready for something every other person in the congregation decides for themselves. He is a grown man who has never once been permitted to be the judge of that, and the Promise's own central rite is the specific thing he has never been judged ready for. Wren has never once framed this as control, and would be genuinely wounded — not performing the wound, actually wounded — by anyone who named it that plainly.

**What Wren has decided to stop thinking about:** whether the fear underneath all of it is really about the water, or about what happens to a household once the person in it is allowed to come back changed on his own schedule instead of Wren's.

**Where this lands at the table.** Steve steps onto the lowering platform at the Full Tide ceremony and Wren calls him back, in the usual register, and it does not work (`quests/tide-pulls-back.md`, The Platform — Steve's Beat). Wren is right this time. Years of being the person who decides is exactly why being right isn't enough, and if he goes down anyway Wren watches the platform drop and has nothing left to say — the first silence in the whole arc.

**The declaration made on Steve's behalf.** Wren said something, once, that functioned like a binding statement but wasn't Wren's to make (`rules/character-creation.md`, Declaring a Price) — a promise about Steve's future, spoken as if his consent were a formality rather than the entire question.

**The grammar is the crime.** A Price is declared in the first person: *I never*, *I must*, *I always*, *I cannot*, *Once I…*, *Whenever…*. Wren stayed inside that grammar and reached outside it anyway — something in the shape of *whenever he goes to the water, I go with him.* Every word of it is Wren's own conduct. None of it is Wren's to decide. Spoken in front of the congregation, in the community's own language of care, and received as devotion. *(Exact wording is a proposal, not fixed — the shape is what matters: a first-person pledge that binds a second person's future.)*

**Which is why the platform is where it comes due.** Steve steps onto it and Wren is not going down with him. Either the pledge breaks, or the world does something about the fact that it can't be kept — and the person the pledge was actually about is the one standing on the platform. See `quests/tide-pulls-back.md`, The Platform — Steve's Beat.

**Only if he goes down.** If the party gets him off the platform, the pledge isn't broken and nothing is charged. This whole thread stays theoretical, and Wren goes on being right about him for another year.

**If he does go down:** a broken pledge normally costs the one who spoke it — a curse, a forced card, a permanent mark. This one wasn't spoken about the one who spoke it, and the cost follows the subject. Wren declared, Steve paid, and Wren is not visibly charged anything at all (`characters/steve-and-pip.md`).

What Wren does with that is played at the table, not decided here.
