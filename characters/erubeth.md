# Erubeth

Regency-backed detective. Loner. Traveling. Always in the middle of something.

**Cards:** `cards/erubeth.md`

*Filed under Masaharu in the archives, which is the older name. `places/vultures-nest.md` is where he is actually written into the world and it calls him Erubeth, so Erubeth is the name. Nothing else changed.*

**His position at the Nest is at `places/vultures-nest.md`** — the Regency backing he does not advertise, the FourthEye thread, what he will and will not trade, and how he behaves toward a party he has not decided about yet. **This file is the man**: what was done to him, what he does about it, the stat block, the traits, and how he fights.

*What he is chasing is at `items/fourtheye.md`, and what makes it is at `bestiary/ocellus.md`. Worth reading both before running him. The drug eats the exact faculty he has built a career on, which is why he is the right man for the case and the last man who should be anywhere near the crate — and it only ever shows its users the minutes on either side of the thing that mattered, which is how his case has gone from the beginning.*

*It also cannot shortcut the case, and a GM should know why before a player thinks of it. The thing FourthEye can be turned on is a specific person you already know by face or name (`items/fourtheye.md`, GM Secret). The man Erubeth actually wants — whoever is running the crates — is a role, and a role is a question, not a person. The drug has never answered a question.*

**Read `## The Room` below before running him at all.** He takes FourthEye. He has taken it for years. That is not a rumour, a suspicion or a thread for the party to uncover slowly — it is the load-bearing fact about him and half of what he does only makes sense once you know it.

Mind 5 / Body 3 / Soul 4 — HP 21
Creature Threat Rating: 12

---

## Appearance

Long coat, wide-brimmed hat. Moves like someone who has already decided where the exits are.

The boomstick is custom — longer barrel than it needs to be, adjustment rings along the stock, a small carved sigil near the grip that he never explains. He makes his own ammunition. Different loads for different jobs. He counts his shots.

**It is the same kind of weapon as Kevin's pepper grinder** (`campaign/kevin.md`, How It Works): hand-worked, one shot per working, and loaded with something its owner made rather than bought. That is why Reload costs a whole Action — the gun is not a thing you thumb a fresh round into, it is a thing you have to set up again. Whether his propellant is the same food-craft Kevin uses or something else entirely is not set.

---

## Behavior

Patient. Reads before he acts. Will let a cycle go by gathering information rather than waste a shot. He knows when you've telegraphed a move and he files it.

Not cruel. Not interested in pain. Interested in resolution.

## The Room

**They were taken first.** His wife and his child were kidnapped by the organization he was dismantling — the same one that moves FourthEye — and held somewhere, and killed there. Not at home. In a room that belonged to them, that they had chosen, that they used for this.

**He uses the drug.** The one he is under Regency authority to eradicate. He has used it for years, he is not confused about what it is doing to him, and he is one of the very few people alive who knows the save exists and can make it (`items/fourtheye.md`, GM Secret). He aims at the man he is hunting — he has the face, he is one of the only people who has ever had it — and **every time he steers it, he lands in that room instead.** The bond is heavier than the intent. It is not a failed roll; the drug goes where he is actually holding, and where he is actually holding has never once been the criminal.

**He has studied it.** Dozens of times, maybe hundreds. Perfect clarity, four or five minutes at a stretch, the way nobody ever gets to look at a scene. He has measured it against itself across years. He knows which board is loose, which corner the light comes into, what was moved between one visit and the next and what has not been moved at all.

**And he cannot place it.** That is the prison. The drug has never told a single user where they were looking (`items/fourtheye.md`), so what Erubeth has is a perfect image of a room and no idea what city it is in. He could walk past the building every day. He has possibly done exactly that.

**Two things follow, and they are the whole reason he is worth playing.**

**The first is what the party can give him.** Anybody who walks into that room and hears him describe it is holding the single thing he has not been able to buy, trade, threaten or earn in years. He will not ask. He has stopped believing the ask works. If a player recognises a room from a description he let slip, that is the moment the character turns all the way around.

**The second is the thing he does not say.** He calls it working the scene, and it is genuinely that — he has pulled real detail out of it, and a GM should let him be right about that. He also knows he would go back if there were nothing left in it at all. Do not have him admit this. Do not have him deny it either. Let it sit exactly where he keeps it.

*Their names are not set. Neither is the room's location — whether the organization still uses it, and whether anything is still in it, is open and should be decided when it pays off rather than now.*

## Trait — Loaded

**This is a Trait, not a Passive** (`rules/character-creation.md`, Passives and Traits) —
it costs no Action, is never played, and has no die. Creatures do not have Passives.

Erubeth begins each combat Loaded.

While Loaded, all his attacks deal +d6 damage.

After any attack — win or lose the reveal — he becomes Unloaded and loses the +d6 bonus until he Reloads.

**Reload** — costs his full Action. A card effect that says "Reload" does it for free.

---

## Trait — Habituated

A **FOURTHEYE** curse card (`rules/card-glossary.md`) is in his deck permanently and does not come out of it. Curses sit outside the deck-size cap (`rules/character-creation.md`), so his deck is 12 cards and the curse rides along on top of them.

**When he draws it, he takes the Scry and does not discard.** The card costs every other user of it a card out of hand — the thread lost, the thing you were about to do. Erubeth lost that thread years ago and has built everything since around not needing it. He is better at being on this drug than anyone the party will ever meet.

That is the trait doing what the character does: at the table the addiction reads as an advantage, cleanly and repeatedly, and nothing about the fight ever shows you the cost.

---

## Combat Identity

High Mind. Low Body. Fragile, precise, dangerous at range.

- Stays Backline. Creates Blind and Staggered to set up the shot.
- Does not rush. Does not overcommit.

*Note what he is built out of. Three of his four signature cards Scry, CASE FILE opens on Scry 3, and its flavour line is "Every shot is preceded by the same question" (`cards/erubeth.md`). That deck was written before any of the above was and needs no revising for it — it was always a man who spends his life looking slightly ahead of where he is standing, asking the same question every time. Now we know what the question is.*
