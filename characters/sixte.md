# Sixte

*A fencer whose whole art is being ready for the thing you have not done yet.*

**Cards:** `cards/sixte.md`

*Stat block is a starting point. Scale to match where he enters the story — early campaign he is a duellist with a reputation and no stake in the party's business, late campaign he is someone who has had time to study them. The three Circles are the character; the numbers around them are negotiable.*

**Mind 4 / Body 4 / Soul 4 — HP 24**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 12

Even across all three, on purpose. He carries one Circle per colour and nothing in his kit privileges one over another — the stat line is the same claim the cards make.

---

## The Three Circles

His signature cards are one move, performed three ways. EMBER CIRCLE, MIRROR CIRCLE, THORN CIRCLE — Red, Blue, Green. Each grants a defence in its own colour, and each makes **the first attack of that colour automatically lose the reveal**. No card spent to block it. No guess. The attack simply does not land.

Then the Circle is discarded. One per colour, once each.

**So he is not hard to hit. He is hard to hit the first time.** A fencer who has set a guard in every line and can close each one exactly once — after which he is an ordinary swordsman with ordinary answers, and knows it.

---

## How He Fights

He is answering, not attacking. The flavour on his own cards says it: *"Red always answers force with force. He just answers first."*

Which means his real weapon is what he does before the fight. He watches. He decides which line you are most likely to come down, and he sets that Circle. Against a party he has never seen, all three guards are worth the same and he spends them as they come. Against a party he has watched, one of them is already waiting for the thing they always do.

*"Show him the same trick twice and watch it stop working."*

**The cost is that he can be emptied.** Three Circles, three colours, and a party that reads him will spend a cheap attack in each line to strip the guard before committing to anything real. That is the intended counterplay and it should be allowed to work — a GM who quietly re-arms him has taken the fight's actual puzzle away.

---

## Running Him

Unhurried in a way that reads as arrogance and is not. He is early, everywhere, for everything. He stands where the exits are without appearing to have chosen the spot.

He does not trash talk and he does not explain. If asked how he does it he will answer accurately and it will not help — the answer is that he decided before you arrived, which is not a technique anybody can copy from a sentence.

**He is not a villain by default.** Nothing in the cards says whose side he is on. An opponent who never lands a first hit is frightening; an ally who has already set a guard against the thing about to happen is the best news the party gets all session. Pick per table.

## Not Yet Set

- **Everything about who he is.** This file is written out of three cards and nothing else — there was no character file behind them, only the deck. Backstory, allegiance, age, where he trained and why he is wherever the party meets him are all open.
- **The name.** Renamed from Kino, which was a collision rather than a preference: `places/vultures-nest.md` already has a Kino, a fifteen-year-old running the Red Scarves on the docks, and he is fully written and embedded in the opening location. Two characters were sharing one name and only one of them had a file. *Sixte* is a fencing guard — the one that closes the outside line — which is what all three of his cards do. Rename him again freely; nothing depends on it.
- **Whether the Circles are learned or innate.** A trained parry and a supernatural one play very differently, and the cards do not say.
