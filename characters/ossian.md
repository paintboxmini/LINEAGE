# Ossian

Head priest, Temple of Unity, capital. Kevin's father (`campaign/kevin.md`).

---

## The War Taught Him One Thing

He was a soldier before he was a priest, and he came out of it with a single conviction he has never revised: **nothing worth doing gets done from indoors.** Not a philosophy he arrived at gradually. Something the war handed him whole, in whatever way wars do that, and he has spent every year since acting on it.

He does not tell war stories. Asked about it directly he will answer the question asked, accurately, and not one sentence past it.

---

## What He Changed

The Temple of Unity is the interfaith cathedral at the heart of the Temple District — the building people come to when they cannot resolve something themselves. Under Ossian it is **mostly empty**, and that is not neglect. It is the reform.

He sends the priests out. Communion is held in the street: on the Marketplace edge, at the gate, on doorsteps, wherever there are people who were never going to walk into a cathedral. Preaching happens where the congregation already is, and the congregation is not in the pews.

**What did not move.** The temple's other function — hearings, the scales, the ritual conditions under which a dispute can actually be settled — still happens in the building, and still waits for people to come to it. Ossian sends out communion and preaching, not arbitration. A quarrel has to be carried through the door under its own power, which is exactly the part Vescal eventually stopped being able to stomach (`characters/vescal.md`).

So the reform is partial, and he knows it is. He will say the two halves are different work. He has never claimed to have fixed the second one.

## The Wine

The temple is renowned for its wines, and it sells them.

**Outside Lady Elara's tax.** No Marketplace License, no brass token, no Warden's chalk mark, no cut going up to the Traders' Hall. The temple sells from its own ground, at its own price, and does not file.

This is not smuggling and nobody is pretending otherwise — it is done in daylight, at volume, by an institution at the centre of the Temple District. It is a standing refusal that has never been formally tested, and the longer it goes untested the more expensive testing it becomes.

There is precedent one street over: Harlan Vetch holds the Temple of the Forge apart from Elara's commercial apparatus on principle too (`places/capital/capital.md`, Temple of the Forge). The two are not the same refusal. Harlan's temple **declines coin as worship** — money left on the anvil is politely returned. Ossian's temple takes the money and declines the tax. Harlan abstains; Ossian does not comply. A GM can play that difference for real friction between two men who both think they are the principled one.

## Running Him

Find him outdoors. He is at the gate, or on the Marketplace edge, or in a doorway in the outer ring with a cup in his hand and four people listening. He is not at the temple, and a party who goes looking for him there will be told so by an echoing room.

Warm, direct, unhurried, and physically present in a way retired soldiers often are — he stands closer than a priest usually stands. He pours for people before he talks to them. He asks what you came for and he expects a real answer, because he gives one.

**He is not a hypocrite and he is not a rebel.** He thinks he is simply doing the job correctly. The tax refusal and the empty cathedral are the same conviction, applied twice, and if you tell him either one is a provocation he will look genuinely puzzled.
