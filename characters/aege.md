# Aege

## Appearance

Lean, weathered face.

---

## Voice

On the road: Speaks in terms of paths, not places. Doesn't explain decisions.

At a shared table, not on the trail: same person, a wider register. Earthy, plainspoken, more willing to talk when the setting is a meal instead of a road. Still doesn't perform mystery, still doesn't waste words on anything that isn't true — just more of them, when there's time to sit.

"Eat. Road's longer on an empty stomach. You can owe the world something useful later."

---

## What She Does

Guides travelers, when asked, toward wherever they're actually headed. Knows the roads, the cuts, the ways that aren't on any map. Reads how someone moves before she agrees to anything — rushing, purposeless repositioning, visible anxiety about formation all tell her something, and she acts on it without saying so.

---

## Backstory

Grew up in the Turnroot Weald. Left as a child. Has lived in and around Vulture's Nest since. She knows the forest the way you know a place you left before you understood it — not as terrain, as texture.

Her family are people of the forest. They stay under canopy year-round, keep forrest customs, and never needed maps because the Weald always routed them where they needed to be.

## Story Hook

**Stage one — the letter, and it is only a worry.** A letter reaches her at the Listing Keel (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Letter). Her family, her younger brother growing up without her, they miss her, they want her safe — and, at the end, the thing that snags: they have read forest signs saying this year's overgrowth season will be a bad one, and they have begun their preparations early.

To them that is a hard season coming and a village to get ready. To her it is the people who taught her to read those signs telling her the forest is doing something it does not usually do.

**She does not decide anything.** She says it out loud, over a meal, to people she met that afternoon, and then she eats her dinner. That is the whole of what she has at the start. A GM who converts this into a departure has spent the thread.

**Stage two — the real news, whenever the table has earned it.** The Root Heart has turned aggressive. The ground her people can safely walk has shrunk to a fraction of what it was, and Rootstalkers that used to pass a forest family without interest have started hunting them. *Then* she goes herself. No one sends her. No one has to.

*The connection is a proposal, not canon: her family may be reading the Root Heart's doing and calling it overgrowth, because overgrowth is the word they have for it. It would mean the letter is already the first symptom, misread by the people closest to it, and that the party heard about the Root Heart before anyone knew that is what they were hearing. Left as a proposal because whether Stage Two is a separate later message or the same thing arriving properly named is Drew's call.*

---

## Where She Appears

**Vulture's Nest — the Listing Keel, first.** She takes a letter from Salla across the bar while the party is buying their room, and walks off with it without a glance at them (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Letter). She eats there later the same day, shares a table if they seem worth one, and buys the meal if the Red Scarves got to them that afternoon. If they ask about the letter, she answers it.

**Vulture's Nest — the east gate**, where she is found when someone wants a guide, and the party's actual guide for the leg to Briarwatch if they hire her. Delivers them to Briarwatch, hands over a Carrion Feather, continues to the Turnroot Weald alone.

*The dinner is the introduction and the gate is the hire. They are the same day and they are not the same scene — she does not sell herself at the table.*

---

## Combat Identity

Reads the field before she commits to anything, same as she reads a traveler's feet before she agrees to guide them.
