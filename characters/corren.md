# Corren

*Bathed once, ordinary, like everyone in the congregation — until one day, decades later, he went back. Dead now. His death is the hidden origin of the Final Current. Nobody but Kaine knows that.*

---

Corren's first bath was the same as anyone's: normal custom, a rite of passage, the community holding the ropes, coming back changed (`factions/people-of-promise.md`, Ritual Bathing). Fluid, in his case — open, unsettled, possibilities still moving in him more than they do for most. It was supposed to be the only one. For most of his life, it was.

He remembered versions of people that don't exist anymore, after that — the self a friend had before a hard decade, the self a stranger showed him once in the water and never became. He greeted people by those selves. Not out of confusion; he wasn't senile and everyone around him knew it. It was fidelity. He genuinely believed the self he'd glimpsed was as real as the one standing in front of him, maybe realer, and he refused to pretend otherwise for the sake of comfort.

People found him warm, at first. Being seen that generously is rare. It curdled fast for anyone he called by a name they'd buried on purpose — an old cruelty, an old cowardice, a person they'd worked hard to stop being. Corren didn't do this to wound. He genuinely didn't understand why it would. To him, every version was still owed the courtesy of being real.

**What actually happened, decades later (GM Secret — not known to Kaine's followers, never said aloud by Corren while he lived, not confirmed to the table unless a GM decides the moment has earned it).** He went back. The norm allows exactly one bath, and he'd already had his — but privately, without telling the congregation, without the ropes, he broke it. He wasn't chasing more of what the first bath gave him. He was chasing the same completion the Last Bath gives the dead (`factions/people-of-promise.md`) — quietly, alone, without ever once calling it that.

**The declaration he made both times.** Before each of his two baths, decades apart, Corren said the same private words — a version of a vow, close enough to a binding statement that anyone who'd heard both would notice. Whatever answers a declaration like that is, by every account of how it's supposed to work, still owed. Corren treated the second bath as the place he might finally find out.

---

## Death (GM Secret — the founding moment of the Final Current, known to no one but Kaine, told by him to no one)

Low tide. Corren went down alone — no ropes, no congregation, onto rock the water only leaves bare a few hours at a stretch, standing where he'd never needed to stand before because his first bath, decades ago, had never required it. Kaine was already up in the cliffs, hidden, for reasons that had nothing to do with Corren at first.

Corren waited. Then it took him the way his first bath never had — thrashing, changing, faster and further than anything the congregation has a word for, going twice as far on a second try as he had on the first. Right before he was undone completely — the porousness finally winning, nothing left to climb back up the rocks — he passed through a form that Kaine, watching from above, read as powerful. Desirable. The single image the rest of Kaine's life has been built on top of — one man, one broken rule, one data point mistaken for a method.

Kaine never went down to him. There was nothing left to go down for by the time he might have. What the congregation knows: Corren went under one day and didn't come back — sad, and genuinely shocking, because as far as anyone knew he'd had his one bath, same as everyone, decades ago, and there was no reason to expect a second. What Kaine knows, and has told no one, not even his most devoted: he watched a man break a rule nobody breaks, try to disappear doing it, and kept the shape of the moment before disappearance as the whole point.
