# Elder Kaine

---

Promise-raised, old enough to have watched many people return to the Unheld.
He forms The Final Current after obtaining the lightning loop.

---

## The Goal

A resonant item aligned with the Storm Seat — unstable, dangerous in any hands, including his. Kaine wants it when the party arrives.

Performing signs and wonders, and crediting them to repeated bathing in the Unheld. He knows the Loop produces the wonders; he conceals it and lets the story of his own "unlocking" explain what people witness. The signs are real. The source is hidden.

Mid-term goal: use The Final Current's growing credibility to absorb the entire People of Promise community across Eclipseria.

Long-term: for a man like Kaine, it wouldn't end with the Promise community. That's just the first institution with infrastructure he can use.

---

## The Final Current

Kaine's sect. Operates within the Promise community. Uses the same ritual bathing the wider congregation already practices. His claim: that the Return ritual — coming back alive from what doctrine only ever describes as final — is proof that potential can be unlocked by anyone willing to go far enough.

More baths, in his theology, means more unlocking. The visible marks on his longest-devoted followers — the ones who've been under more times than anyone can count — read to Kaine and to them, as proof. Animal eyes, patches of stone-like skin, moss where hair should grow, a nose flattened and black, claws, and tails.

### The Communion

The sect's other rite, and the one that actually recruits. Bathing is the demand; the Communion is the invitation, and you don't have to have bathed to take part.

**Everyone collects their own.** Each member goes down to the water and fills a small vessel from the Unheld themselves. Not a bath — a walk down to the tideline and back, at low tide, with a stopper. It costs nerve and nothing else, which is the point: it is the first thing Kaine asks of anyone, and almost nobody refuses it.

**It goes flat on the way home, and everybody knows it will.** Grey water carried inland arrives as ordinary water — that isn't a secret or a revelation, it's the one thing every person in Eclipseria knows about the Unheld (`world/the-unheld.md`). Each member walks back up the cliffs holding a vessel of the most significant substance in their theology, and by the time they reach the gathering it's water. They have all felt that happen, personally, in their own hands.

**Then they pour it together, and Kaine gives it back.** Every vessel empties into one. He puts his hand in it, the water fizzes and pops, and he tells them — with total conviction, because he needs none of it to be a lie except the source — that what they are looking at is the Unheld returned to itself, and that the bathing is what lets him do it. They drink. It is real; the drinker is restored. The ring did it.

**Why it works better than any sermon.** It isn't his water, it's theirs. They went and got it. They watched their own sample die on the walk up, and then they watched it come back and they tasted it. Nothing Kaine says has to carry the argument, because every person in the room has already run the experiment on themselves.

**And it is a small, elegant blasphemy nobody quite catches.** Promise doctrine says everything returns to the Unheld. Kaine has quietly inverted it — he returns the Unheld — and he does it in the community's own vocabulary, in a room full of people who have spent their lives being told that the current only runs one way. Moderates who hear about it are unsettled and can't articulate why.

**It has to be drunk immediately.** The restoration is momentary, he says; hold it and you'll lose it again. Which is convenient, because it means nobody has ever carried a cup of it away to look at later.

---

Kaine doesn't argue doctrine with a new initiate. He shows them a body. Standing among his followers at a gathering, he gestures at whoever's changed the most visibly and says it plainly:

"Look around you, at the other members here today. If you want to unlock your true potential like these devouts, then it's time to take your bath."

The grin that goes with it is too large for the sentence.

## Character Notes

He's convincing — the Promise philosophy genuinely supports his reading of it, which is why moderates struggle to argue with him: he's using their own language.

Kaine has blood red pupils.

Kaine's followers see genuine signs and wonders and choose to hear his explanation of them rather than inspect their source. The first are intimate and useful: water becomes restorative when he touches it — staged as the Communion, above, and never as a parlour trick on any water that happens to be nearby — wood catches fire when he touches it, a tired and depressed person leaves energized after he touches them. He can also call lightning at range, including one public demonstration against a metal pole. He tells them the Unheld unlocked this in him through repeated bathing. The concealed Lightning Loop is what actually produces the wonders, and Kaine knows it. He does not wear it as a public symbol of the Promise — it can disappear among other rings on his hand, or be worn as a necklace when he wants it less conspicuous.

Inspiration for his beliefs: One afternoon, low tide, Kaine hidden up in the cliffs; Kaine watched silently as Corren went down alone, onto rock the tide only bares for a few hours at a stretch, and waited. What happened to him took longer and went further than any bathing Kaine had ever seen — thrashing, changing, past fluid, past anything the congregation has a word for. Right before Corren was completely undone, he passed through a form Kaine read, watching from above, as powerful. Desirable. Kaine never went down. There was nothing left to go down for. What he kept wasn't a method he understood — it was a single image, and a conviction he built backward from it: that going under enough times doesn't end in disappearing, it ends in that.
