# Black Maggie — Origin

She only ever met three others of her race. Two were her parents.

Her parents had no hope for their people. Not cruelty — absence. They lived day to day because they couldn't see a point to anything further. They didn't teach her the full practice. They didn't teach her much of anything. She grew up alone, mostly in the woods, with time and fragments.

Human children mocked her. The horns. The otherness.

What she had: pieces of her heritage. The beliefs surrounding the Archon. Suffering as something sacred, as the domain of the only thing she could connect to. She didn't have the compassion that was supposed to accompany it — the full practice, the rituals for processing and witnessing another's pain with care. She had the part about suffering mattering. She had nothing else.

As a teenager she hurt herself first. Then animals. Then the boys who had mocked her. Then her parents. All in the same spot in the woods — the place she already knew, the place that was already hers.

She stayed there. She kept going. It took her several years to reach a hundred. She wasn't hurrying. She had nowhere else to be.

When the first anchor point formed she felt the Archon's plane draw closer to hers and understood she had been right about something. That was enough.

What she actually needed was for her parents to show her a future worth living in. To show her what her people's gift looked like when it was whole. That version of her was real, and it was recent once. It is not gone — it is just very far down.
