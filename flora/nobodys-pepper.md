# Nobody's Pepper

A low, scrubby bush that fruits in small hard pods, growing on the spoil banks around the Nest's islands — the heaps of gravel and foreign dirt that ships have been dumping out of their holds for two hundred years.

**It is not from here.** Nothing on a spoil bank is. Ballast comes out of a ship as the cargo goes in, and it comes out carrying the soil and seed of wherever it was shovelled up. The banks around the Nest are two centuries of somewhere else, piled in layers, and Nobody's Pepper is the thing that took to it best.

---

## Growth

Spoil banks, ballast heaps, the made ground behind a working quay. It wants poor drainage-free rubble and full sun and it will not grow in good soil at all — a gardener who plants it in a bed gets a healthy green bush and no pods.

**No two banks grow the same pepper.** Heat, size, colour and how long it holds all vary bank to bank, because the plants on each bank descend from whatever came off whichever ship, crossed with whatever was there already. A pepper off the Longbar is small and vicious. One off the shingle behind the old rope walk is fat, mild, and almost sweet. They are unmistakably the same plant and they are not interchangeable in a pot.

---

## Harvesting

**The plant is easy. The provenance is the whole problem.** Picking a pod takes no skill and no permission; there is no season worth naming and the banks are public ground. What you cannot do is sell or cook it without knowing which bank it came off, and a bag of mixed pepper is worth less than either half of it separately.

The Nest solves this the way the Nest solves everything, with records. **Which ship dumped what, where, and when is ledger work** — Jonas keeps that, and the Compact's dumping registers go back further than anyone alive (`places/vultures-nest.md`). A buyer who can read a bank back to a manifest can tell you what a pepper is going to do before he tastes it. A forager who cannot is selling a bag of guesses.

This is the one plant in the region where **the useful expertise is filing rather than fieldcraft**, and it makes a certain kind of person very good at foraging who has never been good at anything outdoors.

---

## Culinary Note

Bright, fast heat with a green edge under it, and it does not survive long cooking — it goes in at the end or it goes in cold. Marta's kitchen uses it, cautiously, in a fish brine.

For a cook working from a grinder rather than a pantry, the variability is the appeal and the trap: the same charge does a different thing depending on a fact about a gravel heap. See `campaign/kevin.md`.
