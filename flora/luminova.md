# Luminova

Translucent leaves, palm-sized, growing in tiers on a low soft-wooded shrub of the middle canopy. They hold a steady cold light — not a shimmer and not a pulse, just on — bright enough to read a face by and not much more.

In a forest adapted to near-total darkness, it is the one thing that volunteers to be seen.

---

## Growth

Middle canopy, on the shaded side of a trunk, in tiers that get dimmer toward the bottom of the plant. A shrub is a soft green glow through the leaf litter from thirty feet out and invisible from forty.

The light does not go out when the leaf is picked. **It does not go out for days**, and there is no way anybody has found to make it stop — the leaves are translucent, so wrapping them dims a harvest without hiding it, and a full pack reads as a lantern somebody has thrown a coat over.

---

## Harvesting

**Picking is trivial. Carrying it out is the gate.**

There is no patience test, no window, no skill. You find the shrub and strip the tiers. The cost arrives afterward, and it scales exactly with greed: **every leaf a party takes makes the walk out brighter.** A handful is a nuisance. A pack full is a moving light in a forest where the Rootstalker hunts by preference in the dark and the Pressure Track is already counting (`quests/turnroot-weald-adventure.md`).

So the decision is a real one and it belongs to the players rather than to a roll — how much is worth being visible for, on a route that will not be the same route on the way back.

**And it will not be the same route.** Paths in the Weald do not lead to the same place twice, and naming the stand out loud to find it again makes the routing worse rather than better (`places/turnroot-weald.md`). Every luminova harvest is a final harvest of that particular shrub. Forest families do not plan around individual stands and are baffled by outsiders who try.

Dried, the leaves finally go dark, which is also when they become worth money.

---

## Use

Dried and crushed to a fine powder, luminova enhances healing in potions and in food — the Weald's other export reagent alongside black thorn seed, and the gentler of the two to obtain.

Fresh, it is light. Forest families use a few leaves in a cord-wrist bundle for close work and consider a carried armful the mark of somebody who does not live here. They are not wrong, and the Rootstalkers agree with them.
