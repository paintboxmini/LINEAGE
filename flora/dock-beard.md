# Dock Beard

*Boiled down it stops being a plant and becomes* greenpitch*, which is what it is called once anybody is paying for it.*

A coarse dark green growth that takes hold in wet standing timber below the splash line — pilings, dock legs, the underside of a hull that has not been hauled out in too long. It looks like matted hair and comes off in sheets.

It is a rot problem. Every maintenance crew in the Nest scrapes it, and every crew scraping it is also harvesting, because **boiled with pitch it makes the seal that keeps the boats dry.** The thing eating the docks is the thing that waterproofs the fleet, and the town has been running that loop so long that nobody finds it remarkable.

---

## Growth

Anywhere timber stands in water and stays there. It does not grow on stone, it does not grow on anything that comes out of the water regularly, and it will not establish on a surface that has been scraped inside the last year or so.

**The first year's growth is worthless.** Young beard boils down to a pitch that never quite sets — tacky in the sun, brittle in the cold, and a hull sealed with it leaks by the second season. It wants three years or more on the timber before it is worth the fuel to cook it.

---

## Harvesting

**The gate is neglect, and that makes taking it a question about somebody else.** The Compact's crews scrape on a schedule precisely to stop beard establishing, so every patch old enough to be worth cooking is a patch where somebody has failed to do their job — a berth nobody is renting, a stretch of dock that lost its argument about whose repair it was, a boat whose owner has stopped coming down to it.

Finding good beard is easy. Finding out whose neglect you are standing under is the work. Scraping a lapsed piling is a favour, a theft, or an opening move depending entirely on the answer, and in a town where the council takes a percentage of everything, it is never nothing.

Scraped sheets keep about a week wet and indefinitely dried. Dried beard has to be re-wetted before cooking and everyone agrees it makes a slightly worse pitch, without anyone being able to say why.

---

## Use

Greenpitch: hull seams, deck seams, the joins in a stilt house, anywhere the town would rather water did not go. It is cheap, it is everywhere, and the Nest sells it up and down the web to ports that have no beard of their own and no idea how to make it.

Raw beard is not food and does not become food. A stretch of it in a cook pot ruins the pot.
