# Hardrun

A ribbon weed anchored to rock and old stonework in the channel, growing in lengths of two or three feet and streaming downstream in the current. Named for the water it wants rather than for itself, which is how most things on this river get named.

**It is only a material while it is under tension.** On a fast tide the ribbons pull taut and the fibre in them locks — cut one then and you have a length of cord that holds its shape, takes a knot, and does not stretch. On a slow tide the same ribbon goes slack and soft, and cutting it gets you a handful of wet green nothing. The plant has not changed. The water has.

---

## Growth

Channel rock, bridge footings, the submerged courses of whatever the town was built on last time. It needs hard anchorage and moving water and it does not grow in the lakes at all.

The best stands are on stretches that run fast most of the year, which in the Nest's web means the narrows and the outside of bends — the places a boat is already working hardest to get through.

---

## Harvesting

**You cut it in exactly the conditions nobody wants to be on the water in.** The window is not rare, the way the deep slow tide is; fast tide comes round like clockwork and everyone knows when. The problem is that a fast-tide narrows is where the current is worst, the footing is underwater, and the boat you are working from is the only thing between you and a long swim.

So it is cut from a boat, by people who are good with a boat, and it is the one harvest in the region where **the skill being tested is the crew's rather than the forager's.** A brilliant naturalist with no river hands comes back empty. Two competent rowers and somebody with a knife come back loaded.

Cut ribbons keep their tension for about a day if they are kept wet and coiled. Let them dry slack and they set slack, and the cord is ruined before it is made.

---

## Use

Line. Most of the working cordage in the Nest starts as hardrun — light line, lashing, net twine, the miles of it that hold a layered dock together. It is not as strong as silk rope (`items/turnroot-weald-items.md`) and it costs a fraction as much, and a town where children learn knots before letters goes through an enormous amount of it.

It is also the reason a Nest crew will say a stretch of river is *good* when they mean it is dangerous. They are not being ironic.
