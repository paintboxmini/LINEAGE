# Daycress

A pale, crisp green that grows in trailing curtains off anything holding still in moving water — mooring chains, the upstream face of a piling, the rope of a fish trap. Small round leaves, a clean peppery bite, and it is the only thing within a day of Vulture's Nest that anybody would call a vegetable.

**It has hours.** Out of moving water it goes from crisp to grey to slime, and the whole arc takes most of a morning. Kept in a bucket it is worse, not better; still water kills it faster than air does. There is no drying it, no salting it, no pickling it — every one of those has been tried by generations of people with an obvious commercial motive, and the result is always the same grey nothing.

---

## Growth

Wherever there is current and something fixed to grip. It does not care whether the tide is fast or slow so long as the water is going somewhere, and it grows thickest on the working waterfront, where there is more fixed gear in the water than anywhere else in the region.

It is not rare, it is not seasonal, and nobody owns it. A child can fill an apron in ten minutes off the underside of a dock.

---

## Harvesting

**There is no gate on getting it. The gate is that it cannot travel.**

This is the only crop in the region where the difficulty is entirely on the far side of the harvest. Cutting is trivial. Getting it anywhere is impossible — and the Nest is a port whose entire reason for existing is that things move through it, where more cargo crosses the docks than anywhere on the continent. Daycress is the one good in town that has never once left.

So it is eaten where it is pulled, within the morning, by people who happen to be standing there. The market has it at dawn and does not have it by noon. It costs almost nothing and it is served at every table in the Nest that has anything to celebrate, because you cannot buy it in advance, you cannot save it, and having it on the table means somebody went and got it this morning for you.

*The name is optimism rather than measurement. It has never had a day.*

---

## Culinary Note

Raw, always. It wilts to nothing on heat and the bite goes with it. Over fish, under fat, in a broth at the moment the bowl leaves the stove.

**It is the counterweight to the rest of the town's food.** Salted strip, dock broth, chewfat — preserved, portable, endlessly shelf-stable (`items/vultures-nest-items.md`). Daycress is the opposite of all of it, and the Nest values it in exactly the proportion that it cannot be stored.

For a cook working a grinder full of shelf-stable charges, it is the ingredient that refuses to load (`campaign/kevin.md`).
