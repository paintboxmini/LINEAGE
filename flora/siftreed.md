# Siftreed

A tall grey-green rush growing in standing shallows on soft ground, in stands dense enough to hide a boat. Ordinary from the waterline up. The stalks make thatch, matting and rough basket, and nobody has ever paid much for any of that.

**The value is underneath.** Siftreed puts out a wide flat root mat rather than a taproot, and the mat grows *through* whatever is in the mud instead of around it. Pull a mature clump and it comes up holding things — coins, fittings, buckles, bone, the odd sealed jar, whatever the river put there before the reeds arrived.

---

## Growth

It only takes on **long-settled deposit** — mud the river has been quietly adding to for decades. New silt is too loose, scoured bank is too hard, and a bar that has moved in living memory will not hold a stand.

Which means a good siftreed stand is a marker. It says this ground has been collecting since before anybody watching was born, and the town already has two other things that say the same thing about the same mud and has never put them together: the wrecks and foundations the deep slow tide uncovers, and the lights that are sitting on those sites before the tide exposes them (`bestiary/spooklight.md`).

---

## Harvesting

**The gate is the neighbours.** Foulhauls live on exactly this ground, for exactly this reason — a river that has been depositing for fifty years deposits bodies too, and a family works the bank collecting (`bestiary/foulhaul.md`). A dense old siftreed stand and a Foulhaul family are two readings of the same fact.

So a harvest here does not start with cutting. It starts about a minute before anything else happens, with a smell on the wind, and the content of the encounter is what the party does with that minute. Move the boat off. Get up something. Decide to work a different stand. Foulhauls are not hunting anyone healthy and never have been, and a crew that understands that can sometimes simply work the far end of a bank while a family works the near one — which is a real option and should be available at the table.

Pulling is heavy, wet, two-person work, and the mat has to come up whole. Cut the stalks and you have thatch. **Only the pull gets you anything.**

---

## Use

Thatch and matting from the stalks, at thatch and matting prices.

Everything else depends entirely on what came up, which makes siftreed less a crop than a slow lottery run on somebody else's history. A stand that produces nothing but river gravel three pulls running is normal. The fourth is why people keep pulling.

**The Nest has opinions about that.** Ground this old holds the town's own previous versions, and a buckle out of a reed mat came off somebody. Most crews sell without asking. Some don't, and the ones that don't are usually the ones who have pulled up something with a name on it.
