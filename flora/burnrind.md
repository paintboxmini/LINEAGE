# Burnrind

*The orange the incendiary orange is actually made of (`campaign/kevin.md`). Until this file existed, the grenade was the only thing in Kevin's whole kit with no ingredient behind it.*

A low, thorny citrus on the spoil banks around the Nest's islands. Knee-high to waist-high, grey-barked, more spine than leaf. It fruits in hard little oranges about the size of a plum, and there is almost nothing inside one — a dry pith, a few seeds, no juice worth the name.

**The whole plant is the rind.** Thick, waxy, and loaded with an oil so heavy it beads on a knife. Squeeze a fresh peel near a candle and the spray catches in the air, which is the trick every dock child in the Nest knows and every dock parent has views about.

---

## Growth

**Another stowaway, same as the pepper.** Ballast comes out of a hold carrying the seed of wherever it was shovelled up, and the banks around the islands are two centuries of somewhere else in layers (`places/vultures-nest.md`, What Grows Here). Burnrind came off a boat from a place nobody has bothered to identify and took to gravel, ash and other people's rubbish the way nothing native ever could.

**It shares the banks with Nobody's Pepper** (`flora/nobodys-pepper.md`) and loses to it everywhere the ground is settled. Where it wins is ground that has just been ruined.

**Because it is fire-following, and that is the whole organism.** A spoil bank is not inert — it is ballast, coal dust, oil-soaked dunnage and a century of tipped rubbish, and it smoulders underneath for years before a stretch finally goes up. Burnrind survives that. More than survives it: a bush that has burned through fruits heavily the following season, heavy and oily and low to the ground, and then spends the next several years making almost nothing at all.

**A bank that has not burned gives you a thorn bush with four bad oranges on it.** A bank that burned last year gives you a crop.

---

## Harvesting

**The gate is that somebody else's disaster has to have already happened.**

Picking is nothing. It is a thorny bush at waist height on open ground and the fruit comes away in the hand. There is no check, no window in the day, no rot clock, and nobody to ask — the banks are nobody's and the fruit is worth nothing to anyone who is not doing what Kevin does with it.

What you cannot do is arrange it. **You need a bank fire, and a bank fire is the thing the Nest is most afraid of**, because the banks are what the islands are standing on and a fire in one is a fire under the town. Setting one is not a clever plan, it is the fastest way to be hanged off a piling by people who will feel they were unusually patient about it.

So the supply line runs entirely through other people's bad luck, and it runs through *knowing*: which stretch went up, how long ago, and whether it burned deep or just took the top off. That is dock talk, ferryman talk, and Foulhaul talk — the people who live on the banks are the people who watch them burn (`flora/siftreed.md`, and the Foulhaul families on the settled deposit).

**Which makes restocking a question asked of a person rather than a problem solved in a field.** For a character whose other four seasonings all have to be taken off something that did not want to give them up, this one is the opposite shape and is meant to be: it is free, it is legal, it is lying on the ground, and you still cannot have any unless you went and found out where.

*A bank that burned this season is not ready. A bank that burned four years ago is finished. The useful answer is a stretch that burned once, last year, and the number of those at any given moment is small and known.*

---

## Culinary Note

**Inedible, and not in an interesting way.** There is no flesh to eat and the rind is bitter enough to be a punishment. It does not break the rule that nothing within a day of the Nest is worth making a meal out of (`places/vultures-nest.md`) — it confirms it. This is a fruit that the most food-deprived town on the river has never once tried to cook.

**What it is for is the oil.** Reduced and sealed the way Kevin reduces and seals everything, the rind stops being a rind and becomes the charge and the casing at the same time — which is why the incendiary orange is the one thing he makes where the rare ingredient and the body of the thing are the same object. Every other round in his kit is ordinary produce with something expensive worked into it. The orange is the expensive thing, wrapped in itself.

**One picking makes two oranges**, and that is the whole ceiling (`campaign/kevin.md`, Supply and restocking).

*The bank children who squeeze peels at candles are doing the entire craft in miniature and getting told off for it. Kevin did the same thing at the other end of the continent with a different fruit and nobody told him off, and that is most of the difference between the two of them.*
