# Name / Price / Distance

A creation tool for building **people, places, and things**.

The Three Cuts are one relationship, not three independent prompts:

> **Name is what you are. Price is how you perform that function. Distance is what separates your function from others' function. The relationship between them is the thing you are.**

Use the three together. Do not treat them as a checklist whose answers can be designed independently.

## Name — What Is This?

Give the subject enough identity that it is no longer interchangeable with another instance of its category.

Ask:

- What is it? What is its essential nature, role, or function?
- What is its actual name?
- What traits make it recognizably itself?
- What history made it this way?

Apply at the subject's scale:

- **Person:** nature or role, name, traits, history.
- **Place:** essential nature, name, distinctive traits, history.
- **Thing:** what it is or does, name, distinctive traits or capabilities, origin and prior use.

This is a fidelity test, not a closed formula. Leave details unspecified when the evidence does not support them.

## Price — How Does It Perform Its Function?

Price is the meaningful cost of acting, expressing the subject's nature, or forcing that nature into the world. The Price should correspond to what was done.

Ask:

- What does this subject have to pay to express what it is?
- Why does that Price fit this particular function rather than being a generic tax?
- How does greater magnitude produce greater Price?
- Where does the consequence land, and why does that placement make sense?

Price is not required to be HP loss or a single mechanical form. It may be a constraint, pressure, collateral consequence, degradation, social consequence, binding consequence, or another expression that fits the act.

For people, Price may appear as a binding constraint. For places, it may appear as pressure or accumulated debt. For things, it should emerge from the specific use of the thing.

Understanding a Price does not automatically grant control over it.

## Distance — What Separates This Function From Others?

Distance defines the subject's relationships and boundaries: what it cannot reach, what is close enough to influence or resonate with it, and what distinctions can collapse.

Ask:

- What can this subject never reach or become?
- What people, things, places, states, or domains are close enough to influence or resonate with it?
- What distinction defines it against something else?
- What would it mean for that distinction to collapse?

Distance is not merely a permanent prohibition. A story may cross or collapse a Distance when the fiction earns that transformation. Do not invent a universal definition of "close" or "no distinction" where the world has not established one.

### What Can Never Be

Every substantial person, place, or thing should carry a meaningful boundary appropriate to its scale: a state of being reality will not simply let it inhabit.

- **Person:** a relationship, life-state, or version of self that cannot be attained.
- **Place:** a domain or kind of place it can never fully become.
- **Thing:** a state of being or function it can never inhabit.

The point is not tragedy for its own sake. The boundary should reveal something about what the subject is.

## Final Test

Read Name, Price, and Distance together.

If they describe three unrelated ideas, the subject is not finished.

The **Name** should establish the function. The **Price** should reveal what expressing that function costs. The **Distance** should define the relationships and boundaries that make that function distinct.

The three together should make the subject feel like **one thing**.
