# Inspiration Guide

Use this when the first version of something feels obvious, flat, or like something that already exists somewhere else. The goal is to find the angle that's true to this world rather than borrowed from a generic one.

Read `world/tonal-bible.md` first if you haven't.

---

## The Core Questions

Ask these before committing to a direction. You don't need to answer all of them — one good answer is enough to find the angle.

**What is this thing's own logic, independent of what the party will do with it?**
A creature, location, or NPC that exists only in relation to the party is set dressing. Give it a reason to be here that has nothing to do with them.

**What was here before? What will still be here after?**
The party is passing through. The world isn't. What does this place or creature look like on the day nobody shows up?

**What does this understand that nobody else does?**
Every interesting NPC has seen something that changed the shape of the world for them. Every interesting location is the site of something that happened. What is it?

**What's the beautiful version of this? What's the horrifying version? Are they the same thing?**
If they're not even close, the content probably isn't deep enough yet.

**What rule does this follow so consistently it becomes its own kind of law?**
Hellraiser logic. Hastur logic. The Cenobites aren't scary because they're random — they're scary because they're not. What's the system underneath?

**If a child who grew up here described this, what would they call it?**
Children in this world use mythic logic. Their vocabulary for strangeness is often more accurate than the adult rationalist framing.

**What does this want on its own terms?**
Not what the party wants from it. Not what the story needs it to do. What does *it* want?

## When You're Stuck on a Location

- Start from what the place was before people arrived. The human layer came second.
- Find the one thing that doesn't fit the rest. That's usually the truth of the place.
- Ask what the locals have stopped noticing. Familiarity is its own kind of horror.
- What's the cost of being here? Not combat cost — what does the place take from you over time?

---

## When You're Stuck on a Creature

- Give it an ecology before you give it a combat role. What does it eat? Where does it sleep? What avoids it?
- Find the version that's beautiful. Then find the version that's wrong. Build toward the overlap.
- What does it do when there's nobody to fight? That behavior is usually more interesting than the attack pattern.
- What would Delicious in Dungeon do with it? If it could be ingredients, what would it make?

---

## When You're Stuck on an NPC

- What have they seen that the party hasn't? That's their edge.
- What have they decided to stop thinking about? That's their flaw.
- What do they do when nobody is watching? That's their truth.
- Are they warm and wrong, or cold and right? Both are interesting. Neither is safe.

---

## The Last Check

Before finalizing anything: does it feel like it belongs to *this* world, or could it exist in any TTRPG?

If it could exist anywhere, it needs one more pass. Find the thing that makes it only possible here — in the Unheld, under this sky, with these rules about how the world works.

That thing is usually already in the content somewhere. It just needs to be moved to the front.
