# Skeinwing

*High-altitude glider. Almost never encountered alive. What the Tollbird's larder sometimes holds instead of a young jackalope.*

**Cards:** `cards/entwined.md`, `cards/from-above.md`, `cards/too-high-to-hear.md`

**Mind 2 / Body 1 / Soul 4 — HP 9**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 7


**Deck (7 — 2 Blue / 1 Red / 4 Green):** DEFLECT, TOO HIGH TO HEAR *(blue)* · FROM ABOVE *(red)* · SYNCHRONY, UNTOUCHED, COMMUNION, ENTWINED *(green)*

---

## The Invariant

The sky is home. The ground is danger. Everything else about this creature follows from that one sentence.

---

## Appearance

A wingspan wider than a grown man is tall, membrane stretched thin between long finger-bones, no fur, no fat — nothing built for cold, because it was never built to need it. The tail is long, almost disproportionate, more limb than afterthought. The eyes don't work, or work so little it doesn't matter; the whole creature is built around a voice too high to hear and an ear built to catch its echo.

Its color reads its metabolism before anything else does. At rest, drifting, it runs a deep, cold blue — slow-blooded, slow-hearted, barely burning. Active and hunting, low and warm, the blue shallows toward something closer to grey. A Skeinwing that's flushed pink or red isn't excited. It's dying.

---

## Behavior

It eats insects, in bulk, on the wing, at altitude — a sky's worth of small things caught a thousand at a time rather than one large thing caught once. This is the whole strategy: never spend more of yourself than you have to, never come down further than the hunting requires, and the hunting rarely requires much, because the sky is thick with food if you're built to sift it.

Its metabolism runs slow almost all the time — not asleep, not quite, but close enough that a stranger watching one drift for a day might call it dreaming. It doesn't stop being social in this state. Skeinwings pair by entwining tails mid-air and locking there, gliding for days on the same current, doing nothing that looks like courtship and everything that is. The lock keeps them from drifting apart on the wind. Nobody has ever seen one end deliberately.

It almost never descends below the treeline. Not caution — arithmetic. Every adaptation it has is spent on staying up, staying slow, staying safe, and every one of those adaptations becomes a liability the instant it's low enough for something with teeth to reach it. It doesn't come down to hunt, doesn't come down to breed, doesn't come down for much of anything. When one does come down, it's because something went wrong — a fog bank at the wrong altitude, a storm it couldn't out-climb, an old injury, a swarm chased a little too far, a little too low.

---

## Why It Dies, When It Dies

It does not die because it is weak. Up where it lives, nothing touches it — nothing else is built to be there, and it is built for nothing else. It dies because everything that makes it extraordinary at altitude means nothing on the ground. Blind, it never needed sight. Fragile, it never needed to survive a hit. Slow to react to a lunge, it never needed to — nothing lunges up where it flies. A creature this specialized doesn't have a weak point down here. It doesn't have a here at all. That's the whole tragedy in one sentence: it isn't out of its depth. It's out of its entire premise.

The Tollbird (`bestiary/tollbird.md`) doesn't hunt these. It hunts the fence line, the road, the low and the slow and the small. A Skeinwing in the larder isn't a kill. It's a mistake that happened to end near a fence.

---

## The Larder, Once

Most days it's what it always is: something small and rust-furred, a young jackalope, something with a collar. Then, once — hanging where nothing should hang — a wingspan too wide for the wire, a membrane too blue to be anything born on the ground, a tail wound once around a barb like it tried to hold on to something and there was nothing there to hold. Nobody at that fence has ever seen one alive. Whatever the party makes of finding one dead is the whole point.

---

## Tactical Purpose

Primarily a discovery, not an encounter — the answer to `places/briarwatch.md`'s "one thing that should not be on a fence at Briarwatch's edge" (The Larder Fence). Its stat block exists for completeness and for the rare table that wants to run it live (an injured Skeinwing found grounded, dying, maybe savable) — not for a fair fight. Creature Threat Rating 7 and HP 9 reflect what it *would* be if it had to defend itself, which is almost never what actually happens to one.
