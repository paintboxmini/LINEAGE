# Hush Bloom

*Flora rather than fauna, filed here with the other ingredient organisms.*

A pale flower of the deep canopy shade in the Turnroot Weald (`places/turnroot-weald.md`), the same general territory the Flower Snake works. Five petals, no scent worth naming, and a habit that makes it far harder to gather than it looks: **it only opens in genuine stillness, and closes at the first vibration.**

Not a defense mechanism against anything in particular. The Weald is loud and busy and the Hush Bloom has simply declined to participate.

---

## Growth

Shade, damp, and undisturbed ground — the further from a game trail the better. It grows in loose scatters rather than stands, which means a patch is a walk, not a harvest.

A closed bloom is worthless. The petals hold the compound and a closed flower has drawn it back down into the root, where it is diffuse enough that processing it is not worth the fuel.

---

## Harvesting

**The stillness is the gate, and it is a patience gate rather than a danger one.** You find a patch, you stop, and you wait for it to forget you are there — long enough that footfall, breathing and shifting weight have all stopped registering. Then you cut without transmitting anything down the stem.

There is no way to hurry it and no tool that solves it. Two people working a patch together do worse than one working alone. It is the exact opposite problem to the Scorchback Beetle (`bestiary/scorchback-beetle.md`), which is why a cook who can reliably produce both is demonstrating something about themselves.

Dried, the petals keep indefinitely.

---

## Culinary Note

Calm, faintly numbing-sweet, and it throws a soft green luminescence in liquid — which is why it belongs in a drink more naturally than on a plate. The numbing is mild in food and considerably less mild concentrated.

It is one of the charges in Kevin's grinder, and the base of his better beverages: `experimental/kevin.md`.
