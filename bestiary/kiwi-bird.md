# Kiwi Bird

*Small flightless forager, Turnroot Weald undergrowth. Famous — among the handful of people who've ever actually caught one — for flesh that tastes and tears like fruit, not meat. Not a threat. A skill problem.*

**Cards:** `cards/peck.md`, `cards/rustle-and-gone.md`, `cards/still-as-litter.md`, `cards/underbrush-dash.md`

**Mind 1 / Body 1 / Soul 2 — HP 6**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 4

**Deck (4 — 1 Blue / 1 Red / 2 Green):** RUSTLE AND GONE *(blue)* · PECK *(red)* · UNDERBRUSH DASH, STILL AS LITTER *(green)*

---

## Description

Round-bodied, long thin beak, feathers more like fine hair than plumage — flightless, low to the ground, built for pushing through root-tangle rather than open air. Forages at night, root-first, sweeping the beak side to side through leaf litter for grubs and fallen fruit.

Nobody's fully explained the flesh. Cut into it and it doesn't behave like a bird — pale, faintly sweet, texture closer to a ripe fruit's than a muscle's. Senshi has opinions about why (something about a diet of nothing but fallen fruit for generations) and no actual proof.

---

## Behavior

Doesn't fight. Freezes first, hoping stillness reads as ground litter — it's patterned for exactly that — and runs for the nearest root-tangle the moment it decides it's been seen. It's not fast. It's just very good at disappearing into terrain built to swallow motion.

The difficulty here was never combat. It's noticing one before it notices you, and then not losing it in the tangle once it runs.

---

## Encounter Setup

Never a fight in the normal sense. A DC 13 Body/Senses check to spot one before it freezes; if missed, it's already gone. Once spotted, catching it plays out as a short chase through underbrush — the bird plays STILL AS LITTER and UNDERBRUSH DASH to buy distance, never PECK unless it's cornered with nowhere left to run.

---

## What Players Learn

| Mechanic | How they learn |
|----------|-----------------|
| Evade as a genuine obstacle, not a coin flip to shrug off | Most of this bird's kit is about not being hit at all, the first time a target actively wants to not be caught rather than not be killed |
| A fight that was never a fight | The correct read is patience and positioning, not damage — PECK existing on the card list at all is almost a formality |

---

## Loot

A clean catch (no PECK ever played, nothing else in the fight) yields the whole bird intact — what Senshi actually wants. A messier catch still yields something, just not enough for the dish he has in mind.
