# Driftfire

**Not a combat encounter. A stretch of water you cannot use.**

*Named 2026-09-25. It drifts, it burns, and at distance it looks like fire on the water — the name does all three jobs and every one of them is what it does to you rather than what it is.*

---

Small, translucent, roughly the size of a thumbnail, and never once seen alone. A Driftfire has no way of going anywhere: no fins, no tail, nothing that pushes. **The current is its entire means of travel**, and that is the whole animal.

**They arrive in the thousands.** A bloom is not a group of them, it is a condition the water is in — a stretch of river gone faintly cloudy in daylight, thick enough in places that a dipped oar comes up coated. **Nobody fights a bloom.** There is nothing to hit, nothing that can be driven off, and no number you could kill that would matter.

## It Goes Round

**The web is a circuit, not a drainage. It goes round forever, and every drop in it has been past every place on it** (`world/geography-overview.md`). *So has the bloom.*

**This is the thing that makes Driftfire different from every other bad day on the river.** A flood passes. A storm passes. A bloom **travels the loop and comes back** — south along the eastern reach, west across the southern stretch, north again, and round. It does not disperse and it does not end. It arrives somewhere else and becomes their problem, and in time it is yours again.

**Nobody can prove it is the same bloom**, and every old hand on the water will tell you it is. The timing is the reason they think so: it is not regular enough to be a calendar and not random enough to stop feeling like one.

## The Tide Decides How Long

**A bloom cannot move itself, so it does exactly what the water does** — and on this web what changes is speed, not depth (`world/geography-overview.md`, Fast tide and slow tide).

- **Fast tide** and a bloom is through in hours. A bad day, and then it is gone and it is somewhere downstream.
- **Slow tide** and it *sits.* Sluggish heavy water holds it in place, and a bloom parked on a slow stretch is there for as long as that stretch stays slow.

**Which makes it predictable, and by a skill the water already has.** Reading which tide a stretch is on and when it will turn is its own discipline (`world/geography-overview.md`) — and a sailor who has that discipline can tell you roughly how long a bloom is going to sit on them. **Nobody needs a new kind of knowledge to handle Driftfire. They need the knowledge they already brag about.**

*And the two ends of the web are always on opposite tides.* A bloom stalled near the coast means the inner web is running hard, which is worth knowing to anybody deciding whether to wait it out or go the long way round.

## What It Costs

**It burns.** Not a wound and not poison in the sense of something that goes on working — a searing that arrives on contact and gets worse for a while before it fades. **Anyone in the water is in serious trouble. Anyone in a boat is inconvenienced**, and the whole character of the hazard lives in that gap.

**The boat problem is the oars and the lines.** You can row through a bloom exactly once before the crew's hands are the problem, and a pullway line dragged up out of coated water has to be handled by somebody. *Most of what arrives at the Nest was rowed or hauled rather than sailed* (`places/vultures-nest.md`), which means a bloom lands hardest on precisely the traffic this web runs on.

**So the real cost is time, and the decision is a trade.** Wait it out, go the long way against the grain, or push through and pay in a crew that cannot work tomorrow. *None of those is a fight and all three are a scene.*

## At Night

**By nightfall the world is pitch black** (`world/eclipseria-overview.md`). And a bloom does not stop glowing when the light goes.

**A mile of river lit from inside**, cold and shifting and bright enough to read by at the bank. It is the most beautiful thing most people on this water will ever see, and it means their week is ruined, **and those are not two facts about it.** People come down to the bank to look. They look while they are working out what it is going to cost them.
