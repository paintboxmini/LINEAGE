# Stiltwood

*Flora rather than fauna, filed here with the other ingredient organisms.*

The tree the region floats on. Stiltwood grows on island margins all through the Nest's web of rivers and lakes, standing on a cage of arched roots with its trunk held clear of the water — the same trick the town itself is doing, which is where the name comes from and probably where the idea came from too.

**Only the lowest third of the trunk is worth anything.** The wood below the old waterline is dense, dark, and does not rot. Above it the same tree is pale, light, and unremarkable — fine for crates and lamp posts, useless for a hull. A grown stiltwood is mostly firewood with a boat hidden in the bottom of it.

---

## Growth

Island margins, brackish or fresh, anywhere the bank is soft enough for the roots to get a grip and the water is shallow enough to stand in. It does not grow inland and it does not grow where the current runs hard all year — fast-tide stretches strip the root cage before it sets.

Slow. A trunk wide enough to plank a small craft has been standing about forty years, which is why stiltwood stands are treated as property on islands that agree about very little else, and why a stand nobody has claimed is worth asking hard questions about.

---

## Harvesting

**The good wood is underwater, and the deep slow tide is the only way to it.** On the long, low slow tide the whole town watches the banks for (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Tide), the water drops far enough to put the root cage and the dense lower trunk in the open air. Cutting crews work those hours and only those hours. Everyone knows the window is coming, everyone is trying to use it, and a crew that misses it waits for the next one.

Cut above the line and the buyer can tell instantly — the colour is wrong and it floats too high. Cut below and the stump is underwater again by evening, which is also how you hide having cut somebody else's tree.

Green stiltwood has to be worked wet. Let it dry before it is shaped and it goes hard enough to blunt tools and stays that way.

---

## Use

Hulls, pilings, dock timber, oar looms. Almost every small craft on the southeastern web is stiltwood below the waterline and something cheaper above it, on the same logic the tree uses.

It is not a trade good the Nest exports so much as the material the Nest is made of. Nobody sells stiltwood to a stranger without asking what they want it for.
