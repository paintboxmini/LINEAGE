# Weeping Wolf

*The grown Ocellus. Who it is, what it eats and why the trade runs on its pups: `bestiary/ocellus.md`, The Grown Ocellus. This file is the fight.*

**Lives in the Dark Garden**, the second layer of the Abyssal Ruins (`places/abyssal-ruins.md`, The Descent).

**Cards:** `cards/weeping-wolf.md`

**A mini-boss** *(Drew, 2026-10-02)* — one wolf is an encounter on its own.

**Mind 4 / Body 5 / Soul 4 — HP 28**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 13 *(and it takes two Actions a turn — read it as well above that)*

**Deck (13 — 4 Blue / 5 Red / 4 Green):** OUT OF MIND, PETRIFYING GAZE, MARKED, FOCUS *(blue)* · LAST THING YOU SEE, MAUL, BLINDSIDE, GORE, SKEWER *(red)* · WEEPING VEIL, SHADE AWAY, INSTINCT, QUICKEN *(green)*

**Hand size 4.** Its own deck, not a blank deck — it is a boss.

---

## Traits

**Trait — Never Still:** **the weeping wolf takes two Actions each turn** *(Drew, 2026-10-02)* — one draw and one free action as normal, and the Action twice. *Two attacks back to back, or an attack and a move, or anything else an Action can be.*

**Trait — A Hundred Eyes:** **the weeping wolf cannot gain Blind.** *There is no covering that many eyes.*

**And it starts unseen.** A weeping wolf is never noticed until it is close (`bestiary/ocellus.md`, The Grown Ocellus) — **the first exchange of the fight is its attack, undefended**, unless somebody had a real reason to be watching for it. **In the Dark Garden it opens with a tearseed** — knocked into the party, or the party driven into a cluster — and the attack comes out of the flash, which Blinds them and not it (`flora/tearseed.md`). **The party can see it coming**: a seed moving against the drift, with leaves snapping shut along its path, is the wolf.

---

## How It Fights

**It takes the party apart one sense at a time.** WEEPING VEIL whips the tears across a whole position and Blinds everyone in it. OUT OF MIND makes a character lose track of it altogether. PETRIFYING GAZE holds one of them still for a moment that costs them their next attack or defence. **Then the red cards land on whoever is left looking at it.**

**Two Actions a turn is the danger, not the damage.** Its die and its stats are a strong creature's, not a monster's. What makes it a mini-boss is that it gets twice as many chances to stack those effects, and **a character who is Blind, has lost track of it and is Staggered is a character it gets to eat.**

**How to beat it:**
- **Ward** stops the next Blind, Staggered or Rooted before it lands (`rules/card-glossary.md`, Ward) — the party's best answer to the whole kit, if it is up before the wolf moves
- **Spread out.** WEEPING VEIL only hits one position
- **Watch for it before it arrives.** The undefended opening is the most dangerous single exchange, and the only one the party can stop by preparing
- **Delay its turn.** Its two Actions come together, so pushing it later in the order gives the party more of their own turns before both land

## How Hard It Is

*Measured 2026-10-02 against Chris, Kevin and Pat, fighting to the end, with everything on this sheet running in the simulator except the undefended opener.* **Against one wolf the party wins about 85% of the time, takes about one Down a fight, and deaths are common — roughly one for every two fights.** Two wolves at once is a likely loss (16%). **That is a mini-boss: beatable, and it costs.** *The undefended opener makes it harder still* — which is exactly what **watching for it, spreading out and getting Ward up** take back.

## Loot

**Whatever the party harvests from the body** *(Drew, 2026-10-02)*. There is no drop table: the players say what they take and how, and the table works out what it is. *A wolf's eyes are a pup's grown large, and a pup's eyes are the part the smugglers kill for* (`bestiary/ocellus.md`, When a pup is done) — so somebody at the table will probably think of them first.
