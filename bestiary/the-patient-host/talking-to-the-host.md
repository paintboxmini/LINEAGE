# The Patient Host — Talking to the Host

No checks for ordinary conversation — the Host is not hiding anything, and it isn't trying to unsettle anyone on purpose. Play these straight. The horror is in what's true, not in what it's tricking you into believing.

**"What are you?"** — "I keep the Milestone. I've always kept the Milestone." Said the way a person states their own name — not defensive, not proud, just correct.

**"Can I see the ledger?"** — Yes, without hesitation. It's shown freely because there's nothing in it to hide. A player who reads it finds their own arrival, timestamped exactly, spelled correctly, filed in order with everyone else's. Ordinary. Unless they go looking further than that.

**A player gives it a name it doesn't recognize** — the one beat of genuine hesitation in the whole encounter. It pauses — not long — then writes it down. *"That's new."* Nothing else. It updates. It doesn't apologize for not already knowing, and it doesn't ask who the name belongs to. This matters: it's a process, not an oracle. It can be surprised. It just doesn't stay surprised.

**"Who were you, before?"** — It doesn't deflect, because deflecting would require knowing there's something to hide. "I don't recall not doing this." That's the whole answer, offered without any sense of loss, because there's nothing left in it to feel the loss.

**Threats, intimidation, raised voices** — no fear response, no anger either. *"You'll need to wait your turn for that as well."* It treats hostility as one more thing to be queued, not a reason to change how it's standing.

**Reading ahead to an entry that hasn't been written yet, or tearing a page out** — the one real transgression, and the Host notices it instantly; there's no check for whether it catches you, only for what you walk away with. **2d10 + Soul vs DC 13** (Read — understanding fast enough to control what you're looking at before the entry closes back over). **Success:** the player learns one true thing before it does (GM's call, tied to the scene — a real fact, not a vague omen). **Failure:** the Host applies **Locked** (`rules/card-glossary.md`) to one card in the player's hand or deck — but this one is explicitly exempt from the ordinary end-of-combat expiration. It doesn't lift when the fight does. Whatever unlocks it has to come from the same kind of source that locked it — which here means the ledger itself, not a short rest or the fight simply ending.
