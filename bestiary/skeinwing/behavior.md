# Skeinwing — Behavior

It eats insects, in bulk, on the wing, at altitude — a sky's worth of small things caught a thousand at a time rather than one large thing caught once. This is the whole strategy: never spend more of yourself than you have to, never come down further than the hunting requires, and the hunting rarely requires much, because the sky is thick with food if you're built to sift it.

Its metabolism runs slow almost all the time — not asleep, not quite, but close enough that a stranger watching one drift for a day might call it dreaming. It doesn't stop being social in this state. Skeinwings pair by entwining tails mid-air and locking there, gliding for days on the same current, doing nothing that looks like courtship and everything that is. The lock keeps them from drifting apart on the wind. Nobody has ever seen one end deliberately.

It almost never descends below the treeline. Not caution — arithmetic. Every adaptation it has is spent on staying up, staying slow, staying safe, and every one of those adaptations becomes a liability the instant it's low enough for something with teeth to reach it. It doesn't come down to hunt, doesn't come down to breed, doesn't come down for much of anything. When one does come down, it's because something went wrong — a fog bank at the wrong altitude, a storm it couldn't out-climb, an old injury, a swarm chased a little too far, a little too low.
