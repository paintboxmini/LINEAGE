# Spooklight

*Lights on the water and the banks around Vulture's Nest, at night. Mostly nothing. Occasionally salvage. Rarely a problem.*

**A spooklight is not a species.** It is a thing people see, and most of the time there is nothing there to see — which is the entire design, and a GM who resolves that ambiguity early has thrown away the good part.

---

## What They Look Like

A steady point of pale light, low over water or the mud at the edge of it, somewhere between a held lantern and a lantern set down. Colour varies — most often a cold white with a green cast, sometimes amber, once in a while a blue that people argue about for years afterward.

**They do not flicker like flame.** That is the tell everybody knows, and the reason nobody ever confuses one with a campfire. They sit, or they drift, at about the pace of a man walking who is not in a hurry.

**And at Vulture's Nest, that is a genuine problem**, because the Nest runs on signal lanterns strung through a maze of rope bridges and crane arms above the water (`places/vultures-nest.md`). A port whose entire night navigation is *read the lights* has lights on it that are not lanterns. Boats have been lost to this. Everybody knows. Nobody has a fix.

River crews call them **false lanterns** when they are being practical and spooklights when they are being honest.

---

## The Folk Rule

Every bargeman on the web knows this and will recite it without being asked:

> **"Runs, and it's nothing. Waits, and it's money. Comes, and it's your own fault."**

That is also the encounter table, and it is worth running it exactly as the rule states, because the rule is *correct*.

### Runs — almost always

**You approach and it goes out, or slides away, or was never quite where you thought.** This is the overwhelming majority and it should be the overwhelming majority at the table too. No roll, no reveal, no creature. Something about wet air and old glass and the angle of the water, and no one has ever got a straight answer.

**Run it as weather.** Describe it, let the party decide whether to go and look, and when they look, it is gone. Do that four or five times across a campaign before it ever does anything else, and the sixth one will land like a hammer.

### Waits — the interesting one

**You approach, and it stays exactly where it was.**

**A light that does not retreat is sitting on something.** It is not wandering, it is *attached* — and what it is attached to is nearly always a piece of quartz that came up the trade web from Glasslight Reach, where the stone keeps the colour of the last light that reached it (`places/glasslight-reach.md`, The Soft Edge). A chip of it in a riverbank is still, quietly, showing you an evening that happened somewhere else years ago.

**So a waiting light marks a place where old things have come to rest**, and the Nest already has a mechanism for putting those on display: on a deep slow tide the water drops and wrecks appear along the banks, old foundations surface, and carved glyphs reappear (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Tide). **The lights are on those sites before the tide uncovers them.**

Which makes a waiting spooklight the closest thing the river has to a treasure map, and river people treat it exactly that way. *Waits, and it's money.*

### Comes — rare, and the file does not fully answer it

**You approach and it approaches back.** Below.

## The Bad Interaction Nobody Planned

**Waiting lights and Foulhauls share ground, and neither of them arranged it** (`bestiary/foulhaul.md`).

A light marks a bank where the river has been putting things for a long time. A Foulhaul family lives on a bank where the river has been putting things for a long time. **They are the same bank, for the same reason, and one of them draws people to it.**

So: the light is bait, and nothing set it. A salvager who has followed the folk rule correctly, done nothing wrong, and found real money is standing in the dark on the exact stretch of mud a family of scavengers works — which is why the Nest has a steady supply of people who went out after a waiting light and did not come back, and why the town's explanation for that is *the lights take them.*

**The lights do not take anybody.** The town is wrong in the ordinary way towns are wrong, and being wrong about the mechanism has not made the advice any less deadly.

## When It Comes Toward You — the Glassgut

*The rare third case, and the only one that is a fight.*

A **Glassgut** is a river bottom-feeder about the size of a sow, broad and low and slow, that roots through glass-bearing gravel and grinds it. Grit works up into its hide and stays there, and the animal goes about its life carrying light it did not ask for and does not use.

**It is not luring anybody.** It has no idea it is glowing. It is coming toward you because you are standing between it and the water, or because you are making the sort of noise that means somebody has disturbed a bank, which in its experience means food has been turned up.

That it looks exactly like a folk-tale come to get you is entirely the observer's problem.

**Mind 2 / Body 5 / Soul 2 — HP 24**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 9

**Deck (9 — 2 Blue / 5 Red / 2 Green):** DISTRACT, STUDY *(blue)* · STRIKE, BREAK, TRAMPLE, DIG IN, GROUNDING STANCE *(red)* · PATIENCE, DELAY *(green)*

All core cards. It has no technique — it is a heavy animal that pushes.

**Trait — Bedded In:** While a Glassgut has not moved position since the start of its last turn, it has Armour 2. It is built to sit in a current and not be moved by it, and standing off and shooting it is the slow way to do this.

**Trait — Carrying the Light:** It cannot hide, ever, in any darkness, and it cannot be ambushed for the same reason — there is nowhere for it to be that isn't lit. Both sides always know exactly where the other one is.

**Measured** *(three starting characters, 700 fights per row, `combat-simulations/encounter_budget.py`; Effects not executed)*: one is a formality at 100%, two is a warm-up at 100% with the odd casualty, **three is the fight at 56%**, four is 12% and kills somebody. They are not social and do not travel in families, so **three is already a strange night** and wants a reason — a gravel bar that has just been exposed by a deep slow tide will do it.

## Running Them

- **Never roll to see what a light is.** Decide, in advance, what tonight's light is, and let the party's own behaviour be the only thing that resolves it.
- **Keep the ratio brutal.** Four or five nothings for every waiting one. One Glassgut in a campaign, maybe.
- **Do not let anybody be right too early.** The folk rule is accurate, and a party that learns it and trusts it is behaving correctly and will still walk onto a bank with a Foulhaul family on it. That is not a punishment for trusting the rule. The rule was never about what kills people.

---

## Not Yet Set

- **Why quartz that has been carried inland still holds its light.** The Unheld cannot be carried, but this is only stone keeping the colour of the last light that reached it (`places/glasslight-reach.md`) — ordinary, if strange. Whether it eventually goes dark, and how long that takes, is open.
- **The blue ones.** Rare enough that most rivermen have only heard about them. Nothing is written and nothing should be until somebody needs it.
- **Whether anything has learned to use them on purpose.** Nothing has, currently. It is the obvious next step and it should stay unavailable for a good while.
