# Kelbi

*A small deer of the Turnroot Weald (`places/turnroot-weald.md`). **Not a combat encounter.** There is no stat block here on purpose — see Running It, below.*

Knee-high at the shoulder, pale-dun, fine-legged, and unbothered by being looked at. What a Kelbi has instead of antlers is a pair of living branches — actual woody growth rooted in the skull, forking the way a young tree forks, and flowering. Small five-petalled blooms, cream going to rust at the edges, opening and closing on the canopy's own dim schedule rather than the animal's.

The branches grow, bud, bloom, and are shed, the way antlers are shed, on no calendar anyone has worked out. A Kelbi carrying a full rack in flower is not a Kelbi in season. It is a Kelbi that hasn't dropped yet.

---

## They Cannot Leave

A Kelbi that crosses out from under the canopy loses its flowers within the day, and the branches go grey and brittle behind them. The animal is fine. It will graze open ground perfectly happily, look entirely ordinary doing it, and — for reasons nobody has established — turn around and go back.

This is why they have never been a problem for anyone on the other side of the Masons' line (`factions-and-races/the-masons.md`, Known Works). The obvious fear, once someone works out what the flowers are, is that a walking animal carrying the Weald's own blooms would seed the forest anywhere it wandered. It doesn't. It can't get the flowers out.

Whether the forest arranged that, or the Kelbi simply can't live any other way, is not established and probably shouldn't be.

---

## They Route True

A Kelbi under canopy goes where the Weald sends it, without resistance and without getting lost — the only thing in the forest that seems to have no opinion about the routing at all. Predators execute the forest's will (`places/turnroot-weald.md`); the Kelbi just accepts it.

**Which makes following one genuinely useful and genuinely dangerous.** A party trailing a Kelbi will not get lost and will not arrive where they intended. It goes where the forest is sending it, which is the entire problem with everything else in the Weald too. Forest families use this on purpose and understand exactly what they're agreeing to when they do.

**Mechanically:** following a Kelbi in good faith counts as earned forest signs for one navigation (`quests/turnroot-weald-adventure.md`, Navigation) — no check. The DM chooses where it leads, and *where the forest wants them* is a legitimate answer.

---

## Hunting Them

They are the practical meat animal under canopy, and the forest-people have eaten them for as long as there have been forest-people. So this is a food problem, not a monster problem.

**They are not hard to approach. They are hard to catch.** A Kelbi will let a person get within a few strides, watch them do it, and then simply not be there — a standing start into cover that is over before a bow is up. Patience doesn't solve it, because the animal is not nervous and has nothing to be worn down. What solves it is terrain: cutting one off against ground it can't take, which means knowing the ground, which in a forest that rearranges its own paths means either a forest family's help or a long time spent failing.

**Suggested handling:** a Body (Senses) or Mind check against ground the DM sets, with Advantage for anyone who has actually scouted the approach, and a flat refusal to let repeated attempts grind it out — a missed Kelbi is a gone Kelbi, and the next one is somewhere else.

**The custom is the real gate, same as everything else here.** A Kelbi taken without gesture is Taking Without Gesture (`quests/turnroot-weald-adventure.md`, Violation Consequence Matrix) and behaves accordingly: the meat sours, the branches go grey in the pack, and a second offense puts the party on the Rootstalkers' list as thieves of the body. Kill one correctly — the gesture made, out loud, before the shot rather than after — and none of that happens.

## Befriending Them

Entirely possible, and the party will probably try. They are unafraid, they are curious about anything that holds still, and they will take food from a hand on about the third visit.

**It is not a taming and it doesn't produce a mount, a pet, or a combatant.** What a party gets is an animal that is willing to be near them, which under this canopy is worth more than it sounds: it will walk ahead of them on its own errands, and they can follow it (above). It will also wander off mid-journey, because it was never going where they were going.

**It will not follow them out from under the canopy**, for the same reason nothing else about a Kelbi leaves. A party that has grown fond of one and is now walking toward the Weald's edge is going to have to notice that on their own.

**A Kelbi will not go toward the Root Heart.** Not fear — it simply turns and grazes and will not be led. The one place the forest doesn't send them is the middle of it.

## Loot

- **Shed branches** — found on the ground, dropped, the animal fine and elsewhere. The patient answer to the same material, and the reason a party that works out the shedding stops hunting. Still requires the gesture; the forest does not distinguish between taking from a living thing and taking from the ground it grew on. See `items/turnroot-weald-items.md`, Flowering Branch.
- **Venison** — ordinary, good, and a real thing to carry into the Holdfast (`quests/turnroot-weald-adventure.md`, Points of Interest). A family that has been losing safe ground all season is not indifferent to meat arriving.

## Running It

**There is no stat block and it isn't an oversight.** A Kelbi doesn't fight, doesn't flee into an encounter, and has nothing to spend a combat round doing. A party that decides to attack one has killed a deer; resolve it as the hunt above, and let the custom do whatever the custom does. Building it a deck would be inventing a fight this animal has never been in.

**Don't make them mysterious.** The forest-people treat Kelbi the way any people treats a familiar food animal — useful, unremarkable, worth knowing the habits of. The flowering branches are strange to the party and ordinary to everyone who lives there, and that gap is the entire register (`world/tonal-bible.md`).
