# Foulhaul

*Riverbank scavengers. A breeding population along the web, and one very old one that has a name.*

**Cards:** `cards/foulhaul.md`

You smell it before anything else happens, and that is the single most important fact about this animal. Carrion and standing water and something sweeter underneath, arriving on the wind a full minute ahead of the thing making it. **A Foulhaul has never once ambushed anybody.** It cannot.

Seven feet standing, though it rarely bothers to stand all the way up. Long black hair over the whole body, including the face — there is no face to find, and people who have been close insist they looked. The head sits straight on the shoulders with no neck worth the name, which is what makes the silhouette wrong at distance before you work out why.

**It is usually carrying something.** That is the other thing everyone remembers. Upright, unhurried, one arm hooked under a load, walking the bank like it has somewhere to be.

---

## What It Does

**They eat the dead, and they have never had to compete for them.** A river carries bodies — drowned crew, livestock, whatever the fast tide took off a bank upstream — and a Foulhaul walks the shore collecting. They do not hunt what is healthy and running. They are strong enough to and it has never been worth the trouble.

**They will absolutely take what is merely still.** Sleeping, injured, unconscious, laid out. The distinction the animal makes is not living-or-dead, it is *moving-or-not*, and a party that has learned to treat them as scavengers rather than predators has learned something slightly wrong in a way that will cost them somebody.

**They follow reliable food.** A stretch of river that produces bodies on a schedule will have a family living on it inside a generation, and they remember schedules better than they remember anything else.

---

## The Smell, and Why It Is a Gift

**Encounters with Foulhauls should never begin with initiative.** They begin with a smell on the wind and about a minute of warning, every single time, and that minute is the encounter's actual content: move the boat off the bank, get up something, get a fire going, put the cargo somewhere, or decide to fight on ground you picked.

A GM who takes the warning away to get a surprise round has removed the only interesting thing about the animal. **The warning is not a weakness. It is the design.**

## Stat Block — Foulhaul

**Mind 1 / Body 4 / Soul 2 — HP 19**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 7

**Deck (7 — 1 Blue / 4 Red / 2 Green):** DISTRACT *(blue)* · OVERBEAR, BURDENED, STRIKE, TRAMPLE *(red)* · THE SMELL OF IT, PATIENCE *(green)*

**Trait — Reek:** Every enemy in the same position as a Foulhaul has 1 Weak at all times — the next damage roll each of them makes subtracts a d6 (`rules/card-glossary.md`). It does not stack from a second Foulhaul in the same position; the air is already as bad as it gets.

**Trait — Carries Off:** When a Foulhaul brings a character to Collapse, **it does not finish them.** It picks them up and leaves — off the bank, into the trees, at a walking pace, without hurrying and without defending itself on the way out. It has what it came for.

**This is the fight's second clock and it is the reason to run them.** A downed character is not dead, they are *cargo*, and the fight now has two win conditions that compete: put the rest of the family down, or go get your friend back before the walk ends. A party that wins the fight and loses the person has still lost.

**They do not fight to the death.** A family that has lost two of its number, or one that has secured a body, withdraws. They are not brave and were never angry.

## The Grandfather

**The old one on the Pneum funeral corridor**, and the reason that stretch of river has a reputation.

Rivermen call him **the Grandfather**, which is not affection. It is the ordinary practice of not saying a dangerous thing's real name where it might be listening, and bargemen who have worked that water for thirty years will call him that without ever once being asked why.

**Pneum calls him the Receiver**, and means something completely different by it (`places/pneum.md`). Their funeral barges have run that corridor for generations, and something enormous has met them at the bank for as long as anyone remembers, and taken what was offered, and gone. To the congregation that is the Unheld accepting its due through a vessel it chose. **It is an old animal that learned a schedule.** Both of those groups are talking about the same creature and neither has ever compared notes with the other.

**Stat Block — the Grandfather**

**Mind 3 / Body 8 / Soul 4 — HP 39**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 15

**Deck (15 — 3 Blue / 8 Red / 4 Green):** DISTRACT, ANTICIPATE, STUDY *(blue)* · THE LONG REACH, STRIKE, BREAK, SUNDER, TRAMPLE, PAIN IS FUEL, STAND, REND *(red)* · WHAT IS OWED, PATIENCE, DELAY, WITNESS *(green)*

**Trait — Old Hide:** Armour 2, permanently. Small hits do very little to him, which pushes a party toward committing their heaviest Red cards and leaves them with nothing held back.

**Trait — Longer Than You Think:** He can attack Backline from Frontline. There is no safe rank against him, and a party that has learned to park its Mind character at the back will find that out in the first exchange.

**Trait — The Offering:** **He does not attack first, and he does not attack anyone who has just given him something.** Put a body in front of him — a dead thing, a downed enemy, an animal, anything that is no longer moving — and he takes it and goes. Every time. It has worked for generations, which is precisely why Pneum thinks it is a sacrament.

**So the boss fight is optional and the party will hate that.** The correct answer to the Grandfather is the thing the congregation has been doing all along, and a party unwilling to do it is choosing a CTR 15 problem on purpose.

## Running Them — measured counts

*Against three starting characters, 700 fights per row (`combat-simulations/encounter_budget.py`). Card Effects are not executed, so these are structural floors (`rules/gm-guide.md`, How many of them).*

| Encounter | Party wins | Avg deaths |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 Foulhauls | 100% | 0.0–0.1 |
| **3 Foulhauls** | **80%** | 1.0 |
| 4 Foulhauls | 25% | 1.8 |
| 5+ Foulhauls | ≤2% | 2.0 |
| The Grandfather alone | 97% | 0.5 |
| Grandfather + 1 | 85% | 0.8 |
| **Grandfather + 2** | **41%** | 1.6 |
| Grandfather + 3 | 8% | 1.9 |

**Two or three is a bank encounter. Four is a crisis.** The travel band is deliberately narrow — these are not meant to be the fight, they are meant to be the reason anchoring somewhere is a decision.

**The Grandfather alone is a formality**, exactly as the count lever predicts for anything this heavy, so **he never comes alone.** He arrives with family, and the escort is the tuning dial: one for a fight they should win, two for a real one, three to kill somebody.

## Where They Are

- **The Pneum funeral corridor** — the long stretch between the lake and the Glasslight quay (`places/glasslight-reach.md`), which is the only water the congregation's barges run. The Grandfather's range.
- **Around Vulture's Nest**, on the layered banks either side of the port, sharing that ground with the spooklights (`bestiary/spooklight.md`). Neither arranged it: a light marks where the river has been leaving things for years, and that is the same reason a family lives there. **The light brings people onto the bank and nothing set it as bait** — a low steady light on dark mud reads as somebody in trouble, so the people who die out there went to help. The Nest blames the lights, which is wrong about the mechanism and has kept roughly the right number of people at home anyway.
- **Anywhere a fast tide puts bodies on a bank on a schedule.** That is most of the web at one time or another, which makes them the default bank creature for travel that stops for the night.
- **Not on the water.** They do not swim well and have no reason to. A boat that stays off the bank does not meet one.

*Which is the trap the region sets, taken as a whole: the bank is dangerous on a slow tide and the water is dangerous on a fast one (`bestiary/gowra.md`). A crew fighting its way upriver has to be on one or the other.*

## Not Yet Set

- **How many are on the corridor.** A family group is the assumption; the actual number has never been counted by anyone with a reason to be accurate.
- **What Pneum does if the party kills the Receiver.** They have not got a framework for it and will not have one by the end of the conversation.
- **Whether the Grandfather has ever taken someone still moving.** Rivermen have opinions. Nothing is written.
- **Whether "haulers" gets anyone killed.** On the river the word already means the men and animals on a pullway (`world/geography-overview.md`, How a boat actually moves), and bank crews use it for these as well. Which one somebody means depends on which bank they are standing on, and the ambiguity is left in on purpose.
