# Wall Reader — Profile

## Appearance

Pale — the color of skin that hasn't seen light in longer than anyone alive has been walking these passages. Long-limbed, human-shaped enough that the first look is the worst one: two arms, two legs, upright, the proportions just slightly wrong in a way that takes a second to name. Where eyes should sit, there's only more of the same smooth, unbroken skin — not scarred over, not healed shut, simply never reopened.

One arm ends differently than the other. The left hand — always the left — has worn itself against stone for so long the fingers no longer fully separate, callused into something closer to a flat plane than a hand, shaped exactly to the texture of the wall it has never once left. The right hand is unmarked. Ordinary. A hand that stopped being used for anything but balance a long time ago.

It moves without hurry, the marked hand always in contact, trailing stone at shoulder height in the same corridor the party is being asked to walk. Nothing about it suggests violence. Nothing about it suggests peace either. It is simply, completely, still doing the one thing it has been doing since before it was this.

## Behavior

It was a person, once. Nothing in the Coil says who, or when, or how long the rule has had them — that isn't the kind of thing this place gives up, and it doesn't need to. What's left isn't tragic the way a fresh death is tragic. It's older than that. It followed the wall-rule so completely, for so long, that the following outlasted the person doing it.

It does not hunt. It does not guard. It isn't waiting for anything, because waiting implies an end it stopped expecting a long time ago. A party keeping correctly to their own wall gets passed alongside or across without so much as a turned head — not indifference exactly, closer to the specific attention a person pays their own reflection: recognized, and not worth remarking on.

Breaking the rule near it changes that instantly. It stops. Turns toward the disruption — sound, a hand lifted off stone, anything that isn't the wall. It does not rush. It has never once needed to rush. It simply, correctly, starts closing the distance, and it will not lose the thread again once it's found it.

If it catches you, it doesn't seem to enjoy it, and it doesn't seem to regret it either. It isn't cruel. It's just no longer capable of being anything else.
