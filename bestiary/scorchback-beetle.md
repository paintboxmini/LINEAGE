# Scorchback Beetle

A thumb-length beetle with a dull, heat-scarred carapace, found in the Ashfall Wastes where the ground stays warm — the Cindersink margins especially (`places/ashfall-wastes.md`). Nothing magical about it: it stores volatile plant oils in an abdominal gland and vents them under pressure, the same chemistry a bombardier beetle uses for its boiling spray.

The oils are the point. Concentrated in the gland and dried, they carry a genuine capsaicin-grade burn and leave a faint ember-glow on whatever they are added to — the hot, physical counterpart to Sapphire Ant's cool blue shimmer (`bestiary/sapphire-ant.md`).

---

## Behavior

Slow, and aware that it does not need to be fast. Scorchbacks graze warm ash for the resinous scrub that grows in it, which is where the oils come from — a beetle taken from cold ground has a thin gland and is barely worth the walk.

They do not flee. A disturbed Scorchback rotates its abdomen toward the disturbance and vents, and the spray comes out near boiling. It is not aggressive so much as entirely unbothered.

---

## Harvesting

**This is the dangerous ingredient, and the danger is the gate.** The gland has to come out whole — punctured in the field, it empties, and what you are left with is a beetle. Taking one intact means either working on a torpid beetle in the cold hour before dawn, or accepting the spray and having something between it and your hands.

A vented beetle is spent for hours and not worth carrying. A patch worked carelessly is a patch that has all vented at once, which is both a burn and a wasted trip.

Dried and ground, the gland yields **cinder flake**.

---

## Culinary Note

Cinder flake is a real seasoning before it is anything else — heat with an oily depth behind it, closer to a toasted chili than a raw one. Senshi would take it (`places/capital/gilded-tusk.md`), and there is no reason he does not already know what it is.

It is also one of the charges in Kevin's grinder: `experimental/kevin.md`.
