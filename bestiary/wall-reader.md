# Wall-Reader

*A blind, many-armed figure in the Coil's lower passages, following the same wall-following rule the party is. It was a person, once. Not anymore.*

**Cards:** `cards/wall-reader.md`

**Mind 1 / Body 4 / Soul 3 — HP 20**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 8

**Deck (8 — 1 Blue / 4 Red / 3 Green):** CERTAIN STRIKE, RHYTHM BREAK, GROUNDING STANCE, DIG IN *(red)* · BLANK *(blue)* · STILL COUNTING, PATIENCE, FLOW *(green)*

**Blank deck:** Blue — BLANK *(Ranged)* · Red — GROUNDING STANCE *(Both)* · Green — STILL COUNTING *(Both)* (`rules/gm-guide.md`, Running simple creatures from a blank deck)

*CERTAIN STRIKE and RHYTHM BREAK are the promoted-to-core versions of this creature's original NEVER LIFTED and YOU CHANGED WALLS (`cards/red-body.md`) — same cards, de-flavored names.*

## Appearance

*Redrawn by Drew, 2026-11-20, for his art.*

**Human-shaped at the core** — pale, long-limbed, the colour of skin that hasn't seen light in longer than anyone alive has been walking these passages — **and around twenty arms.** *They grow from the shoulders, the ribs, down the back and the flanks, too many to count at a glance.*

**Every hand acts as if it has a mind of its own, and every one of them wants the same thing: to keep touching the wall.** As it walks, they **frantically reposition** — peeling off the stone behind, scrabbling forward over one another, slapping flat again ahead — *a constant crawling ripple down its wall side, never fewer than a dozen palms on the stone at once.* **The hands are desperate. The body is not**: it moves without hurry, carried along by them.

**It is blind** — *where eyes should sit there is only smooth, unbroken skin, never reopened.* **Its face never leaves the wall anyway.** *It walks with that blank face turned toward the stone, fixed on it, afraid of drifting out of reach.* **It never looks at the party** — *which is worse, the first time it does.*

**Across its chest, an empty bandolier: worn leather, seven loops, nothing in any of them.** *GM only: they held a Mason's chisels* (`places/the-coil.md`, The Masons' Room; GM Secret). **The chisels are lost somewhere in the Coil's maze** — *one may surface for the party* (`places/the-coil.md`, Loot). **Nobody at the table should read it as anything until Code finds that room.**

**Nothing about it suggests violence. Nothing about it suggests peace either.** It is simply, completely, still doing the one thing it has been doing since before it was this.

## Behavior

It was a person, once. Nothing in the Coil says who, or when, or how long the rule has had them — that isn't the kind of thing this place gives up, and it doesn't need to. What's left isn't tragic the way a fresh death is tragic. It's older than that. It followed the wall-rule so completely, for so long, that the following outlasted the person doing it.

It does not hunt. It does not guard. It isn't waiting for anything, because waiting implies an end it stopped expecting a long time ago. A party keeping correctly to their own wall gets passed alongside or across without so much as a turned head — not indifference exactly, closer to the specific attention a person pays their own reflection: recognized, and not worth remarking on.

Breaking the rule near it changes that instantly. It stops. Turns toward the disruption — sound, a hand lifted off stone, anything that isn't the wall. It does not rush. It has never once needed to rush. It simply, correctly, starts closing the distance, and it will not lose the thread again once it's found it.

If it catches you, it doesn't seem to enjoy it, and it doesn't seem to regret it either. It isn't cruel. It's just no longer capable of being anything else.

## Combat Behavior

Nothing in its kit initiates. GROUNDING STANCE and DIG IN are both about staying exactly where it already is, not closing distance on its own — the fight only starts because the party broke the rule near it. Once it starts, RHYTHM BREAK is the whole point: bonus damage against anyone who's moved position since their own last turn, which is to say, anyone still panicking. CERTAIN STRIKE backs that up — the attack can't be dodged, resisted, or thrown off by blindness, because none of that was ever how you were getting away from this. STILL COUNTING punishes it for the exact thing it's punishing the party for: if it hasn't moved either, the attack hits harder — which means the coldest, most correct play for a Wall-Reader is often to just not move at all and let PATIENCE and STILL COUNTING both come online at once.

The counter is the obvious one, because it's the same counter the whole Coil has been teaching since the entrance: stop moving. It has no reason to press an advantage on a target that's holding still and hasn't broken anything.

---

## Tactical Purpose

The Wall-Reader is a mirror, not a monster — its kit exists to make the Coil's own lesson mechanical instead of implied: follow the rule, hold still, don't panic. It reads as a name for what happens to someone who followed that lesson forever, not as an enemy with a grudge. A party that never breaks the rule near it can walk straight through an encounter with this creature and never draw a card.
