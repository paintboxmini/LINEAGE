# Gowra

*The one everybody has heard of and nobody believes in. Which is the problem.*

**Cards:** `cards/gowra.md`

Long — four times the length of a man and most of that tail — low to the ground on short bowed legs, with webbed feet that end in digging claws. Slate-grey hide in thick overlapping plates. A ridge of blunt horns runs the spine from skull to hips. **Two tusks curve up out of the lower jaw** and are worn flat at the tips from the thing it does with them.

**The tail ends in a blade.** Not a metaphor and not a spur — a flattened wedge of the same plate material, a foot across at the base, honed by use.

---

## Nobody Believes In It

**"A gowra story" means a lie.** At Vulture's Nest and up and down the web, the word has been a byword for nonsense for generations. Somebody's uncle wrestled one. Somebody's cousin knows the cave. There is always a man at the market with a sack of gowra teeth at two coppers each, and he has never been closer to one than his own workbench.

**The word is worn out and there is still a gowra.** That is the entire design of this animal and it should be protected: **everyone who has actually seen one has been laughed at**, so nobody has ever assembled the accounts, so nobody is warned, so it keeps working the same stretch of river for forty years.

**A party that comes back from a bank with a real account will not be believed either.** Bartho will be polite about it. Erubeth will not. Somebody will ask, cheerfully, whether they brought a tooth back.

*And at least one of the teeth in that sack at the market is genuine. The man selling it does not know. Nobody can tell, because nobody is looking, because they are all obviously fake.*

---

## What It Actually Does

**It undermines banks.** Those claws and those worn-flat tusks are for digging, and what it digs is a long chamber back into an undercut riverbank — above the waterline, dry, dark, and reached from the water. It lives there. It has usually lived there a long time.

**Which means the bank above it is hollow**, and nobody upstairs has any way to know. A stretch of bank that a gowra has worked will take a boat's weight and a crew's weight right up until it does not.

**It hunts the fast tide.** When the water runs hard the river brings it things and the noise covers it, and — this is the part that matters for play — **a hard tide is exactly when a crew going upriver has to tie up and wait** (`world/geography-overview.md`, How a boat actually moves). The tide that forces a party onto a bank is the tide that has the gowra out.

**It is an ambush animal and a patient one.** It does not chase, it does not range, and it will not follow a party more than a few hundred yards from its own ground. Outrun it or get onto open ground and it simply stops and watches you go.

**And it takes exactly one thing and leaves.** A gowra is not trying to kill a party. It comes out of the water, takes whoever is closest to the edge, and goes back under the bank with them — and if the rest of them have any sense they will get on their boat and let it. **They will not, which is the encounter** (Takes One Under, below).

---

## The Roar

**In the open it is a loud animal. In an enclosed space it is a weapon.**

Under a bank, in its own dug chamber, in a collapsed hollow — the sound has nowhere to go and arrives as a physical event. People lose the thread of what they were doing. Hands open. Somebody who was about to swing does not.

**It knows the difference.** A gowra caught on open ground does not bother roaring, and a party that works that out has found the actual solution to this animal, which is *not a damage race.*

## Stat Block

**Mind 2 / Body 8 / Soul 3 — HP 37**
**Creature Threat Rating:** 13

**Deck (13 — 2 Blue / 8 Red / 3 Green):** THE ROAR *(signature)*, DISTRACT *(blue)* · TAIL BLADE, TUSKWORK *(signatures)*, STRIKE, BREAK, SUNDER, TRAMPLE, REND, PAIN IS FUEL *(red)* · PATIENCE, DELAY, SHADE AWAY *(green)*

**Trait — Plated:** Armour 2, permanently.

**Trait — Undermines:** Any bank a gowra has worked is hollow and will give way under weight — a boat winched up, a party making camp, four people standing in one place. **This is the encounter generator, not an attack.** The collapse is usually how a fight starts, and it starts with the party at the bottom of a hole, in an enclosed space, with it.

**Trait — Takes One Under:** **The first thing a gowra does is take somebody.** It comes out of the water without warning, closes on whoever is nearest the edge, and is gone with them before the rest of the party has finished turning around. A few seconds under, and then the den.

**Three rules that keep this from being the miserable version:**

**It is a short trip and everybody knows where they went.** The den is *right there* — back under the bank they are standing on, reached from the water, eight or ten feet of it. Nobody is swept downstream. Nobody is lost. **The party does not go back for them; the party goes in after them**, which is a completely different scene and a much better one.

**The den is dry and full of air.** It is above the waterline — that is the whole point of the way this animal digs. The underwater part lasts a few seconds and is narrated, not played. **No breath tracking, no drowning clock, no soaked and useless kit.** The dragged character comes up coughing on solid ground with everything they were carrying, in the dark, alone, with it.

**They are the most interesting person at the table, not the sidelined one.** One character is in the boss room a round or two before anybody else can reach them, and everything they do in that time matters enormously. **Never make that player sit and wait** — they should be acting every single round.

**It does this once.** It has what it came for and it will not go back for a second. The drag is how the encounter opens, not a thing that keeps happening to people in turn.

*Choose the target by position, not by dice. Whoever was nearest the water is a consequence of something a player decided, which lands far better than a roll.*

**Trait — Knows Where It Is:** THE ROAR can only be played in an enclosed position — a chamber, a collapsed bank, under a deck, a cave. **On open ground it is not in the deck at all.** Terrain is this creature's entire difficulty dial.

## Running It — and an honest note on the numbers

*Three starting characters, 800 fights per row (`combat-simulations/encounter_budget.py`).*

| | Party wins | Avg deaths |
|---|---|---|
| One gowra, flat | 99% | 0.4 |
| Two | 23% | 1.8 |

**Take that 99% with a large caveat.** The simulator does not execute card Effects, so it is running a lone heavy animal with no roar, no collapse, and no terrain — which is precisely the version of this creature that is supposed to be survivable. **The floor says one gowra on open dry ground is a bad afternoon and not a disaster, and that is correct.**

**What makes it a fight is the hole it drops you into**, and the fact that it starts with one of the party already in there. A group Staggered on the first exchange, coming into a dug chamber one at a time, with a friend somewhere ahead of them in the dark, is having a very different encounter from the one the table above measures — and the sim cannot model any of it.

**So: one, always.** They are solitary and territorial and two is a wipe at 23%. If a GM has stripped the terrain and wants it to still be dangerous, add a second — but the better answer is to put the terrain back.

## Where They Are

- **Undercut banks on the fast-tide stretches near Vulture's Nest.** Same country as the Foulhauls and the spooklights and, like both of them, keyed to a tide state — except that this one is a **fast** tide animal, which completes the set. *Slow tide has its own dangers on the bank; fast tide has this one in the water.*
- **They do not come into town.** Never have. It is one more reason nobody at the Nest takes them seriously.

## Not Yet Set

- **How many are on the web.** Very few. Solitary, territorial, long-lived, and slow to breed — which is why it has never become common knowledge and never will.
- **Whether the market teeth include a real one.** Written above as a yes, deliberately without saying which, because the good version is that nobody ever confirms it.
- **What a gowra does with a bank it has finished with.** They move eventually. An abandoned chamber is a dry, hidden, defensible room reachable only from the water, and somebody at the Nest would find that extremely useful (`places/vultures-nest.md`).
- **The 1897 problem** — somewhere there is a person who killed one and cannot prove it, and has stopped telling anybody. Unwritten and worth someone.
