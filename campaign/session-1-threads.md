# Session 1 — The Threads at the Nest

**This is a catalogue, not a running order.** It deliberately does not say what happens when. `places/vultures-nest.md`, The Opening Scene writes the dock as a sequence of beats, which is the right way to *understand* it and the wrong way to *run* it — read that once, then run off this.

**The table decides the order.** Every thread below is written to be touched for two minutes, opened for forty, or skipped without anybody noticing. What it is *not* written to be is a queue. **There is more material here than one session needs, and that is the point** — a session where the players choose which thread to pull needs more threads than they will pull.

*Session 0 does character creation and the card draft, so Session 1 opens straight into the world with no table business in front of it (`campaign/session-1-convergence.md`).*

---

## The only things that actually depend on each other

Almost nothing. Written out, the whole spine is:

**The lockdown** happens first, because it is why these people are standing in the same conversation.

Then: **the crash needs the lockdown** (the berth is short because the barge has half the frontage). **The wrackclaws need the crash** (they come for the spill). **The count needs the crash** (it is what Bartho does about it). **The crate and the escort both need the count** (it is the pressure that makes Harlock ask and the reason Jonas is walking anywhere).

**Everything else floats.** The market, the Keel, the letter, Aege, Erubeth, the Scarves and all three characters' private problems have no position in the day at all. Run them whenever the table reaches for them.

---

## The one real dial

**The gap between "Jonas, go aboard" and Jonas actually boarding is a setting, not a fact.** The written scene puts it at ten minutes — a man walking to fetch a ledger, visible the whole way. That produces one specific session and it is not the only one.

| Gap | What the session becomes |
|---|---|
| **Ten minutes** | The crate decision happens now, under visible pressure, immediately after a fight. Fast, tense, and the party will choose tired. *The written default.* |
| **An hour or two** | Jonas is genuinely too busy — a wrecked berth to survey, a crane to log, his own paperwork. The party has time to go to the market and come back. **Harlock's ask lands on their return**, and lands better: they have had time to notice the Moving Crate at the east end before anybody asks them to carry one. |
| **Tomorrow morning** | The whole day is free. The crate becomes the thing hanging over the evening at the Keel, and Aege's dinner happens with it unresolved. |

**The long gap is probably the better one and it is not what the scene is written as.** Stretching it costs nothing — Jonas being overwhelmed is the most in-character thing about him — and it buys the market thread a reason to happen before the decision rather than after it. *Two hours is the recommendation. Ten minutes is a good session too.*

---

## How to read a thread below

**Pulled by** — what a player says or does that opens it. This is the lookup: somebody asks about something, find the thread it belongs to.
**Light** — the version that costs two minutes and still counts as having happened.
**Deep** — what is actually in there if they push.
**Skipped** — what is lost, and what happens in the world anyway.
**Leaves behind** — what is still true afterward.

---

## 1. The lockdown — Bartho and the barge

**What it is.** A stretch of berth is impounded over a Promise ritual barge nobody will explain, and everything moored near it went into a warehouse behind a question (`quests/the-quarantined-barge.md`). All three characters are stopped by it. Bartho handles them as one group because three interviews take four times as long as one.

**Pulled by** — any version of *why can't we leave*, *what's behind the rope*, *who do we talk to*.

**Light.** Bartho explains the hold without apologising for it, does not say the word *cursed*, and the dockhands behind him do. The party learns that official authority exists and the price is not coin. Four minutes.

**Deep.** The whole barge quest is sitting there — Corvel in custody, the Compact unable to take a cut and look away, Bartho's ask if they stall (`quests/the-quarantined-barge.md`). This is the campaign's actual first job and it can absolutely start in session one if they push at it.

**Skipped.** It cannot be skipped — it is the frame. It *can* be three sentences.

**Leaves behind.** Their freight is still impounded. Nothing in the day fixes that.

---

## 2. The Red Scarves

**What it is.** Three kids working the edge, openly, offering to carry bags and show the market. The information is genuine. They are skimming the whole time and nobody ever has anything on them (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Red Scarves).

**Pulled by** — a player clocking the kids and saying so. Or not, which is also fine.

**Light.** Let the table see them and not act. The theft lands later in the market and the party finds out when they check. **A player who spots them has earned that; a player who doesn't has been robbed fair.**

**Deep.** Caught in the act, Kino runs instantly and the others scatter into the stalls. A chase catches Bean, who knows nothing and says all of it. **A chase that goes well still ends at a hole in the Ribs sized for somebody under fourteen**, which is the whole security model and a better scene than catching anyone.

**Skipped.** Lose *robbed by children*, and lose the best version of Aege's dinner — she buys if they got skimmed that day.

**Leaves behind.** The kids have them. Kino will pretend it never happened.

---

## 3. The crash — Harlock brings the Arcadia in

**What it is.** A ship hits the dock hard, coming into a short berth on a fast tide because the impound has half the frontage roped off. Timber goes, a crane arm swings loose, nobody dies and somebody could have.

**Pulled by** — nothing. It happens to them.

**Light.** Spectacle and a confrontation between two men who each know which half of it is their fault. Five minutes.

**Deep.** The confrontation is the Compact's whole politics in one argument — the dockmaster, the pirate, and an impound neither of them wanted. A party that reads the room learns the council has three seats and they are not friendly.

**Skipped.** Do not skip. Everything downstream hangs off it.

**Leaves behind.** A wrecked berth across the middle of the dock, which is also why Jonas can take two hours.

---

## 4. The spill — wrackclaws on the stones

**What it is.** A season's freight goes in the water on a slow tide, and wreck-scavengers come up the stones for it (`bestiary/wrackclaw.md`). Every hand on that frontage is under Compact orders not to touch loose cargo off an impounded berth. **The party are the only people standing there with no rule stopping them.**

**Pulled by** — it comes to them, but they can decline. Nothing forces this fight.

**Light.** **Three wrackclaws** against three starting characters: **the party wins about 99.8% of the time, somebody Collapses in about one fight in eight, and a character dies in about one in twenty** — measured 2026-09-22 over 1,200 fights at each of three seeds, deck as `bestiary/wrackclaw.md` writes it and all three Frontline. *That last figure is the whole session budget spent in one fight (`rules/gm-guide.md`, Pacing Sessions), so it sits on the ceiling rather than inside it.* **Four is about five times as lethal and is a different encounter, not a bigger one.**

**Deep.** It is the Range tutorial with the floor: wet timber at the edge is Frontline, dry stone behind is Backline, and a Melee card needs both of you out on the timber. A player who works that out in their first fight has learned the thing the Oracle spends a third of its design space on, by standing somewhere.

**Skipped.** This is the most expensive thread on the dock in table minutes and the one whose removal costs least. **Harlock's hire does not break without it** — his stated reason is that they are strangers, and strangers are the qualification. The Range lesson can happen in any later fight.

**Leaves behind.** Whatever the scavengers dragged up, on the stones where it can be reached. Nobody drowns, nothing disarms anyone, and losing is a loss rather than a wound: break off and the wrackclaws take what they can drag, the tide takes the rest, **and Harlock watches the party decide it was not their problem.** That is the audition failing, which is a real consequence and not a punishment.

---

## 5. The count — Bartho raises his voice

**What it is.** Bartho shouts, which the party has no way of knowing is rare. **Play the dock's reaction, not the volume** — every hand in earshot stops moving, and people who have worked this port ten years look up because they have not heard that sound before. Then he is quiet again and does the actual damage: *"Jonas. Go aboard. Inventory the cargo."*

**Pulled by** — nothing. It is done to Harlock in front of them.

**Light.** Harlock's face is the entire beat. He has a famous temper and does not use it, because using it confirms there is something to find. He agrees. **That is the most frightening thing he does all session.**

**Deep.** Why it lands: Jonas's ledger is cross-referenced against Quartermaster Voss's intake at Eclipseria South Gate. A full count of a pirate's hold written by the Regency's own man is not a fine, it is a permanent fact in a book Harlock cannot reach.

**Skipped.** Do not skip. This is the hinge the back half of the day turns on.

**Leaves behind.** A clock, at whatever length you set it (The one real dial, above).

---

## 6. The crate — Harlock's offer

**What it is.** A crate on his deck. Carried off, through the crowd, and **set down in the open in the market and left there** — which is the part that sounds wrong and the part he will not explain. Good money, offered without haggling, which from a man famous for his temper is itself information.

**Pulled by** — he comes to them. But only while the count is pending; after Jonas has boarded there is nothing to buy.

**Light.** They take it or they don't. Neither answer needs a scene.

**Deep.** Why strangers: his own crew carrying his cargo off his ship during an impound, minutes before a count, is a confession with witnesses. Why public: **the thing in the box starves in a warehouse**, which is why the last one is standing in the middle of the busiest market on the continent in a plain wooden box (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Arcadia's hold — GM only). A party that cracks the Arcadia gets the route. They do not get the source.

**Skipped.** Refused or missed, Harlock solves it himself — badly — and the party finds out later what they were standing next to. **The Ocellus route survives either way.**

**Leaves behind.** *The best version is a party that takes the money and only learns later what they carried through a crowded market. Nobody lied to them. They simply did not ask, which is what everyone else in that chain decided too.*

---

## 7. The escort — going with Jonas

**What it is.** The other half of the same offer. Jonas, alone, about to board a pirate's ship because the dockmaster told him to, and visibly not wanting to. **He does not ask, because it would not occur to him that he is allowed to.**

**Pulled by** — a player noticing him and offering. Purely elective, and nothing prompts it.

**Light.** They walk him aboard. It is the kindest available thing and it takes two minutes.

**Deep.** They are standing in the hold while the count happens, which means they see what Harlock did not want written down.

**Skipped.** He goes alone and it goes however it goes.

**Leaves behind.** **At a ten-minute gap the two jobs are incompatible and the party cannot take both** — whichever way it went, the other happened anyway. **At a two-hour gap they can do both**, which is a softer session and a perfectly good one.

---

## 8. The market

**What it is.** Unpaved road, canvas between poles, half the faces hooded, practically rather than dramatically.

**Pulled by** — leaving the dock in that direction. No prerequisite at all.

**Light.** Marta's stall, and the sweet jerky is genuinely the best thing they eat for two weeks. Buttons and Such is unmanned and always stocked and nobody knows whose it is.

**Deep.** Harwick Sundries has four mystery objects on the counter that are **deliberately not built yet** — the Tuning Fork Sword, the Desire Compass, the Guard Boots, the Ticking Box. Do not price them or give them mechanics. They are there to be picked up and put down.

**And the Moving Crate is at the east end.** A medium wooden crate, no stall, no owner, shifting slightly, reacting to proximity. **If the market happens before Harlock's ask, the party have already seen one of these** — which is the single best argument for the long gap.

**Skipped.** Costs the Scarves their payoff and the crate its foreshadowing.

**Leaves behind.** Whatever they bought, and whatever the kids took.

---

## 9. The Keel — Salla, and the letter

**What it is.** A tavern two degrees off level where the furniture has been shimmed so many times the legs are all different lengths. Salla pours heavy, asks nothing, and **holds letters for people with no address** — not a service, not advertised, not charged for.

**Pulled by** — wanting a drink, a room, or somewhere to talk without being overheard.

**Light.** *"Aege. Got a letter here for you."* A lean woman takes it, thanks her, walks off. **That is the whole beat.** She does not look the party over. None of it is about them.

**Deep.** The Back Corner is a good place to overhear things and a good place not to be overheard. Salla's boat hook is never dusty and she does not tell that story.

**Skipped.** Then Aege has to arrive some other way, and the letter's quiet setup is lost.

**Leaves behind.** A letter planted that gets answered hours later, after everyone has stopped thinking about it.

---

## 10. Aege, and the Weald

**What it is.** Dinner. She takes a table if they have given her any reason to think they are worth the chair, and she has been watching how they walk since they came in.

**Pulled by** — asking about the letter. Or nothing; she offers the meal unprompted.

**Light.** She buys, if the Scarves got them that day. She does not make anything of it and does not ask what happened, because she can see what happened. *"Eat. Road's longer on an empty stomach."*

**Deep.** Asked about the letter she answers straight: her family in the Weald, her younger brother, and forest signs that the overgrowth season will be a bad one. **And she leaves it there.** She does not announce that she is going, because **she has not decided to go.** *Having her stand up and declare a departure spends the whole thread in one scene and buys nothing for it.*

**Skipped.** Entirely skippable. She is at the Keel tomorrow and the day after. **There is no clock on her at all** — this is the single most movable thread in the session.

**Leaves behind.** *Told no by authority, robbed by children, fed by a stranger.* That is the shape of the day and this is its third act.

---

## 11. Erubeth, not looking up

**What it is.** A man in a long coat and a wide hat with a boomstick across his knees. **He did not look up at the crash. He looked at the party.**

**Pulled by** — approaching him, which they should not be encouraged to do.

**Light.** Nothing comes of it yet. That is the entire instruction.

**Deep.** Don't. He is a Regency agent tracing FourthEye back to its source and he does not have the thread yet either (`characters/erubeth.md`). He has been asking about the Moving Crate because it is the one object in a market of appraisers that nobody appraises, and he has not opened it.

**Skipped.** Nothing is lost. He is a face for later.

**Leaves behind.** One unexplained look.

## The three private threads

**All three are now written into the scene itself** (`places/vultures-nest.md`, The Opening Scene — beats one and five) — Kevin's and Pat's inside the lockdown conversation, Chris's inside the count. They are summarised here so this file still reads as the whole picture.

**The risk they exist to cover:** in a day this crowded, three players could watch a terrific sequence that was not about any of them. Each of these costs one line and makes the day land on a specific person.

**Chris — he cannot afford to be counted.** *"Jonas. Go aboard. Inventory the cargo"* is a dockmaster ordering a Regency-cross-referenced ledger opened on this berth, and Chris is standing on it. **The count is already his nightmare and it is already in the scene doing nothing for him.** **Jonas stops on his way past and asks Bartho — not them — whether he should be taking their names as well. Bartho waves it off.** Eight seconds, nobody threatens anyone, and a Regency ledger came within one shrug of opening on him.

**Kevin — the port knows him.** He ran temple wine into this dock many times and chose it because a person can vanish here (`campaign/kevin.md`). **A dockhand looks up and says his name, warm and pleased to see him, in front of a dockmaster about to start writing things down.** It is friendly, and that is what makes it bad. *He is carrying no cargo and running no errand — that was settled and it stays settled.*

**Pat — the river questions.** He is asking after one living person in a port mid-quarantine, which is exactly the question nobody wants asked out loud right now. **The lockdown is what makes his ordinary question dangerous**, and the port's answer to a dangerous question is not a refusal — **the dockhand simply stops talking and finds something to do with his hands.**

---

## What has to be true when the session ends

Very little.

- **Their freight is still impounded.** Nothing in the day fixes it, and it is now behind a dockmaster having a much worse day than he started with.
- **The count resolved one way or another** — Jonas boarded, or he did not, or he boarded with company. *If the gap ran long and it is still pending at the end of the night, that is a good place to stop.*
- **One thing did not resolve cleanly** (`rules/gm-guide.md`, Pacing Sessions).
- **The Oracle ritual runs.** That is the mechanical heartbeat and it is not optional.

**Everything else is allowed to not have happened.**
